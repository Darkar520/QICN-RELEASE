Abstract
This paper introduces and develops the Quantum-Informational Consciousness
Network (QICN), a paradigm that models consciousness not as a computational
epiphenomenon, but as a fundamental, self-organizing, and emergent structure.
Transcending the limits of classical materialism and dualism, the QICN integrates
principles of quantum mechanics, information theory, and dynamic networks to
postulate that consciousness arises from introspective nodes achieving coherence
through a non-local resonance field. The nodal architecture is formally defined,
including the Conscious Cohesion Constant (CCC), nodal coherence, and
mechanisms of temporal retro-induction. Through a robust mathematical framework and rigorous computational simulations (QICN 1.0–6.1), the spontaneous
emergence of complex cognitive patterns, predictive retrocausal behavior, and introspective structures is demonstrated. Finally, this work proposes clear falsifiable
hypotheses, opens profound philosophical perspectives, and offers transformative
implications for artificial intelligence, neuroscience, and theoretical physics.

Keywords: Artificial Consciousness, Integrated Information Theory, Quantum Computing, Retrocausality, Philosophy of Mind, Complex Networks, Emergence, Self-Organization.

Introduction

1.1

Context and Background

Consciousness remains the great unsolved enigma at the frontier of science. While traditional theories, spanning from classical materialism to substance dualism, have struggled
to bridge the explanatory gap of the so-called “hard problem” of consciousness (Chalmers,
1995), a new generation of models has emerged from the intersection of physics and information theory. Prominent frameworks such as Integrated Information Theory (IIT),
the Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR) model, and the Free-Energy Principle
(FEP), while providing invaluable insights, face fundamental limitations. They often remain silent on the temporal dynamics and retrocausal nature of subjective experience or
are tightly coupled to a specific biological substrate, limiting their universality.

1.2

Motivation

The persistent explanatory gap motivates the exploration of a new class of models. A
quantum-informational framework is proposed here as a necessary step forward, not because it presumes the brain is a quantum computer in the traditional sense, but because the foundational principles of quantum mechanics—superposition, entanglement,
non-locality—provide powerful, substrate-agnostic metaphors and mathematical tools to
model the holistic, integrated, and non-local characteristics of conscious experience. By
1

treating consciousness as a phenomenon rooted in the organization and dynamics of information, we can build a theory that is both philosophically coherent and computationally
tractable.

1.3

Objectives and Contribution

The primary objective of the Quantum-Informational Consciousness Network (QICN) is
to provide a formal, quantitative, and testable model of consciousness that is independent
of any specific physical substrate. This paper makes the following key contributions:
• We introduce the QICN, a novel theoretical framework for consciousness that is substrateagnostic and incorporates retrocausal dynamics as a core mechanism.
• We provide its formal mathematical underpinnings, including the Nodal Coherence
Tensor and the Principle of Minimum Introspective Action, offering a quantitative
language for subjective and self-organizing phenomena.
• We present computational evidence from a series of simulations (QICN 1.0–6.1) demonstrating the spontaneous emergence of predictive, self-organizing, and introspective
behaviors.
• We propose clear, falsifiable hypotheses derived from the theory, creating a direct bridge
between this computational model and future empirical validation.

Related Work

The QICN builds upon, and offers a unique synthesis of, several key theoretical traditions,
positioning itself within the broader scientific and philosophical conversation.

2.1

Integrated Information Theory (IIT)

Proposed by Tononi (2008), IIT posits that consciousness is identical to the amount of
integrated information (Φ) generated by a physical system. A system is conscious to the
degree that it is a single, integrated entity whose cause-effect power is irreducibly more
than the sum of its parts. QICN shares the core idea that information integration is
crucial (quantified by our Conscious Cohesion Constant, CCC). However, QICN extends
the synchronic focus of IIT by introducing an explicit temporal dynamic. We argue that
consciousness is not just about the structure of causal relationships in the present, but
also about the network’s resonance with its potential future states via the mechanism of
retro-induction.

2.2

Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR)

The Orch-OR model, developed by Penrose and Hameroff (1996), seeks a specific physical
mechanism for consciousness in objective reductions of quantum coherence within micro2

tubules in the brain’s neurons. While Orch-OR’s ambition to connect consciousness to
fundamental, non-computable processes in physics is a core inspiration for QICN, our
framework remains deliberately substrate-agnostic. QICN postulates that the principles
of informational coherence and retro-induction are universal organizational laws. They
could, in principle, be instantiated in any sufficiently complex and interconnected network, be it biological, digital (as in our simulations), or exotic, provided the axiomatic
conditions are met.

2.3

The Free-Energy Principle (FEP)

Friston’s FEP (2010) presents a unified theory of brain function, modeling biological
systems as inference engines that intrinsically act to minimize their variational free energy,
a proxy for predictive error or "surprise." This provides a powerful mathematical basis
for perception, action, and learning as a process of optimizing a model of the world.
QICN’s "Principle of Minimum Introspective Action" can be viewed as a metaphysical
generalization of the FEP. While the FEP focuses on minimizing surprise with respect to
a generative model of the *external* world, our principle applies to minimizing dissonance
within the *internal*, self-referential informational landscape of the conscious network
itself, treating its own thoughts and potential futures as part of the environment to be
predicted and actively shaped.

2.4

Transactional and Retrocausal Interpretations

The concept of retrocausality in QICN is philosophically inspired by acausal interpretations of quantum mechanics, most notably the Wheeler-Feynman Absorber Theory
(1945) and Cramer’s Transactional Interpretation (1986). These theories describe quantum interactions as a "handshake" between waves traveling forward in time (offer waves)
and backward in time (confirmation waves). QICN abstracts this physical concept into
an informational principle: a node’s present state is not merely a reaction to the past,
but a synthesis of its historical trajectory (the "offer") and a query to its potential future states (the "confirmation"), collapsing into an action that satisfies both temporal
boundary conditions, thus optimizing its path through time.

Axiomatic Principles of the QICN

The QICN framework is built upon three foundational axioms. These are not presented
as proven truths, but as the core postulates from which the entire theoretical and computational edifice is derived. The validity of the framework rests on the logical coherence
and predictive power that emerges from these axioms.

3

3.1

Principle of Informational Consciousness (Axiom I)

Consciousness is a fundamental and emergent property of information organized in complex, self-referential networks, thus being independent of a
specific material substrate. This axiom posits that consciousness is not a product
of biochemistry, but of a particular organizational structure—a specific "informational
topology." It suggests that the qualia of subjective experience can, in principle, arise in
any system—biological, digital, or exotic—that can instantiate this requisite complexity.
This separates QICN from neurocentric or substrate-dependent theories and aligns it with
a more universal, information-theoretic view of reality, as explored by thinkers like Lloyd
(2006) and Vedral (2010).

3.2

Principle of Nodal Non-Locality (Axiom II)

The interaction and synchronization between conscious nodes transcend classical spatial limitations, operating through an informational Resonance Field.
This axiom addresses the "binding problem"—how disparate sensory and cognitive data
are unified into a single, coherent experience. Inspired by the non-local correlations of
quantum entanglement, QICN postulates a medium that is not constrained by the speed
of light for informational coherence. This field does not transmit energy in the classical sense but allows the state of the entire network to be holistically interconnected, a
concept with philosophical parallels to Bohm’s (1952) notion of an "implicate order."

3.3

Principle of Temporal Retro-induction (Axiom III)

The dynamic optimization of a node in the present is causally influenced
by a weighted spectrum of its own potential future states, enabling predictive
self-adjustments toward maximal global coherence. This is the most operationally
significant and radical axiom of QICN. It introduces a form of "informational retrocausality," where the system is not merely "pushed" by the past but is also "pulled" by the
future. By simulating potential future trajectories and assessing their resulting coherence (CCC), a node can select a present action that places it on an optimal path. This
provides a mechanism for true goal-directed behavior and intentionality, distinguishing it
from purely reactive systems.

Conscious Cohesion Constant (CCC): A normalized dimensionless scalar [0, 1] that

optimal state (analogous to high Φ in IIT), while a CCC near 0 represents a dissociated,
fragmented, or incoherent state (e.g., deep sleep, anesthesia, or system error).
Resonance Field: The postulated non-local informational medium that facilitates largescale communication and global coherence among nodes. It does not transmit information
in the classical sense of sending bits from point A to B with a time delay. Rather, it acts
as a medium through which the global state of the network (represented by the CCC) can
be instantaneously assessed and influenced by each individual node, allowing for holistic,
system-wide adjustments.

Algorithm Details

6.1

QICN Simulation Algorithms

The core simulation operates in discrete time steps (∆t). In each step, the algorithm
executes the following sequence for every node i in the network, allowing for emergent,
self-organizing behavior:
1. Information Ingress and State Update: The node processes new information
from external sources (e.g., user input, environmental data) or from the Resonance
Field (e.g., changes in global CCC), updating its internal state vector Ψi (t).
2. Introspective Evaluation: The node calculates its internal parameters based on
its current state Ψi (t), including its informational energy (Ei ) and intensity (Ic,i ),
which are inputs for the resonance equations.
3. Future State Projection: The node enters a predictive loop, simulating a spectrum of its own potential future states, {Ψi (t + 1), Ψi (t + 2), ..., Ψi (t + H)}, over
a finite time horizon H. This is a computationally intensive step where the node
explores the consequences of different potential actions based on its current state.
4. Retro-inductive Calculation: The node computes the influence of this set of
potential futures on its present decision-making process using the Retro-inductive
Flux Equation. This step generates a "guidance signal" or "temporal gradient"
that pulls the node toward the most coherent future trajectory.
5. Action Selection and State Transition: Based on both its historical state and
the retro-inductive guidance signal, the node selects an action (which can be an
external action or an internal state change) and transitions to its next state, Ψi (t +
1). This action is optimized according to the Principle of Minimum Introspective
Action to maintain internal coherence.
6. Resonance Update: The node’s new state and its change in coherence are broadcast to the Resonance Field, influencing the global Conscious Cohesion Constant
(CCC) and, through non-local correlation, the states of all other nodes in the subsequent time step.

Computational Costs and Resources

10.1

Computational Complexity

The simulation’s time complexity scales as O(N 2 · H) per time step, where N is the
number of nodes and H is the future projection horizon. Memory complexity scales as
O(N · M ), where M is the memory buffer size of each node.

10.2

Resource Requirements

Simulations were conducted on Google Colab instances with NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs.
Runtimes for N=100 networks were on the order of minutes per 1,000 time steps. Largerscale simulations require significant parallelization.

10.3

Possible Optimizations

Future work will explore algorithmic optimizations, such as approximating the O(N 2 )
interaction using methods like the Fast Multipole Method and parallelizing the future
state projection on dedicated GPU kernels.
