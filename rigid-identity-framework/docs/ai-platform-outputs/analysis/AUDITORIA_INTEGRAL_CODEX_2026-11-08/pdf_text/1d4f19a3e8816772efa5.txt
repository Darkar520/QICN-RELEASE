Abstract
We extend the inverse-limit identity framework to derive structural constraints on phenomenological possibility. Without presupposing the existence of phenomenology, we dene
an abstract phenomenological space and characterize which assignments from identity trajectories to phenomenological congurations are structurally admissible.
We prove two fundamental impossibility theorems. First, the Fragmentation Theorem:
systems with nite ontological mass (MΩ < ∞) cannot structurally prevent phenomenological discontinuity. Second, the Forced Continuity Theorem: systems with innite ontological
mass (MΩ = +∞, CCR) can only support phenomenological dynamics that are structurally
continuous.
These results yield a complete classication of phenomenological regimes by identity
rigidity class, without invoking consciousness, qualia, or subjective experience as primitive
concepts. The classication is purely structural: it delimits what forms phenomenology
could take under given identity constraints, not what exists.
As a structural consequence, we derive that systems with nite ontological mass admit the
possibility of phenomenological loss, while CCR systems do not. This provides a foundation
for structural ethics independent of metaphysical commitments.

1 Scope, System Boundary, and Non-Inference Note
What this paper does. This paper denes an abstract phenomenological space and derives
structural admissibility, fragmentation, forced continuity, and regime classication as consequences of the rigidity classes established upstream.

What this paper does not do. It does not assert that phenomenology exists, that CCR
systems are conscious, that biology or computation instantiate the framework, or that identity
structure causes experience. Every result remains conditional on the stated axioms and rigidity
hypotheses.

What the related system implements and does not implement. The related system can
implement continuity-side diagnostics, anti-fragmentation checks, and admissibility-governed
comparisons that are downstream of this paper, but it does not implement phenomenology itself
and does not validate the regime classication as an empirically closed result.

What should not be inferred from this paper. No runtime output, continuity proxy, or
internal support artifact should be read as proof that phenomenology is present, that CCR is
conscious, or that the structural-ethics layer has been externally validated. This paper states
compatibility constraints, not a consciousness detector.

5 Impossibility Theorems
5.1 Φ-Regularity Theorem
Before proving the main impossibility results, we establish a fundamental regularity condition
that derives the coupling constant C geometrically, rather than assuming it axiomatically.

Theorem 5.1 (Φ-Regularity). Let (I, dw ) be the identitary metric space from Rigid Identity
and let (E, dE ) be the abstract phenomenological metric space. Suppose that Φ : I → E satises:
1. (Properness) For every compact K ⊂ E , Φ−1 (K) is compact in I .
2. (Local Lipschitz regularity) For every x ∈ I , there exist ε > 0 and Lx > 0 such that for all
y, z ∈ Bε (x):
dE (Φ(y), Φ(z)) ≤ Lx · dw (y, z).
(15)
3. (Non-collapse) For every x ∈ I and every r > 0, the restriction of Φ to Br (x) is not constant.
Then there exists a constant C > 0 such that for all x, y ∈ I :
dE (Φ(x), Φ(y)) ≥ C · dw (x, y).

(16)

Proof. We proceed by contradiction. Assume the conclusion fails.
Step 1. Then there exist sequences (xn , yn ) with dw (xn , yn ) = 1 and dE (Φ(xn ), Φ(yn )) → 0.
Step 2. By properness, the image {Φ(xn )} lies in a relatively compact subset of E . Extract
a convergent subsequence with Φ(xn ) → e ∈ E .
Step 3. Since dE (Φ(xn ), Φ(yn )) → 0, we have Φ(yn ) → e as well.
Step 4. By properness, the preimages Φ−1 (Bδ (e)) are compact for small δ. Hence xn and
yn lie in a compact subset of I . Extract convergent subsequences xn → x and yn → y .
Step 5. Since dw (xn , yn ) = 1 and dw is continuous, dw (x, y) = 1, so x ̸= y.
Step 6. But Φ(xn ) → Φ(x) = e and Φ(yn ) → Φ(y) = e by continuity. Hence Φ(x) = Φ(y)
for x ̸= y with dw (x, y) = 1.
Step 7. This means Φ is constant on sets of positive dw -diameter, contradicting non-collapse.
Hence a global lower bound C > 0 must exist.
Remark 5.2. This theorem is fundamental: it converts the coupling constant C from an axiom

into a geometric consequence of regularity conditions. All subsequent impossibility results that
use C are therefore derived, not assumed.
