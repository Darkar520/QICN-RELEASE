Abstract

We introduce an abstract phenomenological space E and a compatibility operator Φ :
I → E mapping rigid identity objects to phenomenological regimes. We dene a null regime
∅ ∈ E and prove a Phenomenological Instability Theorem: in any closed causally
rigid (CCR) channelcharacterized by inverse-limit identity, causal isolation, and MIR
geometryjoint null collapse is dynamically unstable under a separately typed compatible
extension witness. Consequently, a CCR realization with such a witness induces a witnessrelative non-null separation. The result is
: it does not posit experience;
it forbids one typed joint-null conguration under stated hypotheses. We provide explicit
coupling bounds in two canonical families (pronite and symbolic dynamics) and prove
universal factorization and non-simulability results. All constructions are purely topological
and categorical, without metaphysical axioms.

Null-Fiber Geometry

The null element ∅ϕ determines a ber

NΦ := Φ−1 ({∅ϕ }) ⊆ I.
The instability theorem can be read as a geometric obstruction on this ber: a certied witness
pair cannot be contained entirely inside NΦ .

Denition 10.1 (Null ber). For a compatibility operator Φ : I → E , the null ber is the
preimage NΦ = {x ∈ I : Φ(x) = ∅ϕ }.

Denition 10.2 (Witness-detecting map). A compatibility operator Φ is witness-detecting on
a set W ⊆ I × I when there exists CW > 0 such that

dE (Φ(x), Φ(y)) ≥ CW dw (x, y)
for every (x, y) ∈ W .

Proposition 10.3 (Null-ber exclusion on detected pairs). Let W be a witness class on which
Φ is witness-detecting with constant CW > 0. If (x, y) ∈ W and dw (x, y) > 0, then (x, y) cannot
both lie in NΦ .
Proof. If x, y ∈ NΦ , then Φ(x) = Φ(y) = ∅ϕ , so dE (Φ(x), Φ(y)) = 0. Witness detection gives
dE (Φ(x), Φ(y)) ≥ CW dw (x, y) > 0, a contradiction.

Corollary 10.4 (Diameter obstruction). If a subset A ⊆ I contains a detected witness pair
(x, y) with dw (x, y) > 0, then A cannot be contained in NΦ .
Proof. Containment A ⊆ NΦ would place both endpoints of the detected pair in the null ber,
contradicting the previous proposition.

Proof. Since q(x) = q(y), Φ(x) = h(q(x)) = h(q(y)) = Φ(y). Hence the left-hand side is zero,
while Cdw (x, y) > 0 for any C > 0.
