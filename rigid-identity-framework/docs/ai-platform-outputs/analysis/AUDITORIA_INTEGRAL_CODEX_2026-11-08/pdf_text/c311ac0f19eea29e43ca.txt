Abstract
We establish a canonical mathematical foundation for contractive dynamical
systems on Hilbert spaces with metric projections onto closed convex subsets. We
prove existence and uniqueness of projections, contractivity of the induced transition operator, existence of unique attractors, global compactness, and structural
non-collapsibility. All results are derived from four minimal hypotheses (H1–H4)
using standard functional analysis. This core serves as the rigorous foundation for
the Rigid Identity Framework, where Papers I and II become corollaries of the
theorems established here. No ontological, phenomenological, or semantic interpretations are assumed; all results are purely structural.

Minimal Hypotheses

We establish four hypotheses that are both necessary and sufficient for all main results.
Hypothesis 2.1 (H1: Projection Structure). Let H be a real Hilbert space with inner
product ⟨·, ·⟩ and induced norm ∥·∥. Let C ⊂ H be a non-empty, closed, and convex subset.
Hypothesis 2.2 (H2: Contraction Operator). Let K : H → H be a bounded linear
operator with operator norm ∥K∥ < 1.
Hypothesis 2.3 (H3: Completeness). The space H is complete (as a Hilbert space, this
is automatic).
Hypothesis 2.4 (H4: Parameter Compactness). Let U be a compact metric space (the
parameter space). Let Γ : U → H be a uniformly continuous function.

2.1

Necessity and Sufficiency

Proposition 2.1 (Minimal Hypotheses). Hypotheses H1–H4 are:
1. Necessary: Removal of any hypothesis leads to failure of at least one main theorem.
2. Sufficient: Together they imply all main theorems.
Proof. Necessity is established by counterexamples in Appendix A. Sufficiency is the
content of Theorems 3.2–6.3.

At the fixed point fu∗ , the linearized dynamics are governed by the Jacobian operator
J = ∂Tu /∂Ψ. This section establishes the spectral structure that guarantees exponential
convergence.

10.1

σ(J) = {1} ∪ {∥K∥n : n ≥ 1} = {1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, . . .}

10.2

Interpretation of Eigenmodes

1. Mode 1 (λ1 = 1): The persistence mode. Perturbations along the invariant manifold C do not decay. This represents structural stability of the attractor.
2. Mode 2 (λ2 = 0.5): The primary dissipation mode. External perturbations are
halved each iteration.
3. Modes n ≥ 3 (λn → 0): High-frequency noise modes. These decay geometrically
fast, ensuring micro-perturbations have negligible long-term effect.

Definition of Class C

Definition 11.1 (Class C of Contractive Projection Dynamics).
C := {(H, C, K, Γ, U) : H1–H5 are satisfied}

(40)

Proposition 11.1 (Universal Properties of C). Every system in C satisfies:
1. Unique metric projection P exists for all states
2. Transition operator Tu is contractive with factor ∥K∥
3. Unique attractor fu∗ exists for each parameter u
4. Global attractor A∗ is compact
5. No attractor is constant (non-collapsibility)
6. Spectral gap ∆λ ≥ 1 − ∥K∥ > 0
7. Relaxation time τ ≤ −1/ ln(∥K∥)

Proposition 11.2 (Morphism Invariance). Let S1 = (H1 , C1 , K1 , Γ1 , U1 ) ∈ C and S2 =
(H2 , C2 , K2 , Γ2 , U2 ) ∈ C. Let φ : H1 → H2 be a bounded linear morphism such that:
• φ(C1 ) ⊆ C2 (manifold preservation)
• φ ◦ K1 − K2 ◦ φ < ε for some ε > 0 (semi-commutativity)
Then:

2. If φ is an isometry with φ(C1 ) = C2 , then φ(A∗1 ) = A∗2 .
3. Spectral properties (λ1 , λ2 , τ ) are invariant under isometric φ.
Corollary 11.1 (Categorical Closure). C is closed under Hilbert space isomorphisms that
preserve convexity of the invariant set.

introducing modified hypotheses and weaker stability guarantees.

12.1

Motivation

C requires convexity (H1) for unique projections. However, many systems of interest
have invariant sets Ce = S 1 (topologically circles) that violate convexity. We develop C R
(“Critical Rigid Systems”) to handle these cases.

Counterexamples for Necessity

These counterexamples show that each hypothesis is necessary.

A.1

Without H1 (Convexity)

Let C = {x ∈ R2 : ∥x∥ = 1} (unit circle, not convex). For Ψ = (0, 0), every point on C is
equidistant. Projection is not unique.

A.2

Without H2 (∥K∥ < 1)

Let K be a rotation by angle θ ̸= 0 with ∥K∥ = 1. Orbits cycle indefinitely; no fixed
point exists.

A.3

Without H3 (Completeness)

Let H = C([0, 1], Q) (rational-valued continuous functions). Cauchy sequences may converge to irrational functions outside the space.

A.4

Without H4 (Compactness)

Let C = (0, 1) (open interval) and Γ(u) = 1/u. As u → 0, attractors escape to infinity;
A∗ is not compact.
