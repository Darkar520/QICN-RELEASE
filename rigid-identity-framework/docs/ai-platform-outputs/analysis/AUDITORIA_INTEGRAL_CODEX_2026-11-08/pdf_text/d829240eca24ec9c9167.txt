Let ℋ = 𝐶([0, 1], Q) (rational-valued continuous functions).

A.5

Why Φ-Regularity Is a Hypothesis

Let ℐ = R with the Euclidean metric, let ℰ = (−𝜋/2, 𝜋/2) with the Euclidean metric, and define
Φ(𝑥) = arctan(𝑥).
The map Φ is proper as a map into the open interval ℰ, locally Lipschitz, and non-constant on
every nonempty ball. However, no constant 𝐶 > 0 can satisfy
| arctan(𝑥) − arctan(𝑦)| ≥ 𝐶|𝑥 − 𝑦|

∀𝑥, 𝑦 ∈ R.

Indeed, with 𝑦 = 𝑥 + 1,
| arctan(𝑥 + 1) − arctan(𝑥)| → 0

as 𝑥 → ∞,

while |𝑥 − 𝑦| = 1. Thus properness, local Lipschitz regularity, and non-collapse do not imply
a global lower Lipschitz bound. Any theorem using such a bound must carry it as an explicit
hypothesis or prove stronger coercive hypotheses.
