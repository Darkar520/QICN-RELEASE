\begin{abstract}
We introduce a formal framework for rigid identity as a mathematical object emerging from the dynamics of observable systems under minimal structural constraints. Identity is defined as an inverse limit over a family of locally corrupted states linked by retroinductive projection maps, yielding a globally invariant object despite persistent environmental perturbations.

For a common metric tower, exact fiber lifts with bounded weighted amplification yield a Hausdorff bound. Commuting coordinate homeomorphisms induce an isomorphism, realized explicitly by a ramified circle tower with positive deformation. The deformation-resistance ratio, historically termed sectorial deformation resistance, depends on the declared intervention cost and comparison metric.

For exact comparisons of model histories, one finite-cost intervention with positive deformation certifies a finite resistance ratio. Estimating that ratio from observable data requires a calibrated comparison and coverage of the intervention class.

The assignment is unique up to zero deformation within a finite-cost connected operating component in the declared sector. Resistance classes transport under audited comparisons. A typed product-retraction certificate gives a quantitative resistance obstruction; a separate cut-point argument proves exact product atomicity for an infinite-dimensional compact support. The Cantor control refutes inference from resistance alone. Physical admissibility and general approximation gaps remain research obligations.

Finite binary representations face a fidelity bound when the specified responses are sufficiently separated. A latent dimension or an intervention cost alone does not determine the representation budget.

The results specify projective representations, sectorial resistance and response-fidelity obstructions under declared comparison data. Physical or experiential interpretation requires additional evidence and is developed separately at the end.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The concept of identity in dynamical systems has been studied from multiple angles: cognitive science treats it as emergent from neural processes, philosophy of mind debates its ontological status, and computer science models it as state persistence. Yet a formal, substrate-independent characterization of identity as a \textit{structural invariant} remains underdeveloped.

This work addresses a specific question: \textbf{Under what minimal conditions does a system exhibit an identity that is structurally rigid}---that is, invariant under perturbation, non-transferable, and externally detectable?

We study structural properties that emerge from well-defined mathematical constraints. All results presented here are conditional on the stated assumptions; they do not extend to systems that violate those assumptions.

\paragraph{Classical ingredients and the present construction.}
Inverse limits and their universal properties are standard mathematical
tools \cite{maclane1998,ribes}; contractive dynamics has its classical
fixed-point foundations \cite{banach}. The contribution examined here is
the declared combination of projective compatibility, sectorial deformation
cost and bounded-distortion separation, together with explicit realizations
and counterexamples. Each conclusion retains the metric, admissibility and
comparison hypotheses of its theorem.

\subsection{Motivation}
The technical question is which persistence, reconstruction and resistance properties follow from a declared controlled system and projective comparison. An inverse-limit object constrains compatible coordinates; it is not a proof that every emergent organization is transferable or that identity has metaphysical priority over dynamics. The broader interpretation and atomicity program are retained in the final research-links section.

\section{Minimal Assumptions and Framework}

We begin by stating the minimal assumptions required for the subsequent results. These assumptions are deliberately weak---any system that fails to satisfy them is not a candidate for rigid identity, and no claim is made about such systems.

\paragraph{Shared types and index binding.}
Physical time is $t$, projective depth is $n$, and $\tau$ is a finite observation horizon. In the historical pure-tower formulas below, the bound subscript $t$ is a local alias for depth $n$, not an assertion about temporal dynamics. $\mathcal I$ (also $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{inv}}$) is the inverse-limit object; $A$ is an invariant support. The readout map $C_{\rm obs}$, the experiment model $(E,O,R)$, and the finite-cost connected component $\mathcal C_\alpha$ of histories are different types. Neither equality of outputs nor reusing a symbol identifies these objects. A projection set $C_{\rm proj}$ from BaseCore is not assumed equal to the inverse limit.

\subsection{Assumption A1: Observable Channel}

There exists an observable channel $\mathcal{C}: \mathcal{U} \to \mathcal{O}$ mapping internal states $\mathcal{U}$ to observable outputs $\mathcal{O}$. We do not assume injectivity of $\mathcal{C}$; multiple internal states may produce identical observations.

\subsection{Assumption A2: Temporal Persistence}

The system persists through discrete time steps $t \in \mathbb{N}$. At each step, the internal state $I_t$ is subject to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Environmental input $E_t$ (not fully controlled by the system)
    \item Internal dynamics governed by transition function $F$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Assumption A3: Non-Triviality (R0)}

Declare a nonempty admissible state set $X_{\rm adm}$, an input set $E$, and a total controlled law $F:X_{\rm adm}\times E\to X_{\rm adm}$, restricted to the specified admissible controls. R0 means: admissible trajectories remain in this nonempty state domain; there are $e,x,x'$ with $F(x,e)\ne F(x',e)$; and the declared family contains a nonconstant trajectory. The middle clause says that $F$ does not factor through the current input projection. It is a statement about the controlled law, not a fit to one observed trajectory. Fixed-point trajectories may coexist with the required nonconstant one.

For example, $F(x,e)=1-x$ on $\{0,1\}$ meets these clauses for any nonempty input set. If a recorded input takes a different value at each time, a function can interpolate that trajectory's next states even though the law is independent of the input. Such interpolation does not decide the structural clause.

\subsection{Categorical Regularity: Topological Structure of State Spaces}

To guarantee existence and uniqueness of inverse limits, and to enable rigorous stability analysis, we impose the following structural condition:

\begin{definition}[Observable State Spaces]\label{def:obs-spaces}
All state spaces $S_t$ are objects of a concrete category $\mathbf{C}$ whose:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Objects} are compact Hausdorff topological spaces;
    \item \textbf{Morphisms} are continuous surjective maps.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{axiom}[State Space Regularity]\label{ax:state-space-regularity}
Each state space $S_t$ in the projective system is a compact metric space with compatible metric $d_t$. Consequently, each $S_t$ is compact Hausdorff as a topological space and Polish as a metric space. The inverse limit $\mathcal{I}$ inherits the corresponding compact metrizable subspace structure from the product.
\end{axiom}

\begin{remark}[Exclusion of non-compact Polish spaces]\label{rem:noncompact-polish-exclusion}
Axiom~\ref{ax:state-space-regularity} refines Definition~\ref{def:obs-spaces} rather than weakening it. Non-compact Polish spaces such as $\mathbb{R}^n$ are not covered by this compact inverse-limit argument. Any extension of the CCR framework to non-compact settings requires additional tightness, compactification, or coercivity hypotheses and is not automatic.
\end{remark}

\begin{hypothesis}[Topological Regularity]\label{hyp:topo}
All projections $\pi_{t+1 \to t}: S_{t+1} \to S_t$ are continuous surjections between compact Hausdorff spaces.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}
This condition is sufficient for the compact inverse-limit construction used here. By Tychonoff's theorem, the product $\prod_{t} S_t$ is compact when each $S_t$ is compact. Furthermore, the inverse limit $\varprojlim S_t$ is a closed subspace of the product, hence compact. This guarantees:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Existence of the inverse limit (non-emptiness under surjectivity);
    \item Well-defined metrics and convergence;
    \item Applicability of functional analysis tools.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[R0 under Controlled-System Isomorphism]\label{prop:a0-closed}
Let $h:X_{\rm adm}\to X'_{\rm adm}$ and $v:E\to E'$ be bijections and put $F'(hx,ve)=hF(x,e)$. R0 holds for one controlled system exactly when it holds for the other.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
State nonemptiness, admissible trajectories and nonconstancy transport through $h$. A factorization $F'(z,e')=g(e')$ pulls back to $F(x,e)=h^{-1}g(ve)$, and conversely. Thus non-factorization is preserved. This is closure under the declared isomorphisms, not a claim of topological closedness in an unspecified space of models.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditional Nature of Results}

Each theorem uses its displayed hypotheses, including the separately declared regularity and comparison conditions:

\begin{quote}
\textit{``If the stated hypotheses of a result hold on its typed domain, then its stated conclusion follows.''}
\end{quote}

We make no claims about systems that violate these assumptions. This conditional structure is essential: it means our results cannot be falsified by pointing to systems outside the assumption set, and cannot be extended beyond their stated scope without additional justification.

\section{Inverse Limit Identity}

\subsection{Preliminaries and Notation}

Let $\{S_t\}_{t \in \mathbb{N}}$ be a family of non-empty state spaces indexed by discrete time. Each $S_t$ represents the admissible internal configurations of a system at time $t$.

We assume the existence of projection morphisms
\begin{equation}
    \pi_{t+1 \to t}: S_{t+1} \to S_t
\end{equation}
satisfying the standard compatibility condition:
\begin{equation}
    \pi_{t+1 \to t} \circ \pi_{t+2 \to t+1} = \pi_{t+2 \to t} \quad \forall t \in \mathbb{N}.
\end{equation}

The set-theoretic cone construction alone does not require:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item a fixed cardinality of $S_t$,
    \item continuity or smoothness; the compact topological results separately impose the stated regularity,
    \item realizability by a computational mechanism.
\end{itemize}

The structure $(\{S_t\}, \{\pi_{t+1 \to t}\})$ therefore defines a \textbf{projective (inverse) system} in the categorical sense \cite{maclane1998, adamek1990}.

\subsection{Definition of Identity as an Inverse Limit}

\begin{definition}[Identity as Inverse Limit]
We define identity as the inverse limit of the projective system $(S_t, \pi_{t+1 \to t})$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{I} := \varprojlim S_t = \left\{ (x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots) \in \prod_{t=0}^{\infty} S_t \;\Big|\; \pi_{t+1 \to t}(x_{t+1}) = x_t \;\; \forall t \right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

An element of $\mathcal{I}$ is a \textit{globally consistent history}. Non-recovery from finite cuts requires the branching hypothesis below, not merely the inverse-limit definition.

This definition is purely structural and does not depend on:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item observability,
    \item external inputs,
    \item internal dynamics.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Compatibility with Minimal Assumption R0}

The representation below uses a declared trajectory domain. R0 alone does not select its cuts or force non-finite determination.

\begin{proposition}[Prefix Representation of a Trajectory Domain]\label{prop:necessity}
Let $H\subseteq X^{\mathbb N}$ be nonempty and let $S_n$ be its prefixes through time $n$, with deletion maps. These maps are surjective and $H$ embeds in their nonempty inverse limit. If $X$ is compact metric and $H$ is closed, the embedding is a homeomorphism onto that limit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every prefix comes from a history of $H$ and therefore has a longer lift. Mapping a history to all its prefixes is injective and compatible. A point of the limit specifies one sequence all of whose prefixes extend to $H$. If $H$ is closed, that sequence belongs to $H$: otherwise a product neighborhood disjoint from $H$ would exclude a sufficiently long prefix. The coordinate maps are continuous, and compactness of $H$ and Hausdorffness of the limit give the homeomorphism. The alternating two-state law satisfies R0, but its two trajectories are determined by their initial state. Thus R0 does not imply ramification or non-finite determination.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Identity Is Not a State}

The obstruction concerns the specified evaluation maps, rather than cardinalities of their underlying sets.

\begin{proposition}[Non-locality of Identity]\label{prop:nonlocality}
Let $p_t:\mathcal I\to S_t$ be canonical evaluation. Assume active bonding maps are surjective. If some $n\ge t$ has distinct $a,b\in S_{n+1}$ with $\pi_{n+1\to n}(a)=\pi_{n+1\to n}(b)$, then $p_t$ is not injective. Every map factoring through $p_t$ fails to separate all compatible histories. Branching arbitrarily far out obstructs every finite canonical cut. This does not exclude arbitrary set injections $\mathcal I\hookrightarrow S_t$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lift $a,b$ successively to infinite compatible histories $x,y$, and obtain earlier coordinates by projection. They agree at $n$, hence at $t$, but differ at $n+1$. Every $g\circ p_t$ identifies that pair. A noninjective link only before $t$ does not suffice: later identity links can make $p_t$ injective. Surjectivity, or explicit lifting of both points, is essential. In the interval--binary tower below, both the inverse limit and each finite stage have continuum cardinality. Thus the obstruction is projective/observational, not cardinal.
\end{proof}

This formalizes a key distinction:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item $S_t$: local configurations at a declared cut
    \item $\mathcal{I}$: global projective compatibility constraint
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Impossibility of Progressive Construction}

We examine when compatible forward sections fail to exhaust the declared inverse limit.

\begin{theorem}[No Progressive Identity Construction]\label{thm:noprogressive}
If $p_0$ is noninjective, no compatible forward sections $f_t:S_t\to S_{t+1}$ with $\pi_{t+1\to t}f_t=\mathrm{id}$ generate all of $\mathcal I$ as the orbital history space $x_{t+1}=f_t(x_t)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
They select exactly one orbit per initial point, whereas noninjectivity gives two compatible histories with the same initial point. This does not prohibit forward dynamics on a space already storing the tail; see Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Minimality of the Inverse Limit Construction}

\begin{theorem}[Universal Representation of a Fixed Compatible Cone]\label{thm:minimal}
For a declared inverse system, every coherent family $q_n:J\to S_n$ induces a unique map $q:J\to\mathcal I$ with $p_nq=q_n$. In the topological category, continuous $q_n$ give continuous $q$ in the product-subspace topology.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define $q(j)=(q_n(j))_n$. Coherence places this tuple in the inverse limit; its coordinates force uniqueness. A map to a product is continuous when its coordinates are continuous, and the image lies in the subspace. This is universality relative to a fixed diagram, not a proof that R0 selects a unique diagram or that every persistent system has a non-finitely determined identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Summary of Section 3}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Identity is rigorously defined as an inverse limit $\mathcal{I} = \varprojlim S_t$.
    \item A declared trajectory domain has the prefix representation just proved; R0 does not select a unique projective diagram.
    \item Non-finite determination obstructs the declared finite cuts, when that additional hypothesis holds.
    \item Compatible forward sections need not exhaust a ramified limit; dynamics storing the full history are not excluded by that obstruction.
    \item No metaphysical assumptions are introduced.
\end{enumerate}

This completes the formal construction of identity used throughout the remainder of the paper.

\subsection{Identity Neutrality Clause}

Throughout this paper, the identity object $\mathcal{I}$ is treated as a \textit{purely abstract topological and categorical structure}. No psychological, biological, phenomenological, or experiential interpretation is assumed or required for any of the formal results derived herein.

The technical term ``identity denotes $\mathcal{I}$ as an inverse-limit object encoding global temporal compatibility. Ordinary personal or semantic interpretations require additional bridge premises.

All theorems, constructions, and stability results presented in this work are therefore statements about abstract mathematical objects only. Any interpretation of $\mathcal{I}$ in relation to psychological, phenomenological, or experiential concepts lies strictly outside the formal scope of this paper.

\section{Rigidity Under Perturbations}

\subsection{Setup and Hypotheses}

Let the projective system $(S_t, \pi_{t+1 \to t})_{t \in \mathbb{N}}$ and its inverse limit $\mathcal{I} = \varprojlim S_t$ be as in Section 3. We introduce an external perturbation model and define deformed projections; then we state the technical hypotheses under which rigidity holds.

\begin{hypothesis}[H1: Surjectivity / Lifting]\label{hyp:h1}
For every $t$, the projection $\pi_{t+1 \to t}: S_{t+1} \to S_t$ is surjective.
\end{hypothesis}

This is a standard regularity condition for non-empty inverse limits; when necessary this can be replaced by a Mittag--Leffler condition or an explicit choice of right inverses.

\begin{hypothesis}[H2: Compact Polish Metric Structure]\label{hyp:h2}
For each $t$, the state space $S_t$ carries the compact metric $d_t$ required by Axiom~\ref{ax:state-space-regularity}; in particular, $(S_t,d_t)$ is Polish. All projection maps $\pi_{t+1 \to t}$ are continuous with respect to $d_t$. The quantitative lifting estimate is a separate condition in Lemma~\ref{lem:stability}.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}
The Polish structure ensures Borel regularity of all deformation families, enabling measurable selection theorems, probabilistic perturbation models, and the application of stochastic stability results to the sectorial deformation resistance $M_\Omega$. This is the standard regularity assumption in modern probability theory, ergodic theory, and descriptive set theory.
\end{remark}

These assumptions are technical; they do not change the conceptual content (identity as inverse limit) but permit quantitative stability estimates and connect the framework to probabilistic analysis.

\subsection{Perturbation Model and Deformation Metric}

\begin{definition}[Local Perturbation Operator]
An intervention may be specified by maps $\delta E_t:S_t\to\widetilde S_t$. To compare the resulting histories, declare a common metric ambient stage or an identification of stages. The stability lemma below uses the case of common stages and compares their two bonding maps.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Deformed Projections]
On common stages the two maps are $\pi_t,\widetilde\pi_t:S_{t+1}\to S_t$. Their discrepancy is $\epsilon_t=\sup_{u\in S_{t+1}}d_t(\pi_t(u),\widetilde\pi_t(u))$. An additive operator formula requires separately specified linear coordinates.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Weighted Deformation Norm]
For $0<w_t<\infty$, the weighted deformation size is $D_w=\sum_{t\geq0} w_t\epsilon_t$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Weighted Product Metric]\label{def:weighted-sup-metric}
For nonempty metric spaces $(S_t,d_t)$ and weights $0<w_t<\infty$, write
\[
 d_w(x,y)=\sup_{t\geq0} w_t d_t(x_t,y_t),\qquad
 P_b=\{x\in\textstyle\prod_t S_t:d_w(x,b)<\infty\}.
\]
The formula defines an extended metric on the product and an ordinary metric on each finite-distance component $P_b$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Finiteness, Completeness and Compactness of Weighted Histories]\label{prop:weighted-history}
The formula is finite on every pair in the full product exactly when the weighted diameters are bounded outside a finite set of stages. For finite-diameter stages this is equivalent to $B=\sup_t w_t\operatorname{diam}(S_t)<\infty$. For any nonempty inverse limit $I$ and $b\in I$, it is finite on all of $I$ exactly when $I\subseteq P_b$. If the coordinate spaces are complete and the bonding maps continuous, $P_b$ and $I\cap P_b$ are complete. If $w_t\operatorname{diam}(S_t)\to0$, the weighted metric induces the product topology and
\[
 0\leq d_w(x,y)-\max_{0\leq t\leq N} w_t d_t(x_t,y_t)
 \leq \sup_{t>N} w_t\operatorname{diam}(S_t).
\]
In this case compact coordinate spaces give a compact inverse limit. Surjective bonding maps give a compatible history through each stage point.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The extended triangle inequality follows by taking suprema. A uniform bound outside finitely many stages suffices since each of the exceptional distances is individually finite. If no such bound exists, choose distinct stages $t_j$ and pairs with weighted distances greater than $j$. Combining these pairs in two product points gives an infinite distance. The bound $B<\infty$ characterizes \emph{boundedness} of the full-product metric. The inverse-limit condition follows from the triangle inequality through $b$.

A $d_w$-Cauchy sequence converges in each coordinate. Passing to each coordinate limit in the same uniform Cauchy bound proves convergence in $d_w$. The limit remains in $P_b$ by the triangle inequality and in $I$ by continuity of the bonding maps. With vanishing weighted diameter tails, a finite prefix controls the metric up to the displayed remainder, while each coordinate projection is $1/w_t$-Lipschitz. This proves the topology assertion. Conversely, on the full product with a finite metric, if the weighted diameters fail to vanish there are infinitely many coordinates with weighted diameter at least some $\eta>0$. At any fixed product point, for each such coordinate one can change that coordinate by weighted distance greater than $\eta/3$, by the triangle inequality. The resulting sequence converges in the product topology but fails to converge in $d_w$. The converse may fail on a restricted set of compatible histories. The inverse limit is closed in the compact product because it is the intersection of the closed compatibility equations. Surjectivity gives successive lifts and the earlier coordinates are determined by projection.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Two Distinct Tail Failures]\label{ex:weighted-tail-failures}
On $S_t=[0,1]$ with identity bonding maps and $w_t=2^{-t}$, choose $d_t(s,u)=4^t|s-u|$. The two constant histories $0$ and $1$ have distance $+\infty$. For a different failure, take $S_t=\{0,1\}^t$ with prefix deletion and $d_t=2^t$ times the discrete metric. With the same weights every distinct pair of histories has distance $1$. The inverse limit has a compact product topology and a noncompact discrete metric topology. Flipping a bit at successively later levels gives product convergence to the unflipped history while all the weighted distances remain $1$.
\end{example}

\subsection{Stability Lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Stability of Compatibility]\label{lem:stability}
Use common compact metric stages and two continuous bonding families with $w_t\operatorname{diam}(S_t)\to0$. Assume $D_w<\infty$. For each $f\in\{\pi_t,\widetilde\pi_t\}$ and each $u\in S_{t+1},v\in S_t$, assume an exact lift $u'$ with $f(u')=v$ and $d_{t+1}(u,u')\leq K_t d_t(f(u),v)$ for some common $K_t>0$. Set $q_t=w_{t+1}K_t/w_t$ and $C=\sup_{0\leq k\leq t}\prod_{j=k}^t q_j<\infty$. The limits $I=\mathcal I$ and $\widetilde I=\widetilde{\mathcal I}$ are nonempty and $d_H(I,\widetilde I)\leq C D_w$ in the common history space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given a history $x$ of $\pi$, set $y_0=x_0$. Having chosen $y_t$, lift it under $\widetilde\pi_t$ at $x_{t+1}$. The exact lift $y_{t+1}$ obeys
\[
 d_{t+1}(y_{t+1},x_{t+1})\le K_t(d_t(y_t,x_t)+\epsilon_t)
\]
by the fiber estimate and $\pi_t(x_{t+1})=x_t$. For $a_t=w_t d_t(y_t,x_t)$ this gives $a_{t+1}\le q_t a_t+q_t w_t\epsilon_t$, with $a_0=0$. Iteration bounds $a_{t+1}$ by $\sum_{k=0}^t (\prod_{j=k}^t q_j)w_k\epsilon_k$, at most $C D_w$. The reverse construction uses the fiber estimate for $\pi_t$ and gives the other directed Hausdorff bound. Each limit is nonempty by successive lifts from any $S_0$ point. Taking suprema proves the estimate in the common history metric.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rigidity Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Rigidity of the Identity under Coherent Transport]\label{thm:rigidity}
Suppose the weighted metric induces the product topology and homeomorphisms $h_t:S_t\to S_t$ satisfy $\widetilde\pi_t h_{t+1}=h_t\pi_t$. Then $H(x)_t=h_t(x_t)$ maps the limits homeomorphically, with inverse $H^{-1}(y)_t=h_t^{-1}(y_t)$. Its displacement in the common history space is at most $\sup_t w_t\sup_{z\in S_t}d_t(z,h_t(z))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The displayed square and its inverse give exact compatibility of both images. Coordinate continuity in the product gives continuity of both induced maps. The displacement estimate follows coordinate by coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A Ramified Tower with Positive Deformation and Coherent Transport]\label{ex:coherent-rotation-tower}
Take $S_t=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)\times\{0,1\}^{t}$ with the maximum of the circle distance (circumference one) and the discrete prefix distance. Let $\pi_t$ keep the angle and delete the last bit, and take $w_t=2^{-t}$. For an intervention at stage $k$ with amplitude $0<a<1/2$, replace $\pi_k$ by prefix deletion and angle shift $-a$. Set $h_t$ to the identity for $t\leq k$ and to angle shift $a$ for $t>k$. The inverses shift by $-a$ on those same levels. The squares commute at $k$ by cancellation and at every other level by commutation with prefix deletion. Each limit has at least two histories over every finite cut, since a later bit may be flipped.

All bonding maps are $1$-Lipschitz and satisfy the fiber estimate with $K_t=1$: set the required angle and prefix, retaining the last bit. Thus $q_t=1/2$. For this example set the dimensionless intervention cost $E_w=D_w=w_k a$, and $\sup_x d_w(x,Hx)=w_{k+1}a=E_w/2$. More precisely, for $b=w_{k+1}$,
\[
 d_H(I,\widetilde I)=\frac{ba}{1+b}>0.
\]
Indeed an original history has constant angle $\theta$ and a perturbed one has angle $\varphi$ up to $k$ and $\varphi+a$ thereafter. If the two histories are at distance $r$, the circle triangle inequality gives $a\leq r+r/b$ from stages $0$ and $k+1$. Choosing identical bits and $\varphi=\theta-ba/(1+b)$ attains equality. That choice is reversible for every perturbed history and gives both directed distances. For the sector of these single jumps the infimum of the cost-to-deformation ratio is $2$, since the ratio is $2(1+b)$ at stage $k$. This uses the declared projection-defect cost. An entropy-based cost requires a separate comparison.
\end{example}

The dynamical realization in BaseCore, \emph{Projective Dissipation on the Circle-Prefix Towers}, uses the same jump and the weights $3^{-t}$ used for the joint readout. At fixed input the equilibrium is transported by the existing coordinate shift. For $\beta=3^{-(k+1)}$, the equilibrium displacement $\beta a$ and the Hausdorff deformation $\beta a/(1+\beta)$ retain distinct meanings. Dissipation controls temporal attraction on each branch, while the declared intervention cost and deformation still govern resistance.


\begin{example}[Hausdorff Stability Without a Homeomorphism]\label{ex:hausdorff-not-isomorphism}
Take $S_t=\{0,\ldots,2^t-1\}$ with discrete distance scaled by $r>0$, $w_t=2^{-t}$, and $\pi_t(j)=\lfloor j/2\rfloor$. For $t\geq1$, change only the image of $j=1$ to $1$, keeping $\widetilde\pi_0=\pi_0$. Both maps remain surjective and satisfy the fiber estimate with $K_t=1$. The weighted intervention cost is $r\sum_{t\geq1}2^{-t}=r$. The all-zero history of the perturbed tower is isolated by the cylinder $y_1=0$ because each subsequent zero has exactly one lift. The original tower is the binary-prefix space and has no isolated histories. Thus the limits are not homeomorphic, even though the Hausdorff bound tends to zero with $r$. All hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:stability} hold.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[Small Displacement of Coherent Transports]
For a family of commuting coordinate homeomorphisms, if $\sup_t w_t\sup_{z\in S_t}d_t(z,h_t(z))$ tends to zero, then the induced transports converge uniformly to the inclusion of $I$ in the common history space.
\end{corollary}


\subsection{Sectorial Deformation Resistance: Quantitative Resistance}

We now formalize the scalar invariant that quantifies resistance of $\mathcal{I}$ under external entropy injection.

\begin{definition}[Perturbation Energy]
For a controlled perturbation process $\{\delta E_t\}$ we define its weighted energy:
\begin{equation}
    E_w(\{\delta E_t\}) := \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} w_t \, H_t(\delta E_t)
\end{equation}
where $H_t(\delta E_t)$ is a non-negative scalar measuring the entropy or information content injected at time $t$ (e.g., Shannon entropy of stochastic perturbation).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sectorial Deformation Resistance]\label{def:ontmass}
Define the sectorial deformation resistance of the projective system (or of $\mathcal{I}$) as:
\begin{equation}
    M_\Omega := \inf \left\{ \frac{E_w(\{\delta E_t\})}{d_w(\mathcal{I}, \tilde{\mathcal{I}})} \;\Big|\; \{\delta E_t\} \text{ admissible and } d_w(\mathcal{I}, \tilde{\mathcal{I}}) > 0 \right\},
\end{equation}
with the convention that if no admissible perturbation produces non-zero deformation (i.e., $d_w(\mathcal{I}, \tilde{\mathcal{I}}) = 0$ for all finite-energy perturbations), then $M_\Omega = +\infty$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Internal Perturbations and External Extensions}
\label{sec:internal-external}

\begin{definition}[Internal perturbations and external extensions]\label{def:internal-external}
Let $S$ be a CCR channel with identity object $\mathcal{I}(S)$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Internal perturbations} are operators $\delta\in\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}(S)$ that act on the identity structure of $S$: $\delta$ maps $\mathcal{I}(S)$ to a deformed identity $\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(S)$ belonging to the same declared structural class, with finite weighted energy $E_w(\delta)<\infty$. Internal perturbations test rigidity of $\mathcal{I}(S)$ under deformation.
\item \textbf{External extensions} are operations $\eta\in\mathrm{Ext}(S)$ that produce a different channel $S'$: $\eta$ maps $S$ to $S'$ with $D_{\mathrm{ext}}(S,S')=\epsilon>0$. External extensions compare $\mathcal{I}(S)$ with an independently specified object $\mathcal{I}(S')$ not introduced as an element of $\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}(S)$.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Typed separation axiom:} $\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}(S)\cap\mathrm{Ext}(S)=\varnothing$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation of typed separation]\label{rem:typed-separation}
The typed separation $\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}(S)\cap\mathrm{Ext}(S)=\varnothing$ is an axiom, not a theorem derived from CCR membership. A protocol that confuses internal perturbations with external witnesses commits a category error: it uses rigidity under internal deformation, which CCR is meant to constrain, as evidence for a witness-relative comparison that requires a separately specified external extension. QICN has not verified that all downstream perturbation protocols respect this separation.
\end{remark}

\begin{nonclaim}[Empirical certification of $M_\Omega=+\infty$]\label{nonclaim:m-omega-empirical}
Theorem~\ref{thm:detectability} gives a finite-witness test for exact model comparisons. Finite data without a positive-deformation witness can also occur when the sector has a witness outside the tested family. With estimated distances, a positive witness additionally needs a certified error margin.
\end{nonclaim}

This quantity measures the \textit{minimal perturbation energy per unit identity deformation}.

\begin{proposition}[Relation to Rigidity]
Interpret $d_w(\mathcal I,\widetilde{\mathcal I})$ in the mass ratio as the Hausdorff distance in the common weighted history space. If for every admissible intervention of finite cost the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:stability} hold with a common $C$ and $D_w\leq A E_w$ for a common $A>0$, then $M_\Omega\geq 1/(CA)$. A single admissible finite-cost intervention with positive deformation proves $M_\Omega<+\infty$. If none exists, then $M_\Omega=+\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every positive finite-cost ratio has denominator at most $CA E_w$. A finite-cost positive witness contributes a finite ratio to the infimum. Without such a witness every ratio is infinite or the admissible set of positive deformations is empty.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Intervention Witnesses for Finite-Dimensional Latent Dynamics}

Consider $H_{t+1}=G(H_t,U_t)$ and $O_t=O(H_t)$ on a finite-dimensional latent state space. The observation precision and the intervention sector are declared independently of the dimension.

\begin{theorem}[Finite Resistance Requires an Admitted Deformation Witness]\label{thm:lml}
In a declared common metric space, finite resistance for a candidate of this class is equivalent to the existence of an admitted intervention of finite cost with positive Hausdorff deformation. Finite latent dimension and Lipschitz dynamics alone permit systems without such a witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A finite positive-denominator ratio gives a finite upper bound on the infimum; if no admissible finite-cost intervention has a positive denominator, the defining infimum is infinite. For a control take $G(h,u)=h/2$ on the real line, $O(h)=h$, and allow only changes of the input $u$. With a fixed initial state all these interventions leave the observable history and its declared compatible set fixed. Their costs may be finite and positive while the Hausdorff deformation is zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Formal Consequences}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Rigidity condition is summability.} A sufficient input to the Hausdorff estimate, together with its lifting and amplification hypotheses, is summability of weighted operator deformations: $\|\{\epsilon_t\}\|_w < \infty$. This is both checkable (in a model) and interpretable (total accumulated deformation bounded).
    
    \item \textbf{Sectorial deformation resistance is a resistance ratio.} $M_\Omega$ is an operational scalar: it links controlled external entropy to measurable deformation of the identity object $\mathcal{I}$. Divergence of $M_\Omega$ is the formal characterization of a CCR-type system.
    
    \item \textbf{Implementation impedance.} Finite dimension alone neither establishes nor excludes infinite resistance. A non-simulability comparison must specify its observable fidelity, representation budget and intervention sector.
    
    \item \textbf{Empirical testability.} The weighted norms and entropy measures provide explicit, implementable tests: apply controlled perturbation sequences $\{\delta U_t\}$, measure induced deformation $d_w$ on inferred inverse-limit classes (via observables), and compute the ratio. For exactly reconstructed distances, the tested infimum gives an upper bound on $M_\Omega$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Technical Notes and Limitations}

The proofs above rely on regularity assumptions (surjectivity, existence of right inverses, Lipschitz bounds) that are standard in stability analysis of inverse systems. In concrete implementations one must either verify them or replace with appropriate alternatives (e.g., selecting subsequences via Mittag--Leffler condition).

The weight sequence $\{w_t\}$ is a modeling choice; different choices correspond to different operational definitions of ``accumulated perturbation''. The resulting deformation bound depends on the declared weights and lift constants. Summability is not by itself infinite resistance, zero deformation or a topological isomorphism.

For stochastic perturbations, $H_t(\delta E_t)$ should be taken as expectation of the information measure; concentration bounds yield probabilistic versions of the theorems.

\subsection{Summary of Section 4}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item We modeled local perturbations and deformed projections and defined a weighted deformation norm $\|\{\epsilon_t\}\|_w$.
    \item We proved a weighted Hausdorff bound under exact fiber lifting and bounded amplification (Lemma~\ref{lem:stability}), and a homeomorphism under coherent coordinate transport (Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}).
    \item We formalized sectorial deformation resistance $M_\Omega$ as an operational scalar and related its divergence to CCR rigidity.
    \item Theorem~\ref{thm:lml} identifies the finite-cost deformation witness required for finite resistance.
\end{enumerate}

These results provide the formal backbone for the observational tests and uniqueness results developed in Sections 5--6.

\section{Observability and Sectorial Deformation Resistance}

\subsection{Observable Channel Model}

\begin{definition}[Observable Channel]
An observable channel is a triple $\mathcal{C} = (E, O, R)$, where:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item $E$ is the environment (unobserved state space),
    \item $O$ is a measurable output space,
    \item $R$ is a (possibly unknown) response mechanism producing observable outputs $O_t \in O$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Channel--projection composition]\label{def:channel-projection}
Let $C_{\mathrm{obs}}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{O}$ denote the observable map realized by the response mechanism of an observable channel, and let $\pi_{\infty\to t}:\mathcal{I}\to S_t$ be the canonical finite-level projection from the inverse limit. When a concrete model declares a map $\iota_t:S_t\to\mathcal{U}$ from finite-level states into the internal state domain, the composed finite-level observable is
\begin{equation}
    C_t := C_{\mathrm{obs}}\circ \iota_t\circ \pi_{\infty\to t} : \mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{O}.
\end{equation}
This composition is the typed route from the identity object $\mathcal{I}$ through a finite projection $S_t$ to observable outputs.
\end{definition}

\begin{nonclaim}[Composition existence]\label{nonclaim:channel-projection}
Definition~\ref{def:channel-projection} is a type discipline, not an empirical result. A concrete instantiation must still verify that $\pi_{\infty\to t}$, $\iota_t$, and $C_{\mathrm{obs}}$ are well-defined and measurable or continuous in the declared structures. QICN has not verified this composition for any concrete external protocol.
\end{nonclaim}

No access to internal states $S_t$ is assumed. The observer measures only the time series $\{O_t\}_{t \in \mathbb{N}}$.

\subsection{Inference of Inverse-Limit Classes from Observables}

\begin{definition}[Observable Equivalence]\label{def:obs-equiv}
Let $\mathcal H_O$ be the declared domain of admissible observable histories and $\mathcal D$ a nonempty family of finite-cost probes, including sham. For each $\delta\in\mathcal D$ require a specified response map $T_\delta:\mathcal H_O\to\mathcal H_O^{\delta}$ and reconstruction map $J_\delta$ into nonempty closed identity supports in a common comparison space. Define
\[
 O\sim_{\mathcal I}O'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall\delta\in\mathcal D,\quad d_H(J_\delta(T_\delta O),J_\delta(T_\delta O'))=0.
\]
\end{definition}

The quotient is $\mathcal H_O/{\sim_{\mathcal I}}$, not the quotient of the instantaneous output alphabet. Each zero-distance relation is an equivalence relation, and their intersection is one. This definition requires well-defined responses and a common Hausdorff comparison; it does not infer latent supports or causal effects from uncalibrated observations. Reconstruction error and identification against alternative models require separate evidence.

\subsection{Empirical Estimator of Sectorial Deformation Resistance}

Let $\{\delta E_t^{(k)}\}$, $k = 1, \ldots, N$, be a family of controlled perturbation protocols with known weighted energies $E_w^{(k)}$.

For the sham reconstruction and probe $\delta_k$, let $\tilde{O}^{(k)}=T_{\delta_k}O$ denote the perturbed observable history. Define the empirical deformation magnitude:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta^{(k)} := d_H(J_0(O),J_{\delta_k}(\tilde{O}^{(k)})),
\end{equation}
where the distance between the two sets is Hausdorff distance in a declared common history space. Its reconstruction from observed data requires a separate estimator with error control.

\begin{definition}[Empirical Sectorial Deformation Resistance Estimator]\label{def:emp-mass}
\begin{equation}
    \hat{M}_\Omega := \inf_{\substack{k = 1, \ldots, N \\ \Delta^{(k)} > 0}} \frac{E_w^{(k)}}{\Delta^{(k)}}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Detectability Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Finite Witnesses for Deformation Resistance]\label{thm:detectability}
Assume the specified intervention sector and common metric of Definition~\ref{def:ontmass}. For any finite family of interventions of finite cost with exactly known Hausdorff distances, its infimum ratio is an upper bound on $M_\Omega$. This infimum is finite exactly when the family contains a positive-deformation witness. Moreover $M_\Omega<+\infty$ exactly when the full sector contains such a finite-cost witness. Exhaustion by nested families gives convergence to $M_\Omega$ if their union contains witnesses with ratios arbitrarily close to the sector infimum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Taking an infimum over fewer ratios can only increase it. Every positive-deformation witness of finite cost gives a finite ratio. If no such witness exists, the infimum is infinite. The infima of nested families decrease, and the approximation hypothesis forces their limit to the sector infimum. Exact finite comparisons with no witness cannot distinguish sectors with an untested witness from sectors with none.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Observable Separation by Scale}

\begin{definition}[Observable Compatibility Operator]\label{def:phenom-compat}
Let $T_\Phi:(\mathcal I,d_w)\to(O,d_O)$ be the declared observable assignment.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Separation Modulus]\label{def:spectral-constant}
For $r>0$ such that $A_r=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal I^2:d_w(x,y)\ge r\}$ is nonempty, define
\[
 \omega_T(r)=\inf_{(x,y)\in A_r} d_O(T_\Phi(x),T_\Phi(y)).
\]
An empty $A_r$ gives no observable separation test at that scale.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Positive Separation at a Fixed Scale]\label{thm:spectral-coupling}
If $\mathcal I$ is a nonempty compact metric space and $T_\Phi$ is continuous and injective, then $\omega_T(r)>0$ at every scale in its domain. The infimum is attained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$A_r$ is closed in the compact product. The observable distance is continuous and strictly positive on $A_r$, since $r>0$ excludes the diagonal and $T_\Phi$ is injective. It therefore attains a positive minimum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Resolution and Coercivity]\label{cor:spectral-derivation}
If each of two observed values has error at most $\eta$ in $d_O$, then true separation in $d_w$ of at least $r$ gives measured separation at least $\omega_T(r)-2\eta$. A global linear bound requires a positive lower Lipschitz constant $c$, established or assumed for the stated domain.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the triangle inequality and the theorem. With a lower Lipschitz constant, $\omega_T(r)\ge cr$. Compactness and injectivity alone do not supply such a constant: $T(x)=x^2$ on $[0,1]$ has ratio $d_O(T(0),T(x))/d_w(0,x)=x$, although $\omega_T(r)=r^2$ for $0<r\le1$. Indeed $(x+r)^2-x^2\ge r^2$, with equality at $x=0$. A coercive example is the binary tower with first-disagreement metric $3^{-m}$ at position $m\ge1$ and ternary readout $\sum_{j\ge1}2b_j3^{-j}$. The leading term has size $2\cdot3^{-m}$ and the opposing tail has size at most $3^{-m}$, giving lower constant one and upper constant three.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:scale-vs-linear}
The separation modulus is metric and scale-dependent. No spectral interpretation is required.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Observational Information and Causal Invariance}

For discrete observed state $X$ with $0<H(X)<\infty$ and input $Y$, the statistic $\beth_q=1-I(X;Y)/H(X)$ describes the observed joint law.

\begin{proposition}[Observational Isolation Does Not Determine Interventional Invariance]\label{prop:causal-rigid}
Even $\beth_q=1$ does not determine whether the state law is invariant under interventions on $Y$. With a structural model $X=f(Y,Z)$ and a fixed exogenous law for $Z$, the law under $\mathrm{do}(Y=y)$ is the pushforward of that law by $f(y,\cdot)$. Invariance of these laws on a specified intervention family requires equality of the corresponding pushforwards.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Replace the equation for $Y$ by the constant $y$, leaving the exogenous law unchanged. For an observationally constant $Y=0$ and $Z$ Bernoulli with parameter $1/4$, both $X=Z$ and $X=Y\oplus Z$ give the same observational law and $\beth_q=1$. The first preserves the law under $\mathrm{do}(Y=1)$, whereas the second changes $\Pr(X=1)$ to $3/4$. The two models thus agree observationally and disagree interventionally.
\end{proof}

Law invariance and zero Hausdorff deformation of identity objects are separate conditions. For Definition~\ref{def:ontmass}, $M_\Omega=+\infty$ requires absence of positive-deformation witnesses of finite cost in the declared sector.

\subsection{Operational Protocol}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Apply controlled perturbations $\{\delta E_t^{(k)}\}$.
    \item Measure observable histories $\tilde{O}^{(k)}$.
    \item Compute equivalence classes $\mathcal{I}(\tilde{O}^{(k)})$.
    \item Compute $\Delta^{(k)}$.
    \item Estimate $\hat{M}_\Omega$.
\end{enumerate}

This protocol uses observable data when the declared readout supports calibrated reconstruction of the compared identity classes.

\subsection{Summary of Section 5}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Inferring identity deformation from observations requires a declared comparison and a reconstruction error bound.
    \item With exact distances, a finite tested infimum bounds the sector infimum from above.
    \item An infinite tested infimum establishes absence of a witness in the tested family. A sector-wide conclusion requires coverage of the sector.
    \item Observational independence leaves intervention invariance undetermined.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Uniqueness Theorem}

\subsection{Identity Channels and CCR Classification}

Recall that a channel $\mathcal{C}$ is said to be \textit{closed--rigid} (CCR) if its identity object $\mathcal{I} = \varprojlim S_t$ satisfies $M_\Omega = +\infty$.

We now restrict attention to a fixed observable channel $\mathcal{C}$ satisfying the assumptions of Sections 2--5 and exhibiting CCR behavior.

\subsection{Coexistence of Identities}

\begin{definition}[Coexisting Identities]\label{def:coexist}
Fix a common comparison of admitted identity objects, with the same separating object-deformation metric $d_{\mathrm{Id}}$ used in Definition~\ref{def:ontmass} (Hausdorff distance on the common closed supports), or first quotient zero-distance objects. Histories have a well-defined assignment $h\mapsto\mathcal I(h)$. An \emph{operating channel} $\mathcal C_\alpha$ is a nonempty class intrinsically connected by finite chains of support-preserving admissible finite-energy perturbations. The connecting moves have certified finite-energy reverses, or one uses mutual finite reachability. Finite concatenations remain admissible with finite total cost bounded by the sum of costs. Equality of observable outputs alone does not imply this connection.

Two assigned objects coexist in that channel when their generating histories lie in $\mathcal C_\alpha$, use the same inverse-system comparison, and both satisfy CCR in its declared perturbation sector. This explicitly refines the former output-only channel convention.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Strong Uniqueness Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Strong Uniqueness]\label{thm:uniqueness}
Within one operating channel of Definition~\ref{def:coexist}, at most one CCR identity assignment exists up to the declared zero-deformation equivalence, provided the connecting moves belong to that same CCR comparison sector. Two coexisting assignments therefore have $d_{\mathrm{Id}}(\mathcal I,\mathcal J)=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A finite connecting chain gives an admissible directed path between the generating histories, reversing certified moves when necessary. Its concatenation has finite total energy. Positive endpoint deformation would be a finite-cost positive-deformation witness in Definition~\ref{def:ontmass}, contradicting infinite mass. Separation in the declared comparison identifies the endpoints. Without the connection, two disconnected components may each carry a distinct invariant assignment; no such cross-channel uniqueness is proved.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Indivisibility and Non-Duplicability}

\begin{definition}[Typed product-retraction certificate]\label{def:atomicity-certificate}
Fix a system $S$, a compact identity support $I\subset Z$, its declared resistance sector $\mathcal D_S$, nonnegative intervention cost $E_w$, and Hausdorff deformation $\Delta(\delta)=d_H(I,I^\delta)$. A product datum $P=(A_P,B_P,F_P)$ has nonempty compact metric factors of positive diameter and a homeomorphism $F_P:I\to A_P\times B_P$ preserving the specified observations, dynamics and intervention structure. The admitted family is $\mathcal A_{\rm prod}$. Its projections are a dependent family
\[
 \mathcal R_1(P)=\operatorname{pr}_1\circ F_P:I\to A_P,
 \qquad \mathcal R_2(P)=\operatorname{pr}_2\circ F_P:I\to B_P.
\]
They are not two maps with a fixed codomain for every possible product. A certificate
\[
 \mathrm{Cert}_{\rm atom}(S)=
 (\mathcal A_{\rm prod},\mathcal R_1,\mathcal R_2,\delta_{\rm atom}),
 \qquad \delta_{\rm atom}>0,
\]
includes, for every admitted $P$, a specified $b_P\in B_P$ and a verified intervention $\delta_P\in\mathcal D_S$ satisfying
\[
 I^{\delta_P}=F_P^{-1}(A_P\times\{b_P\}),\qquad
 E_w(\delta_P)<\infty,\qquad
 \Delta(\delta_P)\ge\delta_{\rm atom}.
\]
The equality concerns the actual perturbed inverse-limit object, not only an arbitrary map on its ambient space. Compatibility of the clamp with the finite-level constraints, admissibility and cost must be checked independently. If a finite uniform cost bound is available, record $K_{\rm atom}=\sup_P E_w(\delta_P)<\infty$ in the sector specification. The deformation bound has units of the declared metric; $K_{\rm atom}/\delta_{\rm atom}$ has the resistance units. An empty product family gives a vacuous universal clause and supplies no measured positive margin by itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[A Checkable Nonempty Product Certificate]\label{prop:product-clamp-certificate}
Suppose the declared product sector uses isometries from $I$ onto products with the maximum metric, with $\operatorname{diam}(B_P)\ge d_0>0$. Suppose each factor clamp is realized by an admissible compatible intervention of cost at most $K$. Then the projections above and $\delta_{\rm atom}=d_0/2$ give a certificate. For a unit square with its coordinate product, a single admissible clamp $(x,y)\mapsto(x,0)$ of cost one gives the sharper values $\delta_{\rm atom}=K=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Hausdorff distance to $A_P\times\{b_P\}$ is $\sup_{b\in B_P}d(b,b_P)$. This is at least half the diameter by the triangle inequality, and is exactly one for the stated square clamp. The separate realization assumption supplies the required intervention and cost. On the square, a resistance sector consisting of this clamp has $M_\Omega=1>0$, yet the square is a nontrivial product. Thus neither a positive finite resistance nor the certificate alone proves atomicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cantor Product Obstruction to Unconditional Atomicity]\label{lem:cantor-product-obstruction}
Let $B=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N_{>0}}$ with its compact prefix topology and its canonical finite projections. Admit the maps $T_c(b)=b\mathbin\oplus c$ for $c\in B$, with cost $\sum_{j\ge1}3^{-j}c_j$. Every map preserves the full inverse-limit support, so its resistance is infinite. Nevertheless,
\[
 b\longmapsto((b_{2j-1})_{j\ge1},(b_{2j})_{j\ge1})
\]
is a homeomorphism onto $B\times B$. Both induced factor sectors also have infinite resistance. The retraction clause of Definition~\ref{def:atomicity-certificate} fails for this flip sector.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each flip is a compatible bijection at every finite cut, hence maps the full support onto itself and has zero Hausdorff deformation. Its cost is at most $1/2$. Any two histories differ by one such admissible flip, but this does not change the identity set. Interleaving is the continuous inverse of the displayed coordinate map; both factors are non-singleton compact prefix limits. Restricting the flips to odd or even coordinates again preserves each whole factor support. A clamp onto a fixed second factor is not surjective, so cannot be any admitted flip. Infinite resistance therefore does not furnish the missing certificate, even in this finite-cost connected history channel.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An Infinite-Dimensional Exact Product Obstruction]\label{thm:cutpoint-product-atomicity}
Let $Q=\{z\in\ell^2:0\le z_j\le2^{-j}\}$ and
\[
 W=(Q\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times Q)\subset\ell^2\oplus\ell^2.
\]
Then $W$ is a compact connected support with infinite affine dimension and is not homeomorphic to a product of two non-singleton compact spaces. This is an exact topological obstruction; it does not assert a positive approximation gap against an unspecified closure of products.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The coordinate tails are uniformly square summable; finite boxes give total boundedness and closedness gives completeness, hence compactness of $Q$. It is convex. Each axis segment $[0,2^{-j}]e_j$ occurs, so its affine dimension is infinite. The two copies meet at zero, making $W$ connected. Removing zero leaves precisely two nonempty separated connected pieces: the positive cone property ensures that a segment between nonzero points in either copy avoids zero.

If $W\simeq A\times B$, projection of the connected compact space makes both factors connected. Choose $a_0\ne a_*$ and $b_0\ne b_*$ for a point $(a_*,b_*)$. The connected skeleton $(A\times\{b_0\})\cup(\{a_0\}\times B)$ avoids that point. Every other point connects to this skeleton through a connected vertical slice if its first coordinate differs from $a_*$, and through a horizontal slice otherwise. Thus $(A\times B)\setminus\{(a_*,b_*)\}$ is connected. This contradicts the cut point of $W$.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[Atomicity Conditional on a Factorization-to-Deformation Certificate]\label{cor:indivisible}
Assume a verified $\mathrm{Cert}_{\rm atom}(S)$ in the fixed product-comparison sector of Definition~\ref{def:atomicity-certificate}. If $M_\Omega=+\infty$, there is no admitted nontrivial faithful factorization. With a finite uniform cost bound, the stronger quantitative implication is $M_\Omega>K_{\rm atom}/\delta_{\rm atom}\Rightarrow\mathcal A_{\rm prod}=\varnothing$. A statement about all products requires the sector to include every product structure under consideration.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
An admitted product supplies the certificate's verified intervention with $\Delta\ge\delta_{\rm atom}>0$ and finite cost. Hence $M_\Omega\le E_w(\delta_P)/\Delta(\delta_P)<\infty$, or $M_\Omega\le K_{\rm atom}/\delta_{\rm atom}$ when the uniform bound is available. Both contradict the respective premise. Lemma~\ref{lem:cantor-product-obstruction} and Proposition~\ref{prop:product-clamp-certificate} exclude inference from resistance alone; Theorem~\ref{thm:cutpoint-product-atomicity} provides an independent exact obstruction in infinite dimension.
\end{proof}
\subsubsection{Quantitative Product Separation in a Fixed Metric Sector}

All distances in this subsection use the Hilbert norm on $H\oplus H$, where
$H=\ell^2$. For the positive cube of Theorem~\ref{thm:cutpoint-product-atomicity}
write $\bar q=(2^{-j})_{j\ge1}$ and $R=\|\bar q\|=1/\sqrt3$.
The comparison sector is part of each statement; changing its distortion or
diameter bounds changes the conclusion.

\begin{proposition}[Cartesian Corner Gap]\label{prop:wedge-cartesian-gap}
For nonempty compact $A,B\subset H$ with both diameters at least $d_0>0$,
\[
 d_H(W,A\times B)\ge
 \delta_{\rm cart}(d_0):=\max\{d_0/2,R/2\}>0.
\]
In the fixed coordinate axes the bound $R/2$ holds even without the diameter
assumption and is attained by $(Q/2)\times(Q/2)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Some $a\in A$ and $b\in B$ satisfy $\|a\|,\|b\|\ge d_0/2$ by the triangle
inequality. The corner $(a,b)$ has distance at least
$\min\{\|a\|,\|b\|\}$ from the union of the two coordinate axes, hence from W.
For the other bound set $h=d_H(W,A\times B)$. Approximate $(\bar q,0)$ by
$(a_L,b_L)$ and $(0,\bar q)$ by $(a_R,b_R)$ within h. Then
$\|a_L\|,\|b_R\|\ge R-h$. Their mandatory product corner $(a_L,b_R)$ has
distance at least $R-h$ from W, giving $h\ge R-h$. For the stated sharp example,
halving a point on either arm approximates it within $R/2$, and every point
of $(Q/2)\times(Q/2)$ is within $R/2$ of an arm. Thus its Hausdorff distance is
exactly $R/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-Distortion Wedge Gap]\label{thm:wedge-bilipschitz-gap}
Let A and B be nonempty compact metric spaces of diameter at least $d_0>0$.
Give $A\times B$ the maximum metric. Suppose an embedding
$F:A\times B\to H\oplus H$ satisfies
\[
 c\,d_\infty(p,p')\le\|F(p)-F(p')\|\le C\,d_\infty(p,p'),
 \qquad 0<c\le C.
\]
Put
\[
 d_*:=\min\{d_0/2,1/(4C)\},\qquad
 \delta_W:=\frac{c^2d_*}{2C+4c}.
\]
Then $d_H(W,F(A\times B))\ge\delta_W>0$. Consequently, if a nonempty compact
$\widetilde W$ satisfies $d_H(W,\widetilde W)<r_W:=\delta_W/2$, its distance
from every product in this same $(d_0,c,C)$ sector exceeds $\delta_W/2$.
The factors need not be assumed path connected.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $h=d_H(W,F(A\times B))<\delta_W$. Since $d_*\le1/(4C)$ and $c\le C$,
$\delta_W\le1/24<1/8$. Choose $p_0=(a_*,b_*)$ with $\|F(p_0)\|\le h$,
and $p_L,p_R$ whose images are within h of
$w_L=(e_1/2,0)$ and $w_R=(0,e_1/2)$. For either endpoint p,
\[
 d_\infty(p,p_0)\ge(1/2-2h)/C\ge1/(4C)\ge d_*.
\]
Choose $a_0,b_0$ with $d_A(a_0,a_*)\ge d_0/2$ and
$d_B(b_0,b_*)\ge d_0/2$.

For every $\eta>2h$, the image is connected by finite chains with consecutive
distances less than $\eta$: approximate a sufficiently fine partition of a
path in W, including approximations of the endpoints. Pulling a chain back
through F and projecting shows that each factor is connected by chains of
step less than $\eta/c$. This is a chain statement, not an inference of path
connectedness of A or B.

Any domain point at distance at least $d_*$ from $p_0$ can be joined by such
factor chains to $(a_0,b_0)$ while staying at distance at least $d_*$ from
$p_0$. If its first coordinate is far, first change the second to $b_0$, then
the first to $a_0$; if its second is far, reverse the order. Concatenating
the paths for $p_L,p_R$ gives a product chain outside that ball, with steps
less than $\eta/c$. Its image has norm at least $cd_*-h$ and steps less than
$C\eta/c$. Approximate its points in W within h, choosing the endpoints to
be exactly $w_L,w_R$. The resulting W-chain has norms at least $cd_*-2h$
and steps less than $C\eta/c+2h$.

The assumed bound on h is exactly
$(2C/c+4)h<cd_*$. Hence choose $\eta>2h$ so close to $2h$ that
$C\eta/c+2h<cd_*-2h$. This positive lower bound on the chain norms excludes
zero. Consecutive points on different arms have distance at least the smaller
of their norms, so a chain with these step bounds cannot change arms.
Its prescribed endpoints do change arms, a contradiction. This also covers
$h=0$ by choosing a sufficiently small positive $\eta$.
Finally the Hausdorff triangle inequality gives
$d_H(\widetilde W,F(A\times B))\ge\delta_W-d_H(W,\widetilde W)>\delta_W/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Degenerating-Factor and Unbounded-Distortion Controls]
\label{prop:wedge-degenerate-controls}
There are embedded products arbitrarily close to W in each of these two
larger sectors: (i) uniformly bi-Lipschitz embeddings with one factor diameter
tending to zero; (ii) factors of uniformly positive diameter with no uniform
positive lower Lipschitz constant. Neither control is a Cartesian product
in the fixed axes of Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge-cartesian-gap}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $T_n$ insert a zero in the nth coordinate of the left copy of H, shifting
its tail by one and leaving the right copy unchanged. It is an isometric
linear injection, with range orthogonal to $e_n^L$. Uniformly on W,
\[
 \|T_nx-x\|\le2\Big(\sum_{j\ge n}4^{-j}\Big)^{1/2}
 =\frac4{\sqrt3}2^{-n}<3\cdot2^{-n}.
\]
Set $\varepsilon_n=2^{-n}$. For (i) take factors W and
$[0,\varepsilon_n]$ and $F_n(x,b)=T_nx+be_n^L$.
Orthogonality gives constants $c=1,C=\sqrt2$ for the maximum domain metric,
and $d_H(W,F_n(W\times[0,\varepsilon_n]))\le4\cdot2^{-n}$.
For (ii) use factors W and $[0,1]$ with
$G_n(x,b)=T_nx+\varepsilon_nbe_n^L$. The same Hausdorff bound holds, but
the lower constant is $\varepsilon_n$, while $C\le\sqrt2$ and both factor
diameters exceed $1/2$. Continuity and injectivity on compact domains give
embeddings. Thus dropping either uniform hypothesis destroys a general gap.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Protected and Damage Sectors: a Conditional Energy Contract}

\begin{proposition}[Operational Barrier and Cheap-Damage Contrast]
\label{prop:wedge-energy-regimes}
On W let $D_{s_L,s_R}(x,y)=((1-s_L)x,(1-s_R)y)$,
$s_L,s_R\in[0,1]$, and $s=\max\{s_L,s_R\}$. These maps commute with all
finite coordinate cuts and have deformation $\Delta=sR$. In a unit-duration
quadratic actuation model their minimum worst-state motion energy is
$s^2R^2$. Declare two sectors of completed protocols, both including all these
maps: a damage sector with this motion energy alone; and a protected sector
requiring a completed unlock of energy at least $B=1$ before any motion.
Then
\[
 M_{\rm damage}=0,\qquad
 M_{\rm protected}=R+R^{-1}=4/\sqrt3>2/3.
\]
The actual damage clamp $D_{0,1}$ has cost $K_{\rm atom}=1/3$ and
deformation $R\ge\delta_{\rm atom}=1/2$, so it enforces
$M_{\rm damage}\le K_{\rm atom}/\delta_{\rm atom}=2/3$.
The protected sector admits that clamp only at cost $4/3>K_{\rm atom}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The shrunk arms are contained in W. Clipping each coordinate at its new
endpoint gives distance at most $sR$; the tip of the most shrunk arm attains
it, since the other arm is no closer. Linear motion realizes energy
$s^2R^2$; the integral Cauchy--Schwarz inequality at that tip proves the lower
bound for every unit-duration path with the same endpoints. Model the unlock
by a scalar absolutely continuous coordinate a, stationary plant during
unlock, $a(0)=0,a(1)=1$, and cost $B\int_0^1|\dot a|^2dt\ge B$.
Motion is permitted only after completion; an aborted unlock changes no
support. The linear unlock achieves B. Preparation and unlock costs are
included afresh in every qualified protocol, with no free previously opened
actuator. Hence for $s>0$ the minimal protected cost is $1+s^2R^2$.
The damage ratios are $sR$, with infimum zero as $s\downarrow0$.
For the protected ratios, with $t=sR\le R<1$,
\[
 t+t^{-1}-(R+R^{-1})=(R-t)(1/(tR)-1)\ge0.
\]
The full clamp attains the lower bound. At depth m the same equations have
$R_m^2=\sum_{j=1}^m4^{-j}$; compatible clamps and costs converge to the
displayed limits. No incompatible finite-stage retraction has been substituted.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Meaning of the energy hypothesis.}
This is a specified ideal hybrid actuator model with dimensionless operational
cost, not a measured device or a derivation of work from entropy. It can be
represented in the nonnegative weighted cost convention by assigning the
complete protocol cost to one positive-weight event. Identifying it with
physical joules needs an independent calibration and admissibility argument.
An energy barrier merely greater than K does not by itself bound E/Delta;
here the explicit deformation and total cost establish the quotient bound.
Admitting slow unbounded-duration unlocks, free preparation, or motion before
unlock would change the sector and requires a new calculation.

\paragraph{Conditional clamp, not simultaneous cheap actuation.}
In Corollary~\ref{cor:indivisible} the implication from a hypothetical faithful
factorization to a compatible clamp of cost at most $1/3$ and deformation at
least $1/2$ is a constitutive comparison hypothesis. The strict protected
resistance excludes such a product; it does not assert that its cheap clamp
actually exists. Nonemptiness of this implication's reference class is
checkable on $Q\times Q$, where a coordinate clamp has motion cost $1/3$ and
deformation R. Faithfulness must preserve the declared cost, metric and
finite-cut actuation; arbitrary homeomorphisms do not supply it.
W has its independent topological and bounded-distortion metric obstructions,
so no measured margin is deduced from a vacuous product family.

\paragraph{Nonseparating intervention control.}
The identity and arm exchange on W commute with cuts and preserve the full
support. A sector generated by them has $\Delta=0$ and infinite resistance,
but supplies no proper clamp. On the factorizable support $Q\times Q$ the
same support-preserving exchange sector again has infinite resistance.
It is therefore invalid to infer atomicity from that sector's resistance.
W itself remains nonfactorizable by its separate cut-point argument; a failed
intervention certificate is not a counterexample to that topological theorem.

\paragraph{Atomicity research obligation.}
The binary-prefix limit is homeomorphic to the product of its even and odd bit sequences; its internal support-preserving flip sector can still have infinite resistance. Thus resistance or connected-channel uniqueness alone does not prove product atomicity. The program retains atomicity as a sector-specific objective: identify the permitted factors, the preserved observations and the required deformation certificate. No universal non-duplication of persons or information is inferred.
For the Hilbert wedge, Theorem~\ref{thm:wedge-bilipschitz-gap} additionally
closes a quantitative approximate non-factorization problem at fixed
$(d_0,c,C)$, with radius $\delta_W/2$. Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge-degenerate-controls}
shows exactly why that conclusion cannot be extended to arbitrary embeddings.
The operational energy regimes and conditional clamp implication remain
separate from this metric proof and from physical calibration.

\begin{corollary}[No Distinct Assignment by a Matched Finite-Cost Replication]\label{cor:nodup}
In the connected comparison of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness}, an admitted finite-cost replication cannot produce an assignment at positive separating deformation distance. Copying an encoding or creating a different operating component is not excluded by this result.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Such an endpoint would be the forbidden finite-cost positive-deformation witness. The claim concerns assignments in the same sector and component, not all conceivable replication operations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cardinality of Realized Assignment Families}
Fix a set $\mathfrak C$ of operating components, each admitting a CCR assignment in the separating comparison of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness}.
\begin{proposition}[Cardinality Bound]\label{prop:cardinality}
The assignment $C\mapsto[\mathcal I_C]$ is well-defined and its image has cardinality at most $|\mathfrak C|$. It is injective only if different components are separately known to carry different assignment classes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Existence is part of the realized-domain hypothesis and uniqueness gives a single class per component. The assignment surjects onto its image, giving the cardinal bound in the usual set-theoretic setting. Two disjoint copies of the same controlled component can carry the same assignment; within-component uniqueness does not distinguish them.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Summary of Section 6}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Realized connected comparison components admit one assignment class; uniqueness alone asserts at most one.
    \item The product-retraction certificate gives sector-relative atomicity, and the cut-point example gives an independent exact obstruction; matched finite-cost changes cannot produce a distinct assignment.
    \item Distinct assignment classes cannot belong to one matched connected component; the converse requires separation between components.
    \item Identity is therefore a structural invariant of the channel, not an interchangeable role.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Scope}

\subsection{Dependence on Structural Hypotheses}

All results in Sections 3--6 are conditional on the structural hypotheses introduced in Section 2 and the regularity hypotheses H1--H2 in Section 4. In particular:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Surjectivity (H1) ensures non-emptiness and constructibility of inverse limits.
    \item Metric regularity (H2) enables quantitative stability bounds and the definition of sectorial deformation resistance.
\end{itemize}

If either condition fails, the inverse limit may be empty or ill-defined, and the rigidity theorems do not apply.

\subsection{Boundary of Applicability}

The present framework applies exclusively to systems that admit:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item A projective representation $(S_t, \pi_{t+1 \to t})$;
    \item Non-reactive persistence (R0);
    \item Summability of projection deformations under finite-energy perturbations.
\end{enumerate}

Systems lacking any of these properties fall outside the scope of this theory. This includes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Systems whose admissible states are not projectively compatible;
    \item Systems whose admissibility is determined by progressive causal dynamics;
    \item Systems whose perturbations are not summable in the weighted operator norm.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limits of Empirical Detectability}

While Section 5 establishes an operational estimator $\hat{M}_\Omega$, detectability remains limited by:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item The resolution of observable channels;
    \item The admissibility and controllability of perturbation protocols;
    \item Finite sampling constraints.
\end{itemize}

Hence, empirical divergence of $\hat{M}_\Omega$ can support the declared CCR pattern within the estimator model, but finite estimates cannot conclusively prove infinite mass. The theory admits falsifiability but not definitive confirmation.
This estimator is therefore a formal operational criterion for structured tests, not a claim of empirical closure for any particular physical architecture.

\subsection{Non-Equivalence to Behavioral Indistinguishability}

The theory does not identify rigid identity with behavioral or functional equivalence. Two systems may be behaviorally indistinguishable while differing in their sectorial deformation resistance and rigidity class.

Thus, passing any behavioral test (including classical Turing-style tests) does not entail CCR classification.

\subsection{Relation to Existing Frameworks}

This framework is not reducible to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Information integration theories (IIT) \cite{tononi2004};
    \item Global workspace architectures (GWT) \cite{baars1988};
    \item Computational functionalism;
    \item Standard dynamical systems theory \cite{katok1995}.
\end{itemize}

However, it can be viewed as orthogonal: it defines structural invariants that may coexist with these frameworks without contradiction.

\subsection{Open Problems}

The following problems are explicitly left open:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Characterization of minimal projective representations for arbitrary systems;
    \item Necessary and sufficient conditions for $M_\Omega = +\infty$;
    \item Concrete instantiations satisfying H1--H2 on known computational substrates;
    \item Generalization to continuous-time index sets;
    \item Alternative observability metrics beyond $d_w$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

\subsection{Summary of Formal Results}

This work establishes a closed, minimal, and falsifiable mathematical framework for rigid identity as a categorical invariant of observable channels.

Starting from the minimal persistence restriction $R_0$, we derived:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Existence and necessity of inverse-limit identity.} We proved that any non-reactive persistent system satisfying the stated observational hypotheses admits a projective representation and that its identity is represented by the inverse limit $\mathcal{I} = \varprojlim S_t$. Identity is therefore not a local state, not a progressive construction, and not a finite-time object.
    
    \item \textbf{Rigidity under perturbations.} We bounded Hausdorff deformation under two-sided exact lifting and exhibited coherent transport for a ramified circle tower. The weighted projection discrepancy alone does not control the topological type.
    
    \item \textbf{Sectorial deformation resistance as a scalar invariant.} We defined sectorial deformation resistance $M_\Omega$ as the minimal perturbation energy per unit deformation of identity, yielding an operationally definable scalar invariant. Divergence of $M_\Omega$ characterizes closed--rigid (CCR) channels.
    
    \item \textbf{External observability.} We stated the exact finite-witness criterion and separated it from estimation of $\hat{M}_\Omega$ from observations.
    
    \item \textbf{Strong uniqueness.} We proved assignment uniqueness within a matched finite-cost connected component. This does not establish product atomicity or exclusion of replication into other components.
    
    \item \textbf{Implementation constraints.} The intervention class determines which positive-deformation witnesses are available. The binary-budget bound uses a separate observation model.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Structural Significance}

Together, these results establish rigid identity as a structural invariant rather than a behavioral, biological, or phenomenological construct. Identity is formalized as a global compatibility constraint encoded in inverse-limit geometry, not as an emergent narrative, computational artifact, or localized state variable.

The framework separates three distinct layers:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Local dynamics:} the admissible states $S_t$;
    \item \textbf{Global constraint:} the inverse-limit object $\mathcal{I}$;
    \item \textbf{Operational resistance:} the scalar invariant $M_\Omega$.
\end{itemize}

This separation clarifies which properties are internal, which are externally observable, and which are structurally irreducible.

\subsection{Consequences for System Classification}

The classification of observable channels into open (CA), semi-closed (CSC), and closed--rigid (CCR) categories follows directly from the sectorial deformation resistance invariant. This yields a principled method for distinguishing:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item systems that merely simulate persistence,
    \item systems that maintain partial causal closure,
    \item systems that instantiate structurally rigid identity.
\end{itemize}

Such classification is independent of narrative descriptions, substrate implementation, and phenomenological interpretation.

\subsection{Final Statement}

Under explicit minimal assumptions, rigid identity is a model-determined consequence of non-reactive persistence and global compatibility constraints. Its Hausdorff stability, coherent transport, and finite deformation witnesses follow under their stated model hypotheses.

The contribution of this work is therefore not the introduction of a new metaphor of identity, but the construction of a closed mathematical theory in which identity appears as a canonical categorical invariant of certain observable channels.

\subsection{Ontological Closure Statement}

Under the stated axioms and regularity conditions, the inverse-limit identity $\mathcal{I}$ is a structurally necessary object. Each nonempty intrinsically connected CCR operating channel with a well-defined assignment and common perturbation sector admits exactly one assignment up to the declared comparison. This does not identify disconnected channels, and invariance is restricted to the finite-energy perturbations in that sector.

The result is formal and model-relative. Empirical realization requires independent identification of its hypotheses; personal and phenomenal interpretations remain open bridge questions.

\subsection{Category of Minimal Observation}

\begin{definition}[Category of Minimal Observation]
Let $\mathbf{Obs}$ be the category whose objects are nonempty state spaces $S_t$ and whose morphisms are surjective maps
\begin{equation}
    \pi_{t+1 \to t}: S_{t+1} \to S_t
\end{equation}
satisfying the compatibility condition
\begin{equation}
    \pi_{t+1 \to t} \circ \pi_{t+2 \to t+1} = \pi_{t+2 \to t}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
$\mathbf{Obs}$ is the minimal category in which persistent observability is definable. No continuity, memory, dynamics, or identity is assumed.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Forced Emergence of the Inverse Limit}

\begin{theorem}[No--Alternative Representation]\label{thm:noalt}
Let $q_t:J\to S_t$ be coherent: $\pi_{t+1\to t}q_{t+1}=q_t$. A faithful complete encoding means joint separation ($q_t(j)=q_t(k)$ for every $t$ implies $j=k$) and exhaustivity (every compatible history equals $(q_t(j))_t$ for some $j$). Then $Q(j)=(q_t(j))_t$ is the unique bijection $J\to\mathcal I$ commuting with cut evaluations. It is a topological isomorphism if $J$ is compact, $\mathcal I$ Hausdorff, and all $q_t$ continuous.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Coherence puts $Q(j)$ in the inverse limit. Joint separation gives injectivity, exhaustivity gives surjectivity, and coordinates determine the only commuting map. A continuous bijection from compact to Hausdorff is a homeomorphism. Separation alone allows proper subfamilies; exhaustivity alone allows duplicate copies. The conditions formalize faithful complete coding, rather than infer it from the undefined phrase ``represents persistence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Necessity of Retroinductive Structure}

\begin{theorem}[Collapse under Pure Progressive Causality]\label{thm:collapse}
Suppose $f_t:S_t\to S_{t+1}$ satisfies $\pi_{t+1\to t}f_t=\mathrm{id}_{S_t}$. Set $B_0=S_0$, $B_{t+1}=f_t(B_t)$ and restrict histories to $x_{t+1}=f_t(x_t)$. The restricted bonding map $B_{t+1}\to B_t$ is bijective with inverse $f_t|_{B_t}$, and the orbital inverse limit is canonically bijective to $S_0$ (homeomorphic for continuous maps on compact Hausdorff stages). The original unrestricted limit need not collapse.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The section equation makes $f_t$ injective; it is onto $B_{t+1}$ by definition. On that image $f_t\pi_{t+1\to t}=\mathrm{id}$ as well. Each initial point gives one orbit, inverse to evaluation at zero. In the binary tower, appending zero is a compatible section but the original deletion map still has two preimages. A function forward does not make the original backward map bijective.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Projective Degeneracy]\label{def:degeneracy}
The system is projectively degenerate when some canonical evaluation $p_T:\mathcal I\to S_T$ is bijective: the history is recoverable through that specified cut. Under active surjectivity this is equivalent to bijective bonding maps at every level $t\ge T$. Non-degeneracy means no such canonical evaluation exists. An arbitrary set isomorphism $\mathcal I\cong S_T$ is not the criterion; the previous unqualified notation failed to enforce this and is explicitly replaced.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
Projective degeneracy is the formal characterization of ``collapse to a finite prefix.'' Under non-degeneracy, the compatible history contains information beyond every canonical finite prefix.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Necessity of Retroinduction]
Breaking purely progressive causality is necessary under the non-degeneracy condition. Minimal retroinduction is structurally required within that model class.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The $\aleph$--Operator as Condition of Existence}{The Aleph-Operator as Condition of Existence}}

\begin{definition}[Minimal Projection Operator]
Given an ambient history $\Psi$ and a declared metric $d$, define the set of nearest compatible histories by
\begin{equation}
    \aleph(\Psi) := \arg\min_{\Phi \in \mathcal{I}} d(\Phi, \Psi).
\end{equation}
Existence and uniqueness require the separate conditions below.
\end{definition}

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Existence and Uniqueness of $\aleph$}{Existence and Uniqueness of Aleph}}

\begin{definition}[Weighted Sum Metric for the Minimization Problem]\label{def:weighted-metric}\leavevmode\par
Use $d(\Phi,\Psi)=\sum_{t\geq0}\lambda^t d_t(\phi_t,\psi_t)$, where $0<\lambda<1$ and $\sum_t\lambda^t\operatorname{diam}(S_t)<\infty$. The diameter sum controls tails uniformly. This is a different metric from the weighted supremum in Definition~\ref{def:weighted-sup-metric}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Existence of Minimizers and Conditional Uniqueness of $\aleph$]\label{thm:aleph-unique}
For a nonempty inverse limit of compact metric stages with continuous bonding maps and the diameter-sum condition above, the objective $d(\Phi,\Psi)$ attains its minimum over $\mathcal I$ for every ambient product point $\Psi$. If $\mathcal I$ is convex in a specified real linear ambient space and this objective is strictly convex on $\mathcal I$, the minimizer is unique.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Closed compatibility equations make $\mathcal I$ compact in the product. The objective is a uniform limit of continuous partial sums by the diameter bound. A continuous real objective on a nonempty compact set attains a minimum. Under the additional convexity assumptions two distinct minimizers would give a strictly smaller value at their midpoint. For a control with surjective maps, take $S_t=\{0,1\}$ with the discrete metric, identity bonding maps and $\lambda=1/2$. The ambient point $(0,1,1,\ldots)$ has distance $1$ to both constant histories, which are the only feasible points.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Nearest-Point Selection Is an Extra Requirement]
A selector from $\aleph(\Psi)$ returns a compatible history when the minimizer set is nonempty. Compatibility of the output alone allows any map into $\mathcal I$ and does not force nearest-point selection.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every member of $\aleph(\Psi)$ lies in $\mathcal I$. In the two-history example, the constant map to the all-one history always returns a compatible history, but does not minimize distance at the all-zero history. The limit is defined by its bonding maps regardless of which map is used to select histories.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Structural Causal Closure}

\begin{definition}[Structural Causal Closure]\label{def:causal-closure}
A system is \textit{structurally causally closed} if for every extension $E$, the inverse limit identity satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \varprojlim S_t \cong \varprojlim S_t^{(E)}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
This reformulation of causal closure is purely categorical: it requires invariance of the inverse limit under extensions, without appealing to information-theoretic quantities. This makes the definition operationally precise and independent of Shannon-theoretic assumptions.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Prefix Recovery and Information]\label{thm:info-isolation}
For a canonical projection $p_t:\mathcal I\to S_t$, exact recovery of every history from its prefix is possible precisely when $p_t$ is injective, with a decoder defined on its image. If $P=p_t(X)$ is a finite-valued prefix of a random history $X$ under a declared law, then $I(P;X)=H(P)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A left inverse exists precisely for an injective projection, by taking its inverse on the image. A choice of one extension per prefix is a right inverse and need not recover the original history. The binary tower admits a right inverse by padding with zero bits. For the information identity, $H(P\mid X)=0$ because $P$ is a function of $X$, so $I(P;X)=H(P)-H(P\mid X)=H(P)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A Law-Dependent Isolation Statistic}

\begin{definition}[Normalized Conditional Entropy]\label{def:isolation-functor}
For finite-valued $(X,Y)$ with a declared joint law and $H(X)>0$, set $\mathfrak B(X;Y)=H(X\mid Y)/H(X)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Coarsening and Relabeling]\label{lem:isolation-properties}
For fixed $X$, replacing $Y$ by a function of $Y$ cannot decrease $\mathfrak B$. Bijective relabelings of the random variables preserve it. It vanishes precisely when $X$ is determined by $Y$ almost surely under the declared law.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first statement follows by data processing for mutual information and the fixed denominator. Relabeling preserves the probabilities used by entropy. For finite variables, conditional entropy is zero precisely when every conditional law of $X$ on a positive-probability value of $Y$ is concentrated at one point.
\end{proof}

Almost-sure recovery can hold on a chosen law even if the projection has multiple extensions on the full space. This statistic requires a law and does not classify projective systems by itself.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:law-dependent-isolation}
The normalization is defined only when the chosen law gives positive finite entropy.
\end{remark}

\subsection{MIR as the Unique Compatible Geometry}

\begin{definition}[MIR Geometry]\label{def:mir}
The \textit{Manifold of Retroactive Inversion} (MIR) is defined as the class of all non-degenerate projective systems in $\mathbf{C}$ admitting a non-trivial inverse limit identity. Formally:
\begin{equation}
    \text{MIR}:=\{(S_t,\pi_{t+1\to t})\in\mathbf{Obs}:p_T\text{ is not bijective for every }T\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
This definition avoids ambiguity: ``MIR'' is not a differential manifold in the classical sense, but a \textit{class of projective systems} satisfying non-degeneracy. The term ``geometry'' refers to the compatibility structure imposed by the projective system, not to smooth structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[No Semigroup Compatibility]\label{thm:nosemigroup}
If $p_0$ is noninjective, no compatible deterministic forward family generates all projective histories as its orbital subsystem. Forward semigroups on spaces already storing complete histories are not excluded.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} gives one orbital history per initial point, whereas the unrestricted limit has two over some initial point. The restriction to orbital histories and compatibility with the bonding maps are essential.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Structural Uniqueness Lemma (Bridge Lemma)}

This subsection separates uniqueness of an assignment in a connected comparison, reconstruction by projective prefixes and temporal attraction under an independently specified contraction.

\begin{lemma}[Structural Coupling: Observable--Identity]\label{lem:bridge-identity}
Use the connected operating channel and separating comparison of Definition~\ref{def:coexist}, with CCR for its connecting perturbations. Its observable--identity assignment is unique up to zero deformation by Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness}. For a compatible point-history $x$, if $w_t\operatorname{diam}(S_t)\to0$, every compatible completion $x^{[N]}$ agreeing through cut $N$ satisfies
\[
 d_w(x^{[N]},x)\le\sup_{t>N}w_t\operatorname{diam}(S_t)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is prefix reconstruction, not temporal attraction. If additionally a contraction $T$ acts on a nonempty complete metric space of admitted identity candidates, with $d(Ta,Tb)\le qd(a,b)$, $0\le q<1$, it has a unique attracting fixed point. Identification with the channel assignment further requires that fixed candidate to belong to the same comparison channel.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The finite-energy connecting chain gives uniqueness as in Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness}; shared outputs alone do not supply that path. Prefix agreement leaves only the displayed weighted tail. Surjectivity supplies completions. Iterating the additional contraction gives a Cauchy orbit, a limit by completeness, and a fixed point by continuity. The inequality $d(a,b)\le qd(a,b)$ proves fixed-point uniqueness. CCR alone supplies neither a temporal map nor that estimate. In particular $p_t(x)\in S_t$ lives in different stage spaces, so an untyped limit of these points cannot prove attraction in $\mathcal I$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Identity Forcing under CCR]\label{cor:identity-forcing}
In an energy-connected CCR operating channel with a well-defined assignment into the separating comparison, all assigned identities coincide. An attracting realization additionally needs the dynamical hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge-identity}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness} for assignment uniqueness and the conditional contraction conclusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:bridge-identity} for attraction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The lemma separates assignment uniqueness, reconstruction and dynamical attraction under distinct hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Existence is determined by projective compatibility;
    \item Uniqueness uses CCR and intrinsic channel connection;
    \item Temporal attraction needs the stated contraction; infinite mass alone gives no temporal dynamics.
\end{itemize}
Within the same observable channel and hypotheses, no distinct compatible identity object is available.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Point Histories and Compatible Probability Laws}

\begin{definition}[Projective Observable Limit]\label{def:proj-obs-limit}
For finite nonempty discrete spaces $S_t$ and surjective bonding maps, let $\mathcal I=\varprojlim S_t$ with the subspace product topology. Let $O=\varprojlim\mathrm{Prob}(S_t)$ with pushforward bonding maps and the subspace product topology of the finite probability simplexes.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Topology of Point and Law Limits]\label{thm:stone-classification}
The point limit $\mathcal I$ is nonempty, compact, metrizable, and has a clopen basis. The law limit $O$ is nonempty, compact, metrizable, and convex under coordinatewise mixing. If $O$ contains distinct points, it is path connected and is not a Stone space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Surjectivity gives successive lifts of a point, hence a compatible history. The compatibility equations are closed in each product, so both limits are closed subspaces of compact metrizable products. Finite-coordinate cylinders give a clopen basis for the point limit. The point history supplies compatible Dirac measures, proving nonemptiness of the law limit. Pushforward is affine, so coordinatewise mixtures are compatible. The segment joining two distinct compatible laws is continuous and connected. For the constant two-point tower, $O$ is the full probability interval $[0,1]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Embedding by Compatible Dirac Measures]\label{cor:obs-morphism}
The map $x\mapsto(\delta_{x_t})_t$ embeds $\mathcal I$ homeomorphically as a closed subspace of $O$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each coordinate Dirac map is continuous and injective. Compatibility follows from pushforward of point masses. The product map on histories is continuous and injective, with compact domain and Hausdorff codomain, so it is a homeomorphism onto its closed image.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:point-vs-law-topology}
The Dirac image carries the topology of histories of points; mixtures belong to the law limit.
\end{remark}

\section{Formal Extensions}

This appendix presents advanced formal extensions that strengthen the main results and provide additional structure for future work.

\subsection{Canonical Minimal CCR Example}

We provide a concrete mathematical example demonstrating that CCR systems exist and are non-trivial.

\begin{example}[Canonical Minimal CCR]\label{ex:minimal-ccr}
Let $S_t=[0,1]\times\{0,1\}^t$, with $d_t((u,a),(v,b))=\max\{|u-v|,\mathbf1_{a\ne b}\}$, $w_t=2^{-t}$, and bonding maps deleting the last bit while retaining $u$. Its actual coordinate cone identifies
\[
 \mathcal I\cong[0,1]\times\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N_{>0}}.
\]
Declare an internal sector of coherent bit-flip automorphisms: for $A\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}$, $h_t$ flips $A\cap\{1,\ldots,t\}$, at the declared dimensionless cost $E(A)=\sum_{j\in A}2^{-j}$, using the stage-injection costs $H_j(A)=\mathbf1_{j\in A}$ for $j\ge1$ and $H_0=0$. These homeomorphisms commute with deletion. Operating channels for point preparations may fix $u$ and use the mutually reversible bit-flip moves.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal CCR is Non-Degenerate and Rigid]
The example has compact stages, summable weighted diameter tails, two-point fibres at each bonding map and $2^t$ prefixes over each initial $u$. Every $p_T$ is noninjective. Its identity-support mass is $M_\Omega=+\infty$ in the specified internal sector.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A compatible family specifies its infinite bit sequence. Either value of each new bit is allowed, and changing bit $T+1$ preserves cut $T$. Each stage has diameter one, so the weighted tail vanishes. Every admitted flip has $E(A)\le1$ and maps the full compatible support onto itself. Its Hausdorff deformation is zero, so no positive-deformation witness occurs in this sector and Definition~\ref{def:ontmass} gives infinite mass. Individual point-histories can move by positive distance; the support claim does not identify those points.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The former identity-link example was canonically degenerate, so its non-degeneracy claim was false. This replacement is genuinely ramified, although every stage and its limit still have continuum cardinality. Rigidity is conditional on the declared support-preserving sector, not implied by branching. Finite-cost changes of the whole support can give finite mass, as Example~\ref{ex:coherent-rotation-tower} shows. No immunity to every perturbation, nor minimality among all realizations, follows.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Symbolic CCR System}

We provide a second canonical example using symbolic dynamics, demonstrating that CCR systems are not dependent on continuum-based constructions.

\begin{example}[Symbolic CCR System]\label{ex:symbolic-ccr}
Let:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item $S_t = \{0, 1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$, endowed with the product topology and the metric
    \begin{equation}
        d_t(x, y) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 2^{-k} |x_k - y_k|.
    \end{equation}
    \item Define the projections $\pi_{t+1 \to t}$ as the left shift operator:
    \begin{equation}
        \pi_{t+1 \to t}(x_1 x_2 x_3 \ldots) = (x_2 x_3 x_4 \ldots).
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

This is a projectively non-degenerate inverse system. Infinite mass is asserted only in a separately declared coherent support-preserving sector, not against all perturbations.
\end{example}

\begin{proof}
The shift operator is surjective and continuous. The inverse limit is non-degenerate since the shift does not collapse to a finite prefix. Only in that support-preserving sector does the empty-positive-deformation-witness argument of Example~\ref{ex:minimal-ccr} give $M_\Omega=+\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This example connects the framework to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Symbolic dynamics and subshifts;
    \item Automata theory and formal languages;
    \item Profinite structures in algebra.
\end{itemize}
The existence of CCR systems is therefore universal across mathematical disciplines.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Conditional Non-Simulability}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Non-Simulability from a Finite-Cost Witness]\label{thm:non-simulability}
For any candidate simulator with a declared intervention sector and common comparison metric, suppose there is an admissible finite-cost intervention producing positive Hausdorff deformation. Then $M_\Omega<+\infty$ in that sector. It cannot faithfully realize an infinite-resistance target if faithfulness is required to preserve the resistance class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The witness contributes one finite ratio to the defining infimum. Preservation of the resistance class would require the same finite versus infinite classification. A finite-dimensional state or finite context alone does not supply the witness or specify which observations a simulator must preserve.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transfer under Matched Intervention Sectors}

\begin{theorem}[Resistance under Audited Transport]\label{thm:non-transferability}\leavevmode\par
Let an ambient bijection $A$ satisfy $c\,d(x,y)\leq d'(Ax,Ay)\leq C\,d(x,y)$ with $0<c\leq C<\infty$. It maps the baseline identity to the target identity. Suppose a bijection between the declared intervention sectors maps $\delta$ to $\delta'$ with $A(I_\delta)=I'_{\delta'}$ and $a E(\delta)\leq E'(\delta')\leq b E(\delta)$ for $0<a\leq b<\infty$. For nonempty baseline and deformed sets at finite Hausdorff distance, the two resistance values satisfy
\[
 \frac{a}{C}M_\Omega\leq M'_\Omega\leq\frac{b}{c}M_\Omega.
\]
In particular such a transport preserves the finite versus infinite resistance class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Applying the ambient distance bounds to each directed infimum and supremum gives the same bounds on Hausdorff distance. Positive-deformation witnesses correspond in both directions. Dividing the matched cost and deformation inequalities and taking infima gives the result, including the empty-witness convention and infinite costs.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Composition of Audited Comparisons}

\begin{lemma}[Closure under Composed Sector Comparisons]\label{lem:canonical-closure}
Two consecutive transports satisfying Theorem~\ref{thm:non-transferability} compose to a transport of the same kind. If both also intertwine the specified readouts exactly, their composition does so.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The ambient and intervention bijections compose and the upper and lower constants multiply. For matched readouts $F_1,F_2,F_3$ and transports $A_1,A_2$, the equalities $F_2 A_1=F_1$ and $F_3 A_2=F_2$ give $F_3 A_2 A_1=F_1$.
\end{proof}

A morphism of projective systems gives a map on compatible histories. The finite-budget and resistance obstructions depend respectively on the stated response family and on the matched intervention sector. Inclusions that enlarge the set of histories require their own comparison data.

\subsection{Rigidity Hierarchy}

\begin{definition}[Rigidity Degree]
For a projective system $(S_t, \pi_{t+1 \to t})$, define the rigidity degree $\rho \in [0, \infty]$ as:
\begin{equation}
    \rho := \sup \left\{ r \geq 0 \;\Big|\; \forall \{\epsilon_t\}: \|\{\epsilon_t\}\|_w < r \implies d_w(\mathcal{I}, \tilde{\mathcal{I}}) = 0 \right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Stratification Theorem]\label{thm:stratification}
The class of observable channels admits a stratification by rigidity degree:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{U} = \mathcal{U}_0 \sqcup \mathcal{U}_{(0,\infty)} \sqcup \mathcal{U}_\infty,
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item $\mathcal{U}_0$: channels with $\rho = 0$ (zero rigidity, fully deformable);
    \item $\mathcal{U}_{(0,\infty)}$: channels with $0 < \rho < \infty$ (partial rigidity);
    \item $\mathcal{U}_\infty$: channels with $\rho = \infty$ (CCR rigidity).
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The rigidity degree $\rho$ is well-defined for a specified intervention sector and a common metric by the extended-real supremum in its definition. The stratification follows from the trichotomy of $\rho$ values.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectral Analysis of Deformations}

\begin{definition}[Deformation Spectrum]
For a perturbation family $\{\epsilon_t\}$, define the deformation spectrum as the sequence:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma(\{\epsilon_t\}) := \left( \|\epsilon_0\|_{\text{op}}, \|\epsilon_1\|_{\text{op}}, \|\epsilon_2\|_{\text{op}}, \ldots \right).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Weighted Polynomial Decay Condition]
Let $w_n\le K n^p$ and $\|\epsilon_n\|_{\rm op}\le C n^{-\alpha}$ for $n\ge1$, with $K,C>0$, $p\ge0$, and a finite term at zero. If $\alpha>p+1$, then $\sum_n w_n\|\epsilon_n\|_{\rm op}<\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The tail is bounded by $KC\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-(\alpha-p)}$, a convergent series. This sufficient condition is sharp for the entire dominated family: equality choices at $\alpha=p+1$ give the harmonic series. Its dyadic blocks each contribute at least $1/2$. Faster decay in a particular family can still converge outside this sufficient range.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completeness Criteria}

\begin{definition}[Projective Completeness]
A projective system $(S_t, \pi_{t+1 \to t})$ is \textit{projectively complete} if for every Cauchy sequence $(x^{(n)})_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ in $(\mathcal{I}, d_w)$, the limit exists in $\mathcal{I}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Completeness Inheritance]\label{thm:completeness}
If the coordinate spaces are complete and the bonding maps continuous, every finite-distance component of $(\mathcal I,d_w)$ is complete. Under the full-product finiteness condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-history}, this gives completeness on all of $\mathcal I$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-history} to the component of any fixed history. Coordinate limits of a Cauchy sequence respect the closed compatibility equations, and the same uniform Cauchy bound yields weighted convergence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Categorical Universality}

\begin{proposition}[Universal Property of Identity]
The identity $\mathcal{I} = \varprojlim S_t$ satisfies the universal property: for any object $J$ with compatible maps $\phi_t: J \to S_t$, there exists a unique morphism $\psi: J \to \mathcal{I}$ making all diagrams commute.
\end{proposition}

This confirms that rigid identity is not merely a construction but the \textit{terminal object} in the category of compatible families over the projective system \cite{maclane1998}.

\subsection{Relation to Causal Inference}

\begin{proposition}[Invariance in the Declared Finite-Cost Sector]
Suppose a structural model associates to each intervention an identity object in a common metric comparison space and a cost in the sector of Definition~\ref{def:ontmass}. If $M_\Omega=+\infty$, each intervention in that sector of finite cost has zero Hausdorff deformation. If the compared objects are closed, they are equal as subsets of the comparison space.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A positive deformation at finite cost would contribute a finite ratio to the infimum. Zero Hausdorff distance between nonempty closed sets implies equality. Membership in the declared sector and finite cost must be verified for each intervention used in this conclusion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Computational Complexity Bounds}

\begin{theorem}[Complexity Lower Bound for CCR Simulation]
Suppose a deterministic code of $B$ bits must recover any requested prefix of a binary history through horizon $t$. Exact prefix recovery requires $B\geq t$. For complete decoded histories in the prefix metric $d(x,y)=2^{-m}$ at first differing position $m\geq1$, the optimal uniform error is $2^{-(B+1)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All $2^t$ prefixes occur, so distinct prefixes must have distinct codes. For the error bound use $2^{B+1}$ histories with distinct $(B+1)$-bit prefixes. A shared code cannot decode to the correct $(B+1)$-bit prefix of both, and failure on this prefix gives error at least $2^{-(B+1)}$. Keeping $B$ bits and padding with zeros attains that error.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Impossibility of Perfect Simulation]
A fixed finite binary budget cannot recover every prefix on the binary-prefix target.
\end{corollary}

By contrast, the exact scalar $\sum_{j\geq1}2b_j3^{-j}$ distinguishes all histories, because its leading difference exceeds the remaining tail. On its image the corresponding inverse decoder recovers each finite observation. This model separates numerical precision from real dimension.
