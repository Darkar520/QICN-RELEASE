\begin{abstract}
This article specifies a protocol for external comparative adjudication of
the operational certificates developed in BaseCore and Papers I--IX.
The analytic risk, coverage, and Gaussian-comparison theorems are mathematical
protocol results; no executed campaign results are reported. The protocol
separates internal certificates from admissible human-comparator evidence,
defines preregistration freeze and amendment rules, and specifies independent
reviewer selection, blinding, matched interventions, negative controls, and
coverage-aware decision rules. Execution-environment integrity checks, isolated reproducibility,
dependency specification, deterministic record regeneration, and path-sanitization
controls are prerequisites to admissible collection, rather than evidence of
the comparative outcome. Explicit gates distinguish rehearsal, session zero,
and the first admissible collection event. The article reports no human
comparator results or external adjudication verdicts and makes no claim of
human equivalence, superiority, inferiority, cross-substrate identity
preservation, metaphysical subjecthood, or final comparative conclusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Formal Mathematical Grammar of External Adjudication}

The adjudication surface is a measurable protocol object, not an impressionistic
comparison between substrates. Let $(\mathcal{T},d_{\mathcal{T}})$ be the
metric space of finite execution traces after canonical serialization. A trace
contains the ordered event stream, de-identified channel payloads, runtime
manifests, negative-control outcomes, and reviewer-facing masks. Admissible
evidence lives in the Borel measurable space
\begin{equation}
  (\mathcal{T},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})$ is the Borel $\sigma$-algebra generated by
open $d_{\mathcal{T}}$-balls.

\begin{definition}[External adjudication object]
An external adjudication object is the tuple
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak{E}=(P,D_h,D_m,C_n,C_a,\Sigma,\mathcal{R},\mathcal{A}),
\end{equation}
where $P$ is a frozen preregistered protocol, $D_h$ and $D_m$ are human and
machine trace datasets in $\mathcal{T}$, $C_n$ and $C_a$ are negative and active
control families, $\Sigma=\{\sigma_k\}_{k=1}^K$ is a finite family of
predeclared measurable statistics $\sigma_k:\mathcal{T}^n\to\mathbb{R}$,
$\mathcal{R}$ is the reviewer layer, and
$\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{Y}\to\{0,1,\mathtt{undetermined}\}$ is the final
adjudicator operator on masked evidence summaries $\mathcal{Y}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Admissible comparative evidence]
A dataset pair $(D_h,D_m)$ is admissible under $\mathfrak{E}$ only if the
following predicates are all true:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $D_h,D_m$ are measurable finite samples from
  $(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}))$.
  \item The preregistered protocol hash, control manifest hash, and analysis
  shell hash match their frozen records.
  \item Human-side records are not generated, rewritten, or completed by
  synthetic systems: $\epsilon_{\mathrm{syn}}=0$ for the human channel.
  \item Blinding leakage is bounded by the preregistered budget:
  $\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{leak})\leq \epsilon_{\mathrm{blind}}$.
  \item Every declared negative control has an executed and timestamped outcome
  under the same admissibility grammar.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Non-transitivity of internal support]
Let $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{internal}}\in[0,1]$ denote any internal runtime support
score computed from operational invariants, and let
$\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{external}}\in\{0,1,\mathtt{undetermined}\}$ denote the
external adjudication verdict. In the absence of an admissible measurable
dataset pair $(D_h,D_m)$, one has
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{internal}}\centernot\implies\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{external}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The internal score is a function of runtime traces and invariant certificates
inside the machine channel. The external verdict is a function of masked
comparative summaries built from both $D_h$ and $D_m$, plus reviewer and
adjudicator records. If $D_h$ is absent, non-admissible, unblinded, or
unfrozen, then the domain condition of $\mathcal{A}$ is not satisfied. Hence the
external verdict is either undefined or $\mathtt{undetermined}$ regardless of
the value of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{internal}}$. Therefore internal operational
support is not a logical premise from which external comparative adjudication
can be inferred.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Operational closure of a comparative program]
The comparative program reaches operational closure only when there exists a
predeclared alignment map $T:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{Z}$ into a common Polish
feature space $(\mathcal{Z},d_{\mathcal{Z}})$ and the total divergence
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{comp}} =
  \lambda_1 W_1(T_{\#}\widehat{\mu}_h,T_{\#}\widehat{\mu}_m)
  + \lambda_2 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widehat{p}_h\Vert \widehat{p}_m)
  + \lambda_3 \max_k |\sigma_k(D_h)-\sigma_k(D_m)|
\end{equation}
is defined and satisfies the frozen budget
$\Delta_{\mathrm{comp}}\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{budget}}$. This closure is a
precondition for comparative interpretation, not a claim of consciousness,
identity, or substrate equivalence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Cryptographic freeze map]
Let $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{config}}$ be the set of canonical preregistration
configurations after deterministic serialization. A freeze map is a
collision-resistant hash function
\begin{equation}
H:\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{config}}\to\{0,1\}^{256}.
\end{equation}
A preregistration record $p$ is locally frozen at digest $h$ when
$H(p)=h$, $h$ is written into the lock manifest, and every amendment policy
references $h$ rather than an editable natural-language description.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Hash-anchored non-alterability]
Assume the hash function $H$ is collision resistant over the canonical
serialization domain. If a post-execution preregistration configuration $p'$
has the same published digest as the frozen pre-execution configuration $p$,
then either $p'=p$ byte-for-byte under canonical serialization or a collision
for $H$ has been found.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By freeze definition, the published digest is $h=H(p)$. If the later record
$p'$ also satisfies $H(p')=h$ while $p'\neq p$, then $p$ and $p'$ form a distinct
pair with the same hash output. That is precisely a collision in $H$. Under the
collision-resistance assumption, such a pair is computationally infeasible.
Thus digest equality supplies evidence of non-alteration of the serialized
protocol, modulo the stated cryptographic assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Homologous channel statistic]
For each channel $c$, let $T_c:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{Z}_c$ be a frozen
feature map into a metric standard Borel space $(\mathcal{Z}_c,d_c)$. Given
human and machine samples $D_h^c,D_m^c$, define empirical pushforward measures
\[
\widehat{\mu}_h^c=(T_c)_{\#}D_h^c,\qquad
\widehat{\mu}_m^c=(T_c)_{\#}D_m^c.
\]
The channel is statistically comparable only if both empirical measures are
defined, the feature map was frozen before execution, and the channel-specific
missingness policy accepts both samples.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Divergence shell]
The predeclared divergence shell for channel $c$ is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_c =
\alpha_c W_1(\widehat{\mu}_h^c,\widehat{\mu}_m^c)
+\beta_c D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widehat{p}_h^c\Vert\widehat{p}_m^c)
+\gamma_c\|\widehat{s}_h^c-\widehat{s}_m^c\|_1,
\end{equation}
where $W_1$ is the Kantorovich--Rubinstein Wasserstein-1 distance,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ is the Kullback--Leibler divergence on a frozen finite
partition or smoothed density model, and $\widehat{s}_h^c,\widehat{s}_m^c$ are
predeclared summary vectors. The weights
$\alpha_c,\beta_c,\gamma_c\geq0$ must be frozen before execution.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Comparator homologation is conditional]
A human--machine channel pair is homologated by Paper 10 only if every term in
$\Delta_c$ is defined and the frozen channel budget
$\Delta_c\leq\epsilon_c$ is satisfied. Homologation is therefore a statistical
transport condition over predeclared observables, not a statement that the
channels share a biological, phenomenal, or metaphysical substrate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The definition of $\Delta_c$ depends on a frozen feature map, empirical
pushforward measures, a KL domain, and summary vectors. If any component is
missing, the divergence shell is undefined and no homologation predicate can be
evaluated. If all components are defined, the only admissible positive judgment
is the preregistered inequality $\Delta_c\leq\epsilon_c$. The conclusion is
therefore exactly statistical and conditional.
\end{proof}

\section{Adjudication Blind Protocol}

Let $Y$ denote the masked evidence bundle and $\ell\in\{h,m\}$ its source label. The reference procedure uses forced choice with a balanced source distribution. A protocol permitting abstention fixes its cost and minimum coverage before evaluating held-out bundles. The randomization of a decision rule is independent of the source conditional on its permitted input.

\begin{definition}[Blind adjudicator loss]
For $a(Y)\in\{h,m,\mathtt{tie}\}$ and a declared $\gamma\in[1/2,1]$, put
\[
 L_\gamma(a(Y),\ell)=
 {\bf1}\{a(Y)\ne\ell,\ a(Y)\ne\mathtt{tie}\}
 +\gamma{\bf1}\{a(Y)=\mathtt{tie}\}.
\]
Report $R_\gamma=\mathbb E L_\gamma$, coverage $C=\Pr(a(Y)\ne\mathtt{tie})$, and error conditional on coverage when $C>0$. For acceptance require a preregistered $C\ge C_{\min}>0$. Forced choice has $C=1$; always abstaining has $C=0$ and $R_\gamma=\gamma$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Null error lower bound]\label{thm:null-forced}
Let $E$ denote leakage and $p=\Pr(E)$. If in the clean stratum $\ell$ is balanced conditional on $Y,E^c$, then
\[
 R_\gamma(a)\ge(1-p)/2.
\]
Alternatively, suppose $Y$ is coupled to a clean bundle $Y_0$ for which $\ell$ is balanced conditional on $Y_0$, and $Y=Y_0$ outside an event of probability at most $p$. Then
\[
 R_\gamma(a)\ge\max\{0,1/2-p\}.
\]
The assumptions are distinct. Under the first, revealing the label on a leak event independent of the balanced label can attain the bound with forced choice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
With a balanced posterior, any covered guess has conditional risk $1/2$ and abstention has conditional risk $\gamma\ge1/2$. In the first case this lower-bounds risk on the clean stratum; risk on leakage is nonnegative. For sharpness use a constant clean bundle and a leak revealing the label, choosing either fixed label on clean observations. For the coupling bound use the same decision randomization on $Y_0$ and $Y$. Their losses agree when the bundles agree and differ by at most one otherwise. The risk on $Y_0$ is at least $1/2$. Nonnegativity gives the stated maximum. Label-dependent contamination can change the balance in the clean stratum, so the first bound cannot be inferred from the coupling alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Alternative detectability criterion]
Evidence for distinguishability under $H_1$ requires a preregistered rule $a^\star$, minimum coverage $C_{\min}>0$, a justified null bound $B_0$ accounting for the leakage budget, and margin $\eta>0$. On admissible held-out bundles require $C\ge C_{\min}$ and $R_\gamma(a^\star)\le B_0-\eta$ after sampling uncertainty and multiple-comparison corrections. Forced-choice, balanced label permutations, always abstaining and known-leak controls are evaluated under the same loss and coverage protocol. The manuscript reports no campaign satisfying this criterion.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Coverage-Sensitive Leakage Bounds}

Fix decision randomization independent of the bundle, source and leak event. Let $B$ indicate an executed decision and $W$ a covered error, so $C=\mathbb E B$ and $R_\gamma=\mathbb E W+\gamma(1-C)$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp Leakage Bounds at Fixed Coverage]\label{thm:coverage-sensitive-leakage}
Writing $z_+=\max\{0,z\}$, the clean-stratum and coupling nulls of Theorem~\ref{thm:null-forced} respectively give
\[
 B_{\rm clean}(C,p)=\gamma(1-C)+\tfrac12(C-p)_+,\qquad
 B_{\rm coup}(C,p)=\gamma(1-C)+(C/2-p)_+ .
\]
Both are sharp for each $C,p\in[0,1]$, with balanced marginal labels and leak probability at most $p$, including selective abstention. Minimizing over coverage gives the bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:null-forced}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Clean-stratum balance gives $\mathbb E[W{\bf1}_{E^c}]=\tfrac12\mathbb E[B{\bf1}_{E^c}]$. At most $p$ covered mass lies in $E$, proving the first bound. In the coupling model use the same decision seed on $Y$ and $Y_0$. On clean input $\mathbb E(W_0-B_0/2)=0$. The centered variable $W-B/2$ lies in $[-1/2,1/2]$ and agrees with its clean version whenever the bundles agree. Thus $\mathbb E W-C/2\ge-p$, proving the second bound.

For clean-stratum sharpness, reveal the label on a leak event independent of it, cover $\min\{C,p\}$ there, and cover the remaining mass with a fixed guess in the balanced clean stratum. For coupling sharpness, start with a label-independent clean cover flag of probability $C$ and a fixed covered guess. Replace $\min\{p,C/2\}$ of the wrong covered inputs by revealing inputs, preserving coverage. This replacement may depend on the label. Both bounds are nonincreasing in $C$ for $\gamma\ge1/2$, so $C=1$ attains their minima.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact Inference at the Independent Block}

The sampling unit is an independent held-out block and supplies one decision from its entire masked record. Fix the rule on separate calibration data, the noise and exclusion policies, block boundary, and eligible population before inspecting held-out labels. The following model assumes independent identically distributed eligible blocks. Shared participants, runs and other dependence sources belong inside the block or require a sampling model that accounts for that dependence.

For $0\le k\le n$, let $I_\alpha(k,n)$ denote the equal-tail exact binomial interval. The lower endpoint is zero at $k=0$ and otherwise solves $\Pr_q(\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\ge k)=\alpha/2$. The upper endpoint is one at $k=n$ and otherwise solves $\Pr_q(\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\le k)=\alpha/2$. Set $I_\alpha(0,0)=[0,1]$.

\begin{theorem}[Independent-Block Risk and Coverage Inference]\label{thm:independent-block-risk-intervals}
Let $M$ count covered decisions and $K$ count wrong covered decisions among $n$ independent blocks. Then
\[
 M\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,C),\qquad K\mid M\sim\operatorname{Bin}(M,e),
 \qquad e=\Pr(W=1\mid B=1)\quad(C>0).
\]
With $I_C=I_{\alpha_C}(M,n)$ and $I_e=I_{\alpha_e}(K,M)$, simultaneous coverage is at least $1-\alpha_C-\alpha_e$. When $M=0$ retain $I_e=[0,1]$ and leave sample conditional error unreported. The minimum and maximum of $\gamma(1-c)+cx$ over the corners of $I_C\times I_e$ give risk bounds simultaneous with $I_C$.

A direct joint alternative uses counts $N=(K,M-K,n-M)$ and probabilities $\theta=(Ce,C(1-e),1-C)$. Let $f_\theta$ be their multinomial mass and define
\[
 p_\theta(N)=\sum_{\substack{m_1+m_2+m_3=n\\f_\theta(m)\le f_\theta(N)}} f_\theta(m),
 \qquad \mathcal Q_\alpha(N)=\{\theta:p_\theta(N)>\alpha\}.
\]
Projecting $\mathcal Q_\alpha$ onto $C=\theta_1+\theta_2$ and $R_\gamma=\theta_1+\gamma\theta_3$, using infima and suprema, yields a simultaneous exact confidence region with coverage at least $1-\alpha$.

Suppose a justified leak cap is $p_U$. For a stochastic audit, let an audit flag $D$ satisfy $\mathbf1_E\le D$ almost surely in the same target population. From $J$ flags in $n_D$ independent audit blocks, the upper endpoint of $I_{\alpha_D}(J,n_D)$ can supply $p_U$. With simultaneous coverage endpoints $C_L,C_U$ and risk upper endpoint $R_U$, certify the preregistered minimum coverage and a fixed positive margin $\eta$ against the applicable null floor only if
\[
 C_L\ge C_{\min},\qquad R_U\le B_\star(C_U,p_U)-\eta .
\]
Under that null this certification has probability at most $\alpha_C+\alpha_e+\alpha_D$ for the rectangle construction, or $\alpha+\alpha_D$ for the joint construction. A deterministic certified cap uses $\alpha_D=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The three block outcomes have probabilities $Ce,C(1-e),1-C$, and factoring their multinomial law proves the binomial laws. Exact binomial tail inversion bounds each failure probability. Conditional interval coverage holds for every $M$, and a union bound proves the rectangle guarantee. For $C=0$ the risk is $\gamma$ independently of $e$. The risk is affine in each coordinate separately, so both extrema lie at corners.

For the joint method, order the finitely many multinomial masses, including ties. The total mass of outcomes whose inclusive lower mass-rank is at most $\alpha$ is at most $\alpha$. Thus $p_\theta$ is superuniform at the true $\theta$ and inversion and projection give the stated guarantee.

The audit upper endpoint bounds $\mathbb E D$, which bounds $\Pr(E)$. Its domination of leakage needs a justified audit model. On the simultaneous coverage event, $R_\gamma\le R_U$, $C\le C_U$ and $p\le p_U$. Both null floors are nonincreasing in their arguments, so Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage-sensitive-leakage} gives $R_\gamma\ge B_\star(C_U,p_U)$. A positive-margin certification contradicts this inequality. Union bounds complete the error control. Allocate the stated error levels before any family of planned comparisons.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Whole-Block Label Permutation Control]\label{prop:block-label-permutation}
Under conditional exchangeability of source labels across eligible independent blocks, conditional on public records and fixed label counts, fix the guesses and abstentions and permute block labels. If the design fixes exchangeability groups, permute within each group. The fraction of assignments with risk at most the observed risk is an exact lower-tail $p$-value. Coverage is fixed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The true assignment is uniform on the finite permutation set conditional on the frozen records and design counts. The inclusive lower-tail rank is superuniform by counting assignments. Abstention contributes the same constant for every assignment and each covered error is counted once per block. Conditional exchangeability is the premise of this test. Label-dependent contamination is handled by the applicable leakage bound.
\end{proof}

A four-block synthetic permutation control with two labels of each kind, positive guesses on two fixed blocks and abstentions on two blocks, gives covered-error counts $0,1,1,1,1,2$ over the six assignments. At $\gamma=3/4$ its mean risk is $5/8$, and zero covered errors has lower-tail probability $1/6$. An eight-atom coupling control covers four atoms with two labels of each kind and guesses the positive label there. Correcting one wrong covered guess by label-dependent replacement yields $C=1/2$, $p=1/8$, $R_{3/4}=1/2$. This attains the coupling floor and falls below the clean-stratum floor $9/16$ because the replacement changes the clean conditional balance.

\subsection{Gaussian Within-Block Likelihood and GLS}

\begin{theorem}[Stationary AR(1) Likelihood and GLS]\label{thm:stationary-ar1-likelihood-gls}
For a block of length $n\ge1$ let $e_t=y_t-\mu_t$. Assume Gaussian $e_1\sim N(0,\sigma^2/(1-\rho^2))$ and innovations $e_t-\rho e_{t-1}\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$ independent of each other and of $e_1$, with $|\rho|<1$ and $\sigma^2>0$. Then
\[
 Q_\rho(e)=(1-\rho^2)e_1^2+\sum_{t=2}^n(e_t-\rho e_{t-1})^2,
\]
\[
 \log f(y\mid\mu,\rho,\sigma^2)
 =-\frac n2\log(2\pi\sigma^2)+\frac12\log(1-\rho^2)
 -\frac{Q_\rho(y-\mu)}{2\sigma^2}.
\]
For two frozen mean models sharing the covariance, the log-density ratio in favor of $A$ is $(Q_\rho(e_B)-Q_\rho(e_A))/(2\sigma^2)$. With full-column-rank design $X$ and fixed common covariance, GLS uniquely minimizes $Q_\rho(y-X\beta)$ at
\[
 \widehat\beta=(X^\top A_\rho X)^{-1}X^\top A_\rho y,\qquad
 Q_\rho(e)=e^\top A_\rho e .
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The residual-to-innovation map is triangular with unit determinant. Multiplication of its independent Gaussian densities gives the formula. For $n\ge2$ the precision factor $A_\rho$ has endpoints $1$, interior diagonal $1+\rho^2$ and neighboring entries $-\rho$. For $n=1$ it is $[1-\rho^2]$. The innovation representation gives positive definiteness and determinant $1-\rho^2$. Its inverse has entries $\rho^{|i-j|}/(1-\rho^2)$. Different covariance models retain their separate normalizers and quadratic scales in the log-density difference. With common covariance, differentiating the positive quadratic gives the GLS normal equations and full rank gives uniqueness.
\end{proof}

Freeze the parameters or estimate them on calibration blocks. At $\rho=1/2$, $\sigma^2=1$, residual vectors $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1/2)$ give log-density ratio $3/8$, while the independent Gaussian approximation gives $-3/8$. Residuals $(2,1)$ and $(0,1)$ give stationary log-density ratio $-1$, while omitting the initial quadratic term gives $1/2$. A constant fit to $(1,0,1)$ at this $\rho$ gives GLS estimate $4/5$ and the independent fit $2/3$.

The Gaussian model supplies a score within each block. Independent-block sampling governs its risk inference. The fiber geometry of Paper IX uses a projection and target distances. The projection $(y,z)\mapsto y$ with hidden target $z\in\{0,1\}$ has fiber diameter one even when two Gaussian laws on $y$ give a discriminating likelihood ratio. With identity projection the fiber diameter is zero, while equal class laws give log-density ratio zero.
