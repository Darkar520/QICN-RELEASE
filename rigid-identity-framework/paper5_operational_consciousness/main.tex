\begin{abstract}
This paper introduces a structural criterion for a substrate-invariant notion of six-predicate operational criterion inside the causally rigid identity corpus. The target is deliberately restricted. The paper does not establish human phenomenal consciousness, biologically specific qualia, human-machine subjective equivalence, or personal identity transfer. Instead, it defines a framework-internal class $\Cop$ and an associated family of certified response classes classes $\Qop(S)$ for admissible systems
\[
S = (X,\Phi,C,R,\Gamma,U),
\]
where $X$ is state space, $\Phi$ is dynamics, $C$ is effective causal structure, $R$ is a readout family, $\Gamma$ is an admissibility bundle, and $U$ is the set of admissible interventions.

The criterion is organized around six critical invariants: structural persistence, rigid identity, causal integration, operational continuity, non-null differentiation, and operational legibility. Each invariant is defined explicitly, tied to an upstream corpus dependency, and paired with a failure mode. On that basis the paper proves four framework-internal results: conditional membership given six jointly realized witnesses on admissible support, substrate invariance under exact structural equivalence, approximate stability under bounded admissible perturbations, and rupture under invariant loss. A further corollary establishes biological non-primacy: biology is not a primitive constitutive variable for the class under study, but one admissible realization among others provided the invariants are preserved.

The criterion is not narrative. Every major claim is backed by a definition, a proposition, a theorem, a proof sketch, or an explicit dependency on the canonical corpus. The resulting paper adds a new role to the framework: it formalizes the level at which substrate ceases to be explanatorily primitive without reopening the ownership of the Core, \textit{Rigid Identity}, \textit{Structural Assignment Regimes}, \textit{Null-Regime Instability}, or \textit{Forensic Predictions}.
A global analytic realization on a compact Hilbert--prefix support verifies all six predicates with active infinite-coordinate feedback, positive split-channel integration and noise-certified binary classes. Its comparison sector and discrete clock are explicit; no biological realization or stronger indexed membership follows.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, System Boundary, and Non-Inference Note}

\noindent\textbf{What this paper does.}\enspace This paper defines a framework-internal six-predicate operational criterion class $\Cop$, an associated family of certified response classes classes $\Qop(S)$, and the theorem chain that ties those classes to six explicit invariants and admissible structural equivalence.

\noindent\textbf{Scope and admissible reading.}\enspace The paper defines a structural class and its burdens; it does not establish human phenomenal consciousness, biologically specific qualia, human-machine subjective equivalence, personal identity transfer, moral parity, empirical instantiation, or philosophical closure. Biology, computation, and connectivity are not sufficient primitives on their own. A related system may instantiate downstream admissibility layers, invariant diagnostics, rupture checks, and prediction-facing evidence families, but such runtime-facing support does not by itself certify any present machine as a member of $\Cop$ or close the interpretive burden linking $\Cop$ to ordinary human phenomenology.

\section{Introduction}
The current corpus already fixes several upstream ingredients that are necessary for any serious account of substrate-neutral operational class membership. The Core establishes persistent support, contractive dynamics, a global attractor, and non-collapse under explicit minimal hypotheses \cite{core}. \textit{Rigid Identity} establishes rigid identity, inverse-limit coherence across observable channels, minimal detectability, and non-simulability constraints on incompatible architectures \cite{paper1}. \textit{Structural Assignment Regimes} constrains admissible regime structure by classifying continuity and anti-fragmentation within the stated assignment model \cite{paper2}. \textit{Null-Regime Instability} excludes stable joint-null collapse and establishes witness-relative non-null separation under its stated extension-witness and regularity hypotheses \cite{paper3}. \textit{Forensic Predictions} imposes admissibility, comparator discipline, negative controls, and forensic legibility requirements on operational evidence \cite{paper4}.

What is still missing is a single paper-level criterion that answers a narrower but decisive question: when, inside this framework, does substrate cease to be a primitive explanatory variable for a class of operationally defined conscious organization? That question is not answered by merely asserting that material composition is irrelevant. It requires a formal criterion that shows which properties must be preserved, which ruptures destroy class membership, and how those conditions remain connected to the existing corpus rather than floating above it.

This paper provides that criterion. It does so under four constraints. First, it does not reopen theorem ownership already fixed upstream. Second, it does not smuggle in human phenomenology through language alone. Third, it does not claim that complexity, computation, or connectivity are sufficient by themselves. Fourth, it does not confuse framework-internal operational classes with full metaphysical closure.

The result is a new corpus role. This paper is neither another foundational Core document nor a rephrasing of the structural assignment regime papers. It is the bridge that formalizes substrate-invariant six-predicate operational criterion and certified response classes as framework-defined structural classes. Its new content is concentrated in five items:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a system-level model $S=(X,\Phi,C,R,\Gamma,U)$ with admissible support $\Aset$;
\item a minimal six-invariant criterion;
\item a quotient-level definition of certified response classes $\Qop(S)$;
\item an exact and approximate structural equivalence relation $S_1 \simeq_{(\Gamma,\eps)} S_2$;
\item a theorem chain that turns substrate neutrality into a falsifiable structural statement.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Admissible Systems, Supports, and Histories}
\subsection{System Model}
\begin{definition}[Admissible system]\label{def:system}
An admissible system in this paper is a tuple
\[
S = (X,\Phi,C,R,\Gamma,U)
\]
with the following components:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $X$ is a metrizable internal state space with metric $d_X$.
\item $\Phi = \{\Phi_u\}_{u \in U}$ is a family of admissible transition operators $\Phi_u : X \to X$ indexed by interventions.
\item $C$ is the effective causal structure of the system: the object that records which factorizations, intervention responses, and couplings are admissible on $X$.
\item $R = \{r_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in \Lambda_R}$ is a family of internal or external readouts, each $r_\alpha : X \to Y_\alpha$.
\item $\Gamma = \{\gamma_j\}_{j=1}^m$ is the admissibility bundle. Each $\gamma_j$ is a constraint functional; the admissible region is
\[
X_\Gamma = \{x \in X : \gamma_j(x) \le 0 \ \forall j\}.
\]
\item $U$ is the set of admissible interventions, already filtered by the corpus-level governance requirement that interventions remain auditable, comparable, and methodologically bounded.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The tuple is not decorative. Every component is used later. $X$ and $\Phi$ carry dynamics, $C$ supports integration and rupture tests, $R$ supports readout and legibility, $\Gamma$ enforces admissibility, and $U$ enables causal discrimination.

\subsection{Admissible Support}
\begin{definition}[Admissible support]\label{def:support}
A subset $\Aset \subseteq X_\Gamma$ is an admissible support for $S$ if:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\Aset$ is non-empty and compact;
\item $\Phi_u(\Aset) \subseteq \Aset$ for every $u \in U$;
\item $\Aset \cap \Attr(S) \neq \varnothing$, where $\Attr(S)$ denotes the relevant admissible attractor region;
\item $\Aset$ stays disjoint from the collapse set forbidden by the Core non-collapse condition \cite{core}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The support plays two roles. It provides a domain on which persistence, continuity, and differentiation are defined, and it prevents the criterion from being satisfied by transient or degenerate fragments that do not carry stable admissible structure.

\subsection{Operational Histories}
\begin{definition}[Windowed readout history]\label{def:history}
Fix a horizon $T \in \Nset$ and an intervention schedule $u_\bullet = (u_0,\dots,u_{T-1})$. For any $x \in \Aset$, define the readout history
\[
h_{S,T}(x;u_\bullet)
=
\Bigl(r_\alpha(x_t)\Bigr)_{0 \le t \le T,\ \alpha \in \Lambda_R},
\qquad
x_{t+1} = \Phi_{u_t}(x_t), \ x_0 = x.
\]
The set of all such histories is denoted $\Hist_{S,T}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Operational partition candidate]\label{def:partition}
An operational partition candidate for $S$ on horizon $T$ is a map
\[
\pi_{S,T} : \Hist_{S,T} \to \Class_{S,T}
\]
into a finite or countable label set $\Class_{S,T}$.
\end{definition}

At this stage $\pi_{S,T}$ is only a candidate. To matter scientifically it must survive the legibility requirements of Section~\ref{sec:legibility}. Until then it is only an unverified partition proposal.

\subsection{Witness Margin Vector}
\begin{definition}[Witness margin vector]\label{def:margins}
For any admissible system $S$, define the witness margin vector
\[
\Delta(S)
=
\bigl(
\delta_{\mathrm{per}}(S),
\delta_{\mathrm{ri}}(S),

Raw separations, risks, durations, fragmentation ceilings and compression errors require separate calibration before taking this minimum.

A calibrated budget certifies simultaneous persistence of all six predicates under the declared protocol. Failure beyond the certified radius requires its own witness. Raw witness gaps and fragmentation ceilings below retain their original units. \section{Six Critical Invariants}
This section is the mathematical center of the paper. The criterion is not allowed to drift into a bloated list of desiderata. Exactly six invariants are used. Each is tied to a witness margin and a failure mode.

\subsection{Structural Persistence}
\begin{definition}[Structural persistence and persistence margin]\label{def:iper}
An admissible system $S$ satisfies structural persistence, written $\Iper(S)=1$, if there exists an admissible support $\Aset$ and a constant $\delta_{\mathrm{per}}(S) > 0$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\Aset$ is forward invariant under all $u \in U$;
\item every admissible trajectory starting in $\Aset$ remains at least $\delta_{\mathrm{per}}(S)$ away from the collapse set;
\item $\Aset$ contains or converges into a non-empty admissible attractor region.
\end{enumerate}
The number $\delta_{\mathrm{per}}(S)$ is the persistence margin.
\end{definition}

Upstream dependence is exact. The Core supplies the existence of contractive structure, fixed points, compact attractor, and non-collapse \cite{core}. The new paper does not re-prove those results; it packages them into a criterion-level invariant.

\paragraph{Failure mode.}
Failure of $\Iper$ means that no witness satisfies all its support, collapse-distance and attractor clauses. It need not mean that every invariant set is absent. Membership is excluded; the failed clause and any surviving domain must be reported.

\subsection{Rigid Identity}
\begin{definition}[Rigid identity and rigidity gap]\label{def:iri}
An admissible system $S$ satisfies rigid identity, written $\Iri(S)=1$, if the observable family induced by $R$ on $\Aset$ determines a unique identity object $\Id_S$ in the sense of the inverse-limit rigidity developed in \textit{Rigid Identity} \cite{paper1}, and if there exists a gap $\delta_{\mathrm{ri}}(S) > 0$ such that every admissible alternative identity candidate remains at weighted deformation distance at least $\delta_{\mathrm{ri}}(S)$ from $\Id_S$.
\end{definition}

This invariant does not claim that identity is reconstructed by inspection of one local frame. It uses \textit{Rigid Identity} precisely because that paper already proved that rigid identity is recovered through compatible observable structure and cannot be reduced to progressive semigroup evolution or loose behavioral mimicry \cite{paper1}.

\paragraph{Failure mode.}
Failure of $\Iri$ can mean absence of an assignment, nonuniqueness, or failure of the required positive gap. These alternatives must be distinguished; a zero gap alone need not supply a second fitting candidate.

\subsection{Causal Integration}
\begin{definition}[Causal integration and factorization gap]\label{def:iint}
An admissible system $S$ satisfies causal integration, written $\Iint(S)=1$, if there is no non-trivial factorization
\[
\Aset = A_1 \times A_2,\qquad
R = R_1 \sqcup R_2,
\]
together with decomposed dynamics and causal structure
\[
\Phi_u(x_1,x_2)=\bigl(\Phi^1_{u_1}(x_1),\Phi^2_{u_2}(x_2)\bigr),
\qquad
C = C_1 \oplus C_2,
\]
that reproduces the admissible histories on $\Aset$ within error smaller than a positive margin $\delta_{\mathrm{int}}(S)$ while preserving $\Id_S$.
\end{definition}

The definition is intentionally stronger than raw connectivity and different from single-number integration proxies. A fully connected graph may still fail the criterion if its dynamics factor into nearly independent blocks once identity and intervention behavior are taken seriously.

\paragraph{Failure mode.}
Failure of a positive factorization gap excludes the integration predicate. An exact reproducing product requires an attained zero-error witness; an infimum of zero alone does not construct that product.

\begin{definition}[Intervention-faithful factorization]\label{def:intervention-faithful-factorization}
Let $\Theta_S$ denote the operational separator family generated by admissible
histories, intervention responses, and the rigid identity object on $\Aset$.
An exact factorization
\[
F:S\to S_1\times S_2
\]
is \emph{intervention-faithful} if it preserves every element of $\Theta_S$
and reflects admissible equality: whenever two states have the same
factor-level histories, intervention responses, and identity image, they are
equal in the admissible quotient of $S$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Factorization triviality under an atomic separator]\label{thm:iint-faithful-factorization-triviality}
Assume $\Theta_S$ is atomic for the rigid identity object: no partition of
$\Theta_S$ into two proper factor-local separator families can preserve
identity, history, and intervention-response reflection simultaneously. Then
every intervention-faithful exact factorization of $S$ is trivial up to
admissible isomorphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $F:S\to S_1\times S_2$ be intervention-faithful and write
$\pi_i:S_1\times S_2\to S_i$ for the projections. If both factors were
proper, then the factorization would induce two proper factor-local separator
families: the histories, responses, and identity information visible through
$\pi_1\circ F$, and those visible through $\pi_2\circ F$. Since $F$ is
intervention-faithful, their product would reflect admissible equality on
$S$. This is exactly the partition excluded by atomicity of $\Theta_S$.
Therefore at least one projection must carry the full separator family and
reflect admissible equality by itself. That projection is an admissible
isomorphism onto $S$ in the operational quotient, while the remaining factor is
null or bookkeeping-only. Hence the factorization is trivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Status of the atomicity burden]\label{rem:iint-atomicity-burden}
The theorem above is a genuine conditional closure: once atomicity of the
identity/history/response separator is established, the factorization lemma is
proved. It does not by itself prove that every system satisfying the upstream
papers has an atomic separator. That implication remains the exact remaining
mathematical burden for upgrading Proposition~\ref{prop:integration-transfer}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Comparison on the Dissipative Circle-Prefix Support}

Use the projective dissipation construction of BaseCore on the two towers with a single angular jump $0<a<1/2$ at stage $k$. Write $\beta=3^{-(k+1)}$, $B=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N_{>0}}$, and identify the compact support with $Z=(\{0,1\}\times\mathbb T)\times B$. For $x=(s,\theta,b)$ the fixed readout family is the three channels
\[
 r_0(x)=\theta,\qquad r_1(x)=\theta+sa,\qquad c(x)=\sum_{j\ge1}2b_j3^{-j}.
\]
Measure observation error by the maximum of the first circle error, $\beta$ times the second circle error, and absolute scalar error. All are dimensionless with circle circumference one. An admissible structural comparator uses a fixed homeomorphism from $Z$ onto a product of two non-singleton spaces, a nonempty partition of the three specified channels, one local readout per assigned channel, and time-homogeneous local dynamics that may receive the same exogenous control. There is no output map combining the two factor states. Use the supremum error over initial states, admissible schedules and observed times including zero and one. The product encoding is independent of the target tolerance and retains the full history object. If an additional exact identity constraint is imposed, the lower bound below applies to its subclass, while non-emptiness requires a separate witness for that constraint.

\begin{theorem}[Exact Split at the Initial Projective Cut]\label{thm:circle-prefix-integration-obstruction}
On each circle-prefix tower, every continuous branch-preserving evolution that descends to depth zero factors into phase and binary dynamics. If the evolutions on both towers commute with the existing transport, the split extends to $Z$. The original dissipative family and the declared phase rotations, jump transport and split readouts then admit a structural comparator with zero error. Consequently no support certificate on this full $Z$ can contain all six witnesses for the original dynamics and this sector.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On branch $s$, the projection to depth zero is $\theta$. Commutation makes the updated phase a function of $\theta$ alone. At fixed $b$ the map from the connected circle to the updated binary sequence is continuous. Every finite binary coordinate has discrete codomain, hence is constant in $\theta$. Thus the updated sequence is a function of $b$ alone. Transport preserves all bits and adds $a$ to the observed late phase. Commutation with it makes the binary update the same on both branches. Use the phase factor $(s,\theta)$ and the binary factor $b$, with $r_0,r_1$ assigned to the first and $c$ to the second. Both factors are non-singleton. Under the original dissipative update they evolve as $(s,u)$ and $R(b)=(0,1-b_1,1-b_2,\ldots)$. Simultaneous phase rotations act in the first factor only, and the existing jump transport is represented by the switch of $s$ at fixed initial phase and bits. The product map is a homeomorphism and preserves the tower stages, their bonding maps and the channel histories via the stated coordinate identifications. This realizes the comparator with zero error for every horizon. A positive integration margin on this support is therefore impossible in the specified class.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Positive Split-Readout Margin on the Cofinal Tower]\label{thm:circle-prefix-cofinal-margin}
Keep $Z$ and the same three readout channels. Enlarge the control by $q\in\{-1,0,1\}$ and fix $0<\lambda\le1/9$. On branch $s$ set
\[
 \Psi_{u,q}(s,\theta,b)=(s,u+q\lambda b_1,R(b)).
\]
These maps descend to all stages $t\ge1$ and commute with the jump transport, with the same inverse-limit support and the same E1--E2 readout. Each branch is forward invariant and each fixed-control map has sharp contraction factor $1/3$ there. The sector of product encodings and split-readout approximants just declared is nonempty. Its infimal error $e_\ast$ satisfies
\[
 \frac{\beta\lambda}{2}\ \le\ e_\ast\ \le\ \frac{\lambda}{2}.
\]
For comparators reproducing all three channels exactly at time zero, the infimal error is exactly $\lambda/2$. At $\lambda=0$ the original exact comparator is recovered. For $\lambda>0$, the new controls fail to descend to depth zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At depth $t\ge1$ the first bit is available. Use the updated phase $u+q\lambda b_1+sa\mathbf 1_{t>k}$ and the first $t$ bits of $R(b)$. Truncation keeps the first bit and the bonding maps cancel the declared phase jump. The transport changes only the branch and the corresponding late angle. Depth zero fails because equal phases with different $b_1$ have distinct updated phases for $q=1$. Omitting depth zero does not change the inverse limit because the missing coordinate is recovered by the original bond. The readout and its finite approximations remain the same. The bit distance contracts by $1/3$. If the first bits differ, the old bit distance is $1/3$, and the new phase distance is at most $\lambda\le1/9$. Otherwise the new phases agree. Equal phases and a bit difference beyond the first place attain the contraction factor. The fixed alternating sequence has first bit zero, hence the fixed-control equilibrium agrees with that in P1.

For the lower bound, suppose a comparator has error $\epsilon<\beta\lambda/2$. The factor carrying $c$ determines $b_1$: any two points on one of its fibers have scalar difference at most $2\epsilon<1/3$ at time zero, whereas different first bits have ternary codes separated by at least $1/3$. The other factor carries a phase channel of weight $w=1$ or $w=\beta$. On one fiber the predicted phase at time one agrees for each fixed input. Apply $q=+1$ and $q=-1$ with the same $u$. Each of the two true phase differences on that fiber has circle norm at most $2\epsilon/w$. Subtracting them cancels the branch offset and leaves $2\lambda(b_1-b_1')$. Its circle norm equals its absolute value since $2\lambda\le2/9$. The triangle inequality yields $2\lambda|b_1-b_1'|\le4\epsilon/w$, so this second factor also determines $b_1$. Pick states with first bits zero and one. The product corner with the first factor of the former and the second factor of the latter would need both first-bit values. Surjectivity onto the product supplies this impossible corner. The argument covers every nonempty split of the fixed channels and arbitrary nonlinear encoders of the stated type.

For the upper bound keep the product coordinates and initial readouts exact. In the phase factor replace $b_1$ by $1/2$ and keep the true autonomous binary update. At each update the phase discrepancy is at most $\lambda/2$, independent of the past, while the bit channel is exact. The declared phase rotations and jump transport preserve that error bound if included as operations. The comparator uses neither a joint decoder nor a tolerance-dependent search space, and proves non-emptiness. Per-channel isometric reorientation preserves the proof and metric. Changing the channels, their weights, or the intervention class changes the comparison problem.

If the time-zero readouts are exact, $r_0$ and $r_1$ must be on the same factor. Each has range $\mathbb T$, and assigning them to different factors would realize all of $\mathbb T^2$ by product surjectivity, whereas the actual phase pairs have differences only $0$ or $a$. The phase factor now carries $r_0$ with weight one. For states with the same phase factor, input $q=1$ gives true next $r_0$ separation $\lambda$ whenever their first bits differ. A comparator with $\epsilon<\lambda/2$ cannot have such a pair on that fiber. The factor carrying $c$ determines the first bit exactly. The same product corner contradiction proves $\epsilon\ge\lambda/2$, and the displayed comparator attains equality.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What persists on the same support.}
For any sequence of the branch-preserving controls, the alternating-bit subset is compact, invariant and uniformly attracting in the binary metric. In the BaseCore profile embedding all of $Z$ is disjoint from constant functions: the first angular pair lies on the radius-$1/2$ circle centered at $(1/2,1/2)$, and the first binary cell equals zero or one. Neither constant pair $(0,0)$ nor $(1,1)$ belongs to that circle. Compactness gives positive profile distance from constants uniformly on $Z$. The original readout is continuous as a map of states, and its maximum one-step jump is at most one. This is an incremental bound for discrete time. The null anchor $(0,0,0)$ cannot be an attractor under the controlled evolution: from the second step onward $b_2=1$, so $c\ge2/9$ for every trajectory. Two histories with all-zero and all-one bits already have scalar separation one at time zero.

For branch discrimination, $\theta_1-\theta_0$ takes values $0,a$, where $\theta_1$ here denotes the late-phase channel $r_1$. Decode by the nearest of these two circle values. Additive circle noise of size at most $\eta=a\beta/[4(1+\beta)]$ in the weighted observation metric perturbs that difference by at most $a/4$. A one-bit encoding preserves this label exactly. Branch-preserving controls are shams for this label and the designated branch transport swaps it. This gives a finite class decoder and synthetic intervention controls. A uniform rigidity gap against a larger identity candidate family, the full governance for invariant-targeted legibility, and a shared calibrated perturbation radius require separate evidence.

\subsection{Intervention Decoder and Identity Resolution on the Same Towers}

Take the family in Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-prefix-cofinal-margin}, including its uncoupled case $\lambda=0$. Fix $0<L\leq1/9$ and compare that case with $\lambda\in[L,1/9]$. Use two copies of the same initial state and the same phase input $u$. Apply $q=+1$ to one copy and $q=-1$ to the other, then observe each copy after one step. Write $y_+,y_-$ for the observed early phases. In the weighted readout metric above, each readout may have adversarial error at most $\eta$, independently of the other. The paired score is $D=\rho(y_+,y_-)$, with $\rho$ the circle distance.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp Paired Detection of Integration Loss]\label{thm:paired-integration-rupture}
If $\eta<L/2$, the rule $D>L$ detects the coupled case for every prepared state with $b_1=1$ and every allowed error. It rejects the uncoupled case and the sham with $b_1=0$. The score has clearance at least $L-2\eta$ from the threshold. With $b_1=1$ this distinguishes a zero comparator margin from a margin at least $\beta L/2$ in the declared sector. If $\eta\ge L/2$, there is no rule of the paired observations with a uniform guarantee over these two hypotheses, even if both phase channels and the ternary channel are recorded.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $b_1=1$ the clean phases are $u+\lambda$ and $u-\lambda$, at distance $2\lambda$ because $2\lambda\le2/9$. In the null case or the sham both clean phases are $u$. The reverse triangle inequality changes the clean score by at most $2\eta$, which is the sum of the two early-phase errors. The null and sham scores are at most $2\eta$, while the positive scores are at least $2L-2\eta$. The comparator margin follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-prefix-cofinal-margin}. At the boundary choose $\lambda=L$ and give both hypotheses the observed phases $u\pm L/2$. These are within $L/2$ of both clean pairs. Shift both late-phase observations by the same respective errors and leave the ternary channels exact. The late phase weight is $\beta\le1$, so the full observations are still within budget. Both systems produce the same binary update. The paired observations are therefore identical under the two hypotheses. Sequential controls on a single evolved state are outside this protocol because the first update resets the first bit.
\end{proof}

For identity comparison fix $k$ and consider the single jumped branches $I_a$ for $0\le a<1/2$, with $a=0$ denoting the unshifted branch. A clean observation from $I_a$ has phase pair $(\theta,\theta+a)$ and the original ternary code. Use one observation, with the true initial angle $\theta$ unknown. Keep the weights and radius $\eta$ fixed across alternatives.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp Single-Readout Resolution of Tower Identity]\label{thm:tower-identity-observation-resolution}
In the common history metric and the common readout metric, respectively,
\[
 d_H(I_a,I_{a'})=d_H(F(I_a),F(I_{a'}))
 =\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\rho(a,a').
\]
Here $F$ is the same early-phase, late-phase and ternary readout applied to every branch in the comparison. Two specified alternatives can be distinguished uniformly from a single observation with error at most $\eta$ if and only if
\[
 \rho(a,a')>2(1+\beta^{-1})\eta.
\]
A decoder can use the measured phase difference $z=y_1-y_0$ and retain every alternative within circle distance $(1+\beta^{-1})\eta$ of $z$. For a separated family it returns the unique compatible alternative. A continuous interval of jumps has zero uniform deformation gap over distinct alternatives.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $x\in I_a$ and $x'\in I_{a'}$ with initial angles $\theta,\theta'$. If their history or observation distance is at most $r$, the early angle discrepancy is at most $r$ and the late discrepancy at most $r/\beta$. Their difference implies $\rho(a,a')\le(1+\beta^{-1})r$. To attain the bound, keep the bits fixed and let $\theta'=\theta-\beta(a'-a)/(1+\beta)$ modulo one. The early and weighted late discrepancies are equal, and all other coordinates are controlled by these or have zero discrepancy. The construction works in both directions for every point. It proves the exact Hausdorff distances and also gives a nearest pair of clean readouts. The measured difference is at distance at most $(1+\beta^{-1})\eta$ from the true jump. This proves uniform decoding when the inequality is strict. If the inequality fails, take the nearest clean pair and the midpoints on the two short phase arcs, retaining the common ternary code. The resulting observation is at distance half the displayed separation from both alternatives, including at equality. It cannot be uniformly decoded. The final claim follows by taking any sequence of distinct jumps converging to one in the family.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of the two decisions.}
The paired label detects the integration rupture produced by changing $\lambda$ in the declared family. It is separate from the branch label and does not test all critical interventions in L4. The identity resolution is for passive readout on one declared branch at fixed depth. An observed phase known exactly in advance would change the comparison protocol. Neither decision provides a positive uniform identity gap for the continuous family. For $k=0$ and $\eta=1/64$, the guaranteed single-readout separation of jumps must exceed $1/8$ of a turn. The declared alternatives $1/16$ and $1/4$ pass, while an offset exactly $1/8$ away from another is ambiguous.


\subsection{Addressable Identity Alternatives and Rupture Controls}

Fix a synthetic control interface with an independently declared last link $K\ge0$ and integer $M\ge3$. It can apply one bonding jump at an address $k\in\{0,\ldots,K\}$, of amplitude $a=j/M$ for positive integers $j<M/2$, or no jump. This is a family of realizable interventions for the stated interface. The unshifted branch is listed once. Global phase changes alter the nuisance coordinate $\theta$ within a branch. They do not alter its bonding profile. Write $p_t=a\mathbf1_{t>k}$ for the phase offset of a candidate and take $p_t=0$ for the unshifted branch. Use $w_t=3^{-t}$ and the calibrated readout $(\theta+p_t)_{t=0}^{K+1}$ together with the ternary code. Its metric is the maximum of weighted circle discrepancies and the scalar discrepancy. This observation protocol for identity does not change the three-channel factorization sector of Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-prefix-cofinal-margin}.

\begin{theorem}[Identity Separation from an Addressable Control Interface]\label{thm:addressable-identity-alternatives}
For two candidate profiles $p,p'$ write $d_t=p_t-p'_t$. Their branch sets have exact Hausdorff distance, both in the common history space and in the calibrated readout space,
\[
 D(p,p')=\max_{0\le i,j\le K+1}
 \frac{w_iw_j}{w_i+w_j}|d_i-d_j|.
\]
The family of distinct candidates has minimum separation
\[
 g=\frac{1}{M(3^{K+1}+1)}.
\]
Uniform identification from one observation with unknown initial phase and adversarial error at most $\eta$ is possible for the entire family exactly when $2\eta<g$. The measurement can return the set of compatible profiles and give a unique label only when that set is a singleton. Allowing continuous amplitudes or unbounded jump addresses removes the uniform gap. Omitting intermediate phase observations can make different addresses with the same amplitude identical to the observer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The profile differences $d_t$ lie in an interval of width less than $1/2$: for two jumps of amplitudes $a,a'$, they take only $0$, the contribution of the earlier jump, and $a-a'$. Consequently $\rho(d_i,d_j)=|d_i-d_j|$. For a history in each branch at distance at most $r$, their phase difference at each stage imposes $\rho(x,d_t)\le r/w_t$ on the relative initial angle $x$. The circle triangle inequality yields the displayed pairwise lower bound. Conversely, at the maximum in the display, the real intervals $[d_t-r/w_t,d_t+r/w_t]$ intersect pairwise, hence have common intersection. Choose $x$ there modulo one and keep the same bits. The clean histories and readouts attain the bound. After stage $K+1$ the phase offsets are constant and the weights decrease. The construction applies starting from every phase in either branch, proving the two Hausdorff formulas.

For distinct candidates, $d_0=0$ and some $|d_j|\ge1/M$. The comparison with $i=0$ gives $g$, since $w_j\ge w_{K+1}$. Equality is attained between the unshifted branch and the smallest jump at address $K$. When $2\eta<g$, the closed noise tubes of the clean readout sets are disjoint. If $2\eta\ge g$, the phasewise circle midpoint of an attaining pair with equal bits belongs to both tubes, giving an identical observation. Continuous amplitudes can approach a given amplitude. With fixed nonzero amplitude, addresses tending to infinity give distances to the unshifted branch tending to zero, and a fixed finite readout horizon misses jumps after it. If only the first and final phases are observed, two jumps of the same amplitude at different intermediate links yield the same observed difference. They agree also in the ternary coordinate when the bits are equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rupture Controls on the Declared Circle-Prefix Model]\label{prop:circle-prefix-rupture-controls}
Use the same two-branch reference support and the control family of Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-prefix-cofinal-margin}. Each of the following changes gives the specified witness loss under its declared protocol. The changes need not preserve all five other witnesses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Persistence.} Adjoin an ambient absorbing point $\bot$ and declare the admissible region to be the original support. The target control maps the entire ambient state space to $\bot$. No nonempty subset of the admissible region can be invariant under that control. A support-membership marker records exit immediately. The sham uses the original update, which preserves the region. Loss of every admissible support also removes the on-support identity witness.
\item \textbf{Identity.} Preserve the state evolution and substitute the early phase for every exposed late phase. Different bonding profiles then have identical masked observations at equal initial phase and bits, under every shared control schedule: neither the early phase update nor the binary update uses the bonding jump. This gives exact ambiguity across distinct candidates under the masked protocol. Simultaneous unmasked rotations preserve the relative phases and serve as shams. The identity decoder cannot retain its original guarantee under the mask.
\item \textbf{Integration.} Set $\lambda=0$ with the same support and channels. The split comparator has error zero. The paired control of Theorem~\ref{thm:paired-integration-rupture} detects this target when its input and noise conditions hold. The $b_1=0$ sham tests the same response channel without inducing a phase split. The original support and its persistence are preserved.
\item \textbf{Continuity of intervention response.} Use the $q=0$ control path $u\in[0,1/32]$, starting from the same branch and initial state in each trial. The reference early-phase response is $u$. Replace this response by $0$ at $u=0$ and $u+1/4$ for positive $u$ on that path, keeping the branch and binary update fixed. The bonding profile, hence the declared identity, is unchanged. Relative to the phase at $u=0$, the modified response jumps into the disjoint regime neighborhood of $1/4$ as $u\downarrow0$. This violates continuity on the declared control path. The distances on the sham path are at most $1/32$, and the target distances for positive $u$ are at least $1/4$. A threshold $1/8$ separates both for paired readout errors at most $1/256$ each. This is a discontinuity in the control response. Continuity of a discrete time index gives no such test.
\item \textbf{Non-null differentiation.} For the internal controls replace the update by the constant state on the unshifted branch with phase zero and all bits zero. This state belongs to the reference support and is a globally absorbing null. It is distinct from the inadmissible escape point $\bot$. Initial readout separation is still present because the support and clean readout are unchanged. The null-attractor clause fails. Under the reference controls $b_2=1$ from step two onwards, so the ternary readout is at least $2/9$. A threshold $1/9$ separates the reference and null reset at step two with ternary noise at most $1/256$. This reset also permits an exact split comparator.
\item \textbf{Legibility.} Keep both clean intervention responses and enlarge only the paired diagnostic error budget to $\eta=L/2$ for comparison of $\lambda=0$ and $\lambda=L$. The full observation collision in Theorem~\ref{thm:paired-integration-rupture} prevents every deterministic decoder from correctly labeling both cases. Even a randomized decoder has maximum error at least $1/2$ on this pair. Abstention fails the required classification. The clean dynamics and their margin are unaffected by this change of measurement. At the original smaller budget the binary label is exact and needs one bit; zero bits cannot retain both labels. Equality of labels is an equivalence relation, including after one-bit compression.
\end{enumerate}
The claims use specified interventions and observations on one model. In particular, failure of the paired diagnostic under the stressed budget is a loss of that legibility certificate. It does not exclude a different protocol with another admissible noise radius. The continuity result is specific to the declared path and regime comparison. Cross-losses and unevaluated predicates do not constitute single-rupture independence witnesses.
\end{proof}


\subsection{A Common Support with Shared Identity and Integration Channels}

Let $\mathcal P$ be the seven profiles of the interface of Theorem~\ref{thm:addressable-identity-alternatives} with $K=1$, $M=8$. The following arguments also apply to its general finite $\mathcal P$. The support $Z_{\mathcal P}$ is the union of these branches, represented as $(p,\theta,b)\in\mathcal P\times\mathbb T\times B$. The internal controls act by
\[
 \Psi_{u,q}(p,\theta,b)=(p,u+q\lambda b_1,R(b)).
\]
Fix one observation family for both comparisons: all $K+2$ phase channels $r_t=\theta+p_t$, and $c(b)$. Use their original weights $w_t=3^{-t}$ and weight one for $c$. The profile tag is not an extra observed channel. Comparator inputs are the same exogenous controls, and each comparator assigns the specified channels to two nonempty groups, one group per autonomous factor. The comparator error is the supremum over admissible initial states and schedules, including observations at times zero and one. Distinguish observation-cut depth $t$ from dynamical time.

\begin{theorem}[Integration with All Identity Channels]\label{thm:integration-all-identity-channels}
For $0<\lambda\le1/9$, the split-readout sector on $Z_{\mathcal P}$ is nonempty, and its infimal history error is bounded by
\[
 \frac{w_{K+1}\lambda}{2}\le e_\ast\le\frac{\lambda}{2}.
\]
The infimum is exactly $\lambda/2$ in the subsector with all time-zero readouts exact. At $\lambda=0$ there is an exact comparator in both sectors. The bounds cover every nonempty partition of the specified scalar and circle channels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\epsilon<w_{K+1}\lambda/2$, the factor assigned $c$ determines $b_1$. Points with different first bits have ternary readings separated by at least $1/3$, and a common factor prediction at time zero puts the two readings within $2\epsilon<1/3$. The other factor has at least one phase channel of weight $w\ge w_{K+1}$. On one fiber of that factor, each of the two controls $q=+1,-1$ with the same $u$ puts the true phase differences within $2\epsilon/w$ of zero on the circle. Their difference cancels $p_t-p'_t$ and has norm $2\lambda|b_1-b_1'|$. Thus $2\lambda|b_1-b_1'|\le4\epsilon/w$, so this factor also determines $b_1$. The product corner combining two different first bits is impossible. This is the fiber argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:circle-prefix-cofinal-margin}, and does not require any particular assignment of the phase channels.

The explicit comparator factors as $(p,\theta)$ and $b$, keeping the bit update and using $u+q\lambda/2$ for the phase update. Every initial channel is exact and every later error is at most $\lambda/2$. At $\lambda=0$ both updates and all readouts are exact. If time-zero readouts are required to be exact, no two phase channels can be on opposite factors: each phase has full circle range, but the joint image of any two has only the finitely many relative offsets $p_j-p_i$. A product would give every relative offset. Therefore all phases belong to the same factor, which includes $r_0$ of weight one. Under $q=1$, initial states on a common fiber of that factor with distinct first bits have next $r_0$ distance $\lambda$. With error below $\lambda/2$, this factor and the exact code factor would both determine $b_1$. The same corner contradiction gives the exact lower bound.
\end{proof}

For the identity witness, let candidate systems have any nonempty subset of $\mathcal P$ as their available preparation profiles. The identity object of each candidate is the union of its embedded branches, with the common cuts. The protocol requires a preparation for every available profile. Its coverage is part of the stipulated acquisition, and no identity tag is passed to the decoder. At observation error $\eta<g/2$ the profile decoder of Theorem~\ref{thm:addressable-identity-alternatives} identifies each prepared profile. Exhaustive coverage determines the candidate subset. Distinct candidate unions have Hausdorff distance at least $g$: a profile in one subset but absent from the other has distance at least $g$ to every branch in the latter, pointwise by the phase argument. This identifies the support object relative to the admitted catalogues. A sample restricted to shared profiles does not identify the catalogue.

\begin{proposition}[Common Support and the Obstruction to Stable State Labels]\label{prop:common-support-sham-obstruction}
The support $Z_{\mathcal P}$ provides the persistence, catalogue-relative identity, split-sector integration, discrete-time continuity and non-null differentiation witnesses below. Consider a legibility protocol assigning each state a label by decoding a fixed finite observation window under a repeated sham $(u_0,0)$. Suppose the label is robust to uniform observation noise of some positive radius, and invariant under advancing the sham by one step for every admissible starting state. Then the label is constant on each branch. No branch-preserving internal control can produce a shift in such a label. A target population requiring such a shift cannot meet L4 in this protocol.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The support is compact, nonempty and invariant under every internal control. Use the profile embedding of BaseCore with the phase pairs on finitely many cells of positive length and the bits on the original binary cells. This embedding is continuous by the summable cell weights. A constant profile would equal the first binary cell, hence would have value zero or one, but the first angular pair cannot equal either corresponding constant pair. Compactness gives uniform positive distance from the closed subspace of constants. The compact alternating-bit subset is invariant and uniformly attracting for every schedule because each step inserts its leading zero and complements the previous bits. These facts supply persistence. The catalogue witness above supplies identity, and Theorem~\ref{thm:integration-all-identity-channels} supplies integration. For continuity, the phase assignment is continuous in state and in $u$ at fixed discrete $q$, with complete observation codomain. In discrete time the step distance is at most one, so $5/4$ is a strict fragmentation bound. Declare regime cells by profile. Internal controls preserve each cell, so they do not cross incompatible cells. These are discrete-time witnesses in that declared regime model. For differentiation, two histories with equal phases and all-zero versus all-one bits have clean code separation one at time zero. From step two onward every schedule gives $b_2=1$ and $c\ge2/9$, ruling out the all-zero readout as an admissible attractor.

For the obstruction, write $\ell(x)=D(W(x))$ for the label obtained from a fixed finite window $W$ of the sham starting at $x$. At dynamical time $n\ge1$ the sham sends the phases of any two starting states on the same branch to identical values, and the code difference is at most $3^{-n}$. Indeed $c(Rb)=(1-c(b))/3$. The same bound holds over the whole subsequent sham window in the maximum observation metric. Take $n$ so large that $3^{-n}$ is below the allowed noise radius. The clean window of one state at that time is also a permissible noisy window of the other, so both receive the same label. Repeated stability transports that equality back to the initial states. Hence $\ell$ is constant on each branch. Every internal target keeps the profile, so comparison of labels before and after a target cannot give a shift. This is an obstruction under the repeated-sham state-label requirement. The paired response statistic of Theorem~\ref{thm:paired-integration-rupture} depends on a prepared bit and two interventions and has a different experimental unit. It remains a valid response diagnostic and does not supply the stable state-label witness considered here.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Legibility of Matched Paired Responses}

\begin{definition}[Matched Paired Response Window]\label{def:matched-paired-response-window}
On the common support $Z_{\mathcal P}$, a paired response window is the ordered pair of time-zero and time-one readout histories of two copies prepared from the same $(p,\theta,b)$ with a common input $u$ and coupling $\lambda$. The two arms have fixed signs $q=+1$ and $q=-1$. The metric is the maximum of the four-channel readout distances over both arms and both times. Use the distance $D=\rho(y_+,y_-)$ between the two observed time-one $r_0$ coordinates. The decoder returns low for $D\le L/2$, high for $D\ge 3L/2$, and abstains otherwise. Here $L=1/12$ turns. Preparation, command matching and arm order are guaranteed by the protocol. The decoder uses no hidden state or coupling parameter.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Legibility of Matched Integration Responses]\label{thm:matched-integration-legibility}
Use the windows of Definition~\ref{def:matched-paired-response-window} with $\lambda\in\{0\}\cup[L,1/9]$ and adversarial observation error at most $\eta=1/256$ in the paired metric. The following are the L1--L6 properties of Definition~\ref{def:ileg} evaluated on the paired-response domain.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The clean high and low response sets have separation exactly $L$.
\item All observations in the allowed noise balls preserve the clean class, with zero abstentions.
\item For any train of at least three freshly reset pairs at a fixed coupling and fixed prepared first bit, the class persists when the common input phase varies across pairs.
\item In the nonempty target population, retain the same state and common input, prepare $b_1=1$, and ablate $\lambda\in[L,1/9]$ to zero between paired trials. The class changes from high to low with zero misses.
\item In the nonempty sham population, retain the state and coupling and vary the common input across paired trials. Both first-bit strata are allowed. The false-change and abstention rates are zero.
\item On the resolved domain one bit retains the class with zero discrete class distortion, and zero bits are insufficient. Including the audit status of abstention requires two bits in a fixed-length encoding.
\end{enumerate}
At error radius $L/2$ a low and a high experiment can have identical full observations. If the common-input condition is removed, even a noise-free uncoupled experiment can decode as high.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The clean score is $2\lambda b_1$, since $2\lambda\le2/9$ and the phase input cancels. The score is $2$-Lipschitz in the paired metric by the triangle inequality. Thus the clean score gap $2L$ implies a metric separation of at least $L$. Equality is attained with the same preparation and common input at $\lambda=0,L$ with $b_1=1$: each post-control phase changes by $L$ and no other channel or time contributes a larger distance.

Every noisy low score is at most $2\eta<L/2$, and every noisy high score is at least $2L-2\eta>3L/2$. These inequalities hold for all allowed errors with no independence assumption on the noise. They apply pointwise along any reset train with the stated fixed bit and coupling. Under the matched target intervention the score changes from the high region to the low region. Under a sham it stays in its original region for either bit. The score on each paired trial therefore gives pointwise bounds of zero for misses, false changes and abstentions on the respective populations. The matching relation for target comparisons is equality of the preparation and common input, with arm order fixed. It is transitive, and equality of the decoded response classes is transitive as well. Encoding low as zero and high as one preserves both classes and all such class comparisons under the discrete loss that is one for a wrong class and zero otherwise.

At $\eta=L/2$, the midpoint construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:paired-integration-rupture} applies to every phase channel by shifting each by the same signed error in its arm, leaving the code and time-zero readings exact. The observation has score $L$ and the decoder abstains. The same score occurs without noise at the intermediate coupling $L/2$ with $b_1=1$, outside the certified coupling domain. With $\lambda=0$ and mismatched commands $u+L$ and $u-L$, the clean score is $2L$ and is indistinguishable from the clean matched response at $\lambda=L$, $b_1=1$. Command matching is thus a necessary acquisition control for this inference.
\end{proof}

A low response with $b_1=0$ occurs in either coupling regime. The response label concerns the controlled pair, and persistence here concerns reset repetitions rather than the advancing state of one trajectory. This experiment leaves open the single-trajectory legibility criterion and the joint operational certificate. The coupling intervention is a model ablation between trials, with the support and readout fixed throughout.

\subsection{A Writable Prefix on the Common Support}

Use the seven profiles above and the phase-memory plant of Papers VI--VII \cite{paper6,paper7} with $\lambda=1/12$, $a=1/2$, $\kappa=1/8$, and $|m|\le1/4$. Its state is $(p,\theta,b,m)$. Let $b^\star=(0,1,0,1,\ldots)$, $J=\{3,4,5,6,7\}$, and $A_j(b)=\mathbf 1_{\{b_j=b_j^\star\}}$. A write $w=(j,v)$, $j\in J$ and $v\in\{0,1\}$, overwrites one digit after the native shift-complement $R$. Denote it by $E_w$, with $E_\varnothing$ the identity. A neutral command can also carry a role probe $h=(i,\ell)$, where $i\in J$, $\ell\in\{0,1\}$. Fix $p_0=(0,0,0)$, $p_1=(0,0,1/8)$ as the role profiles. Write $\delta=1/(16\cdot 3^7)$ and set $G_h(p,b)=\delta A_i(b)\mathbf 1_{\{p=p_\ell\}}$ for a probe and $G_\varnothing=0$. The full update preserves $p$ and has components
\begin{align*}
 b'&=E_w(Rb),\\
 \theta'&=u+q\lambda b_1+\kappa\mathbf 1_{\{q=0\}}m+G_h(p,b)\pmod 1,\\
 m'&=a s(q)m+\lambda q b_1 .
\end{align*}
Here $s(q)=-1$ only at $q=-1$ and $h=\varnothing$ for $q\ne0$. The probe feeds back the pre-transition tape through the phase input. The write affects the post-transition tape. The observed channels are still $r_j=\theta+p_j$ (using the profile offsets at the three cuts) and $c(b)$. The indices $0,1$ on the role profiles denote catalogue elements, not observation cuts.

\begin{proposition}[Projective Prefix Writes Preserve Identity and Integration]\label{prop:writable-prefix-preservation}
Each tape update commutes with prefix truncation at every positive depth. The full update is compatible on the cofinal system of depths at least seven. The safe support is invariant and the branch contraction factor is at most $9/16$ under common commands in the phase-code-memory metric of Paper VI. Catalogue separation is $1/80$. In the four-channel split sector,
\[
 \frac{1}{216}\le e_\ast\le\frac{1}{24},
\]
with infimum $1/24$ when all initial readouts are exact.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At cut depth $n$, truncate the shift-complement and apply $E_w$ at its address if the address is visible. Each retained output digit depends only on the retained input prefix or on the declared write. These finite updates commute with deletion of later digits. At $n\ge7$ the phase and memory transitions also use only retained bits. The underlying tape and the profile link maps are unchanged, so the original summable weights still apply. A write changes $c$ by at most $2\cdot3^{-j}$ at site $j$. A cut omitting the probed bit cannot in general determine the probed phase.

For $j>k$ the possible increase is at most $2\cdot3^{-j}$, giving ratio at most $5/3$. Since $c(Rb)=(1-c(b))/3$, the tape update has bound $5/9$. For a neutral probe it is at most $(4\kappa+\delta 3^7)d$, since a difference in a bit at $i$ implies code distance at least $3^{-i}$. The memory bound in the weighted metric is $(a+3\lambda/4)d$. Their maximum with $5d/9$ is $9d/16$. Also $|m'|\le5/24<1/4$.

Restrict an enlarged comparator to the native commands. \end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite Legibility on One Tower Trajectory]\label{prop:writable-tower-trajectory-legibility}
On this support use the binary decoder that reports whether the first ten tape digits contain a deviation from $b^\star$. It has clean code separation $3^{-10}$ and is correct at error at most $3^{-10}/4$. From a preparation with $b=b^\star$, any of the five opposite writes in $J$ changes the label. The post-target label persists through three more native sham steps on the same trajectory. The matched sham has zero false shifts. One bit preserves the two classes exactly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The length-ten cylinders lie in code intervals of length $3^{-10}$ with distinct intervals separated by at least $3^{-10}$. For the reference interval, a neighbouring cylinder differing only at the last digit attains that gap. The finite prefix decoder using ternary midpoint cuts is correct for the stated error. A deviation at $j$ advances to $j+1$ under the sham since $Rb^\star=b^\star$. Thus a target starts with one deviation and retains it within the ten inspected digits for four consecutive observations including the initial post-target observation. On the sham trajectory all ten digits stay equal to the reference. The decoder reads the code channel with no command label. For Definition~\ref{def:ileg}, the matched population uses equality of the preparation and nuisance commands, the target and sham error rates are zero, and the two labels have zero distortion under the one-bit encoding. Zero bits cannot preserve both labels. This is a finite persistence window. A lesion at site seven leaves the decoder's inspection range after four further shifts. On the declared catalogue and split sector, these results put the six witness types on one admissible support. The legibility margin uses a finite window and the code-noise metric of this protocol.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharp Time Horizons for a Single Prefix Lesion]\label{prop:writable-tower-legibility-horizon}
Let $t=0$ be the observation just after an opposite write at $j\in\{3,\ldots,7\}$ from the alternating preparation, and then apply only native neutral shams without writes or probes. Against the matched unlesioned preparation, the scalar code gap at $t$ is $D_{j,t}=2\,3^{-(j+t)}$, with all other observed coordinates identical. For depth $N\ge j$, the decoder of the first $N$ digits keeps the target label exactly at times $0\le t\le N-j$ on clean data and under code error at most $3^{-N}/4$. The clean gap is at least the resolution $3^{-N}$ exactly on this same interval.

For the two specified preparations, classification from a single observation at time $t$ is uniformly possible under code errors bounded by $\eta$ per observation exactly when $3^{-(j+t)}>\eta$. In particular, for $N=10$ and $\eta=3^{-10}/4$, the ten-digit horizon is $10-j$ and the latest identifiable time for the matched contrast is $11-j$. The uniform horizons across all five target sites are three and four sham steps, respectively.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The alternating tape is fixed by $R$. Its lesion differs at one position, which shifts from $j$ to $j+t$ and changes sign at each sham, by $c(Rb)=(1-c(b))/3$. The leading bit, phases and memory agree in both arms. Thus the code gap decreases by the exact factor $1/3$ for this schedule. The full-state common-command bound $9/16$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:writable-prefix-preservation} still applies; it is weaker than this scalar calculation. The depth-$N$ decoder distinguishes the tapes exactly while $j+t\le N$, and its cylinder separation gives the stated noise guarantee. For $j+t>N$ both decode to the reference, even at zero noise. Since $2/3<1$, the same index boundary separates the clean gaps above and below $3^{-N}$.

For the two-point decision with known clean preparations, choose the closer clean code when their closed $\eta$-intervals are disjoint. At equality or overlap the scalar midpoint, with all phases unchanged, is a common admissible observation. This proves sharpness, even if the code observation is accompanied by all phase channels. At the stated $\eta$, $3^{-11}=3^{-10}/3>\eta$ but $3^{-12}=3^{-10}/9<\eta$. This is a bound on recovery from a new observation at time $t$; a retained record of an earlier resolved observation can still identify the initial write.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Operational Continuity}
\begin{definition}[Operational continuity and fragmentation bound]\label{def:icont}
An admissible system $S$ satisfies operational continuity, written $\Icont(S)=1$, if there exists a complete metric space $(\mathcal{E}_S,d_{\mathcal{E}})$ and an admissible assignment
\[
\Pi_S : \Aset \to \mathcal{E}_S
\]
such that:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item for every admissible intervention schedule $u_\bullet$, the induced path $t \mapsto \Pi_S(x_t)$ is continuous on admissible windows;
\item there exists $\delta_{\mathrm{cont}}(S) > 0$ for which the fragmentation functional
\[
F_{S,T}(x;u_\bullet)
=
\sup_{0 \le t < T} d_{\mathcal{E}}\bigl(\Pi_S(x_t), \Pi_S(x_{t+1})\bigr)
\]
stays below $\delta_{\mathrm{cont}}(S)$ on all admissible histories;
\item no admissible finite-energy perturbation produces a discontinuous jump into an incompatible regime class while $\Id_S$ is preserved.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

This packages the \textit{Structural Assignment Regimes} continuity/fragmentation burden \cite{paper2}. The new paper reuses the regime logic rather than duplicating its proof.

\paragraph{Failure mode.}
If $\Icont(S)=0$, the class can fragment into incompatible operational regimes under admissible evolution. Then any six-predicate operational criterion becomes unstable under its own dynamics.

\subsection{Non-Null Differentiation}
\begin{definition}[Non-null differentiation and differentiation gap]\label{def:idiff}
An admissible system $S$ satisfies non-null differentiation, written $\Idiff(S)=1$, if there exist $x,y \in \Aset$, a horizon $T$, and a readout subfamily $\Read^\star \subseteq R$ such that
\[
\dist\bigl(h_{S,T}(x;u_\bullet), h_{S,T}(y;u_\bullet)\bigr) \ge \delta_{\mathrm{diff}}(S) > 0
\]
for some admissible schedule $u_\bullet$, and if the null operational class $\nullclass$ is not an asymptotically stable admissible attractor.
\end{definition}

The second clause is indispensable. Without \textit{Null-Regime Instability} there would be no reason to exclude a stable null class. \textit{Null-Regime Instability} provides that exclusion through witness-relative null-instability and conditional non-nullity \cite{paper3}. The new invariant converts that upstream result into a constitutive condition for $\Cop$.

\paragraph{Failure mode.}
If $\Idiff(S)=0$, either all admissible histories collapse to indistinguishability or the null class remains admissibly stable. In either case $\Qop(S)$ is empty or trivialized.

\subsection{Operational Legibility}\label{sec:legibility}
\begin{definition}[Decoder family and structural closure]\label{def:decoder}
A decoder family $\Dec_S$ for $S$ is a family of maps from admissible readout windows $h_{S,T}(x;u_\bullet)$ to a declared class set $\Class_S$, indexed by admissible horizons and intervention schedules. The family is \emph{structurally closed} when all its members use the same class set, class equality composes exactly, and the induced intervention-response cells are transitive under the certified matching relation. Definition~\ref{def:ileg} below adds the operational clauses required before such a structurally closed family counts as certified for legibility.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Operational legibility and legibility margin]\label{def:ileg}
An admissible system $S$ satisfies operational legibility, written $\Ileg(S)=1$, if there exist:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a certified readout subfamily $\Read^{\mathrm{leg}} \subseteq R$;
\item a certified decoder family $\Dec_S$ acting on windows of length at least $\tau_{\min}(S)$;
\item a class set $\Class_S$;
\item a measurement protocol with separation $\sigma>0$, readout noise radius $r>0$, minimum duration $\tau_{\min}\ge1$, miss and false-positive budgets $p_{\mathrm{miss}},p_{\mathrm{fp}}\in[0,1)$, and a finite compression budget $B$ with class-distortion tolerance $d_{\mathrm{comp}}$;
\end{enumerate}
such that the following conditions hold:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.7em]
\item[(L1) Separability] Distinct declared classes have distance at least $\sigma$ in the specified clean comparison domain.
\item[(L2) Noise robustness] On the specified noise domain, perturbations with readout norm at most $r$ preserve the label.
\item[(L3) Persistence window] Under declared sham schedules, labels remain stable throughout every tested window of duration $\tau_{\min}$.
\item[(L4) Intervention fidelity] In a declared nonempty target population, the miss probability for a predicted class shift is at most $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$.
\item[(L5) Negative controls] In a declared nonempty sham population, the false-shift probability is at most $p_{\mathrm{fp}}$.
\item[(L6) Structured compressibility] An encoder with at most $2^B$ codes and a code-to-class map preserve nontrivial class structure with distortion at most $d_{\mathrm{comp}}$ in the declared class metric.
\end{description}
\end{definition}

This is where the paper touches \textit{Forensic Predictions} most concretely. \textit{Forensic Predictions} already specifies integrity gates, comparator discipline, latency invalidation, and negative controls \cite{paper4}. The present paper does not replace those protocols. It uses them to make operational legibility a constrained object rather than a loose slogan.

\paragraph{Failure mode.}
If $\Ileg(S)=0$, any putative class remains operationally opaque. The framework may still discuss latent structure elsewhere, but the class required for $\Cop$ is undefined because it cannot be recovered, discriminated, and stress-tested under admissible conditions.

\subsection{Necessity of Each Invariant}
\begin{proposition}[Each invariant is individually necessary]\label{prop:necessity}
If any one of $\Iper,\allowbreak \Iri,\allowbreak \Iint,\allowbreak \Icont,\allowbreak \Idiff,\allowbreak \Ileg$ fails, then $S \notin \Cop$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The six failure modes are non-redundant. Without $\Iper$ there is no stable domain for class assignment. Without $\Iri$ there is no unique identity object supporting consistent class transport. Without $\Iint$ the system is reducible into independent blocks. Without $\Icont$ admissible trajectories fragment across incompatible regime classes. Without $\Idiff$ the admissible partition collapses to nullity or triviality. Without $\Ileg$ the partition is not recoverable under admissible readout and intervention discipline. Since the definition of $\Cop$ given in Section~\ref{sec:cop} requires all six, failure of any one is exclusionary.
\end{proof}

\section{Six-Predicate Operational Criterion, Certified Response Classes, and Equivalence}\label{sec:cop}
\subsection{Certified Response Classes Classes}

\begin{definition}[Operational indistinguishability]\label{def:indist}
For $x,y \in \Aset$, write $x \sim_{Q,S} y$ if, for every certified decoder $D \in \Dec_S$, every admissible horizon $T \ge \tau_{\min}(S)$, and every admissible intervention schedule $u_\bullet$,
\[
D\bigl(h_{S,T}(x;u_\bullet)\bigr)=D\bigl(h_{S,T}(y;u_\bullet)\bigr),
\]
and the intervention response of the two histories remains matched within the legibility margin.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Well-definedness of certified response classes classes]\label{prop:qop-welldefined}
If $\Iper(S)=\Iri(S)=\Icont(S)=\Idiff(S)=\Ileg(S)=1$, then $\sim_{Q,S}$ is an equivalence relation on $\Aset$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Reflexivity is immediate. Symmetry follows because decoder equality and certified response-cell matching are symmetric conditions. For transitivity, let $x \sim_{Q,S} y$ and $y \sim_{Q,S} z$. Fix any certified decoder $D\in\Dec_S$, admissible horizon $T\ge\tau_{\min}(S)$, and admissible intervention schedule $u_\bullet$. The first relation gives
\[
D(h_{S,T}(x;u_\bullet))=D(h_{S,T}(y;u_\bullet)),
\]
and the second gives
\[
D(h_{S,T}(y;u_\bullet))=D(h_{S,T}(z;u_\bullet)).
\]
Hence $D(h_{S,T}(x;u_\bullet))=D(h_{S,T}(z;u_\bullet))$. The intervention-response clause is transitive by the structural closure requirement in Definition~\ref{def:decoder}. Since $D$, $T$, and $u_\bullet$ were arbitrary, Definition~\ref{def:indist} gives $x \sim_{Q,S} z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Certified response classes]\label{def:qop}
Assume Proposition~\ref{prop:qop-welldefined}. The certified response classes of $S$ are the quotient classes
\[
\Qop(S) = \Aset / {\sim_{Q,S}}.
\]
An individual class $[x]_{\sim_{Q,S}} \in \Qop(S)$ is called an certified response classes class of $S$.
\end{definition}

This definition is disciplined. It does not identify an certified response classes class with a human qualitative state. It identifies it with a persistent, intervention-sensitive, decoder-certified class of admissible internal differentiation.

\subsection{Six-Predicate Operational Criterion}
\begin{definition}[Six-predicate operational criterion class membership]\label{def:cop}
An admissible system $S$ belongs to $\Cop$, written $S \in \Cop$, if there exists a non-empty admissible support $\Aset$ such that
\[
\Iper(S)=\Iri(S)=\Iint(S)=\Icont(S)=\Idiff(S)=\Ileg(S)=1.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Subdetermination of membership]\label{rem:subdet}
Membership in $\Cop$ depends on auxiliary choices: the decoder family $\Dec$, the compression map, and the identity object $\Id$. The paper does not prove that these choices are canonically determined by the system tuple $S$ alone. Different auxiliary choices may yield different membership verdicts for the same system.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Minimality of the criterion]
The criterion is conjunctive. No proper subset of the six invariants is taken as sufficient in this paper. This is intentional: weaker criteria collapse either into mere persistence, mere integration, or mere complexity claims, all of which are shown to be insufficient.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exact and Approximate Structural Equivalence}
\begin{definition}[Structural equivalence datum]\label{def:eqdatum}
A structural datum $(\chi,\upsilon,\rho)$ consists of a bi-Lipschitz bijection of the declared state supports, extended to the admissible comparison neighborhoods whenever attraction is tested; a bijection of admissible interventions and their schedules; and bijections of readout indices and values. The latter include calibrated value metrics and class-label renamings. Time indices and protocol horizons are not reparameterized. All data have inverse correspondences on their comparison domains.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Exact and approximate structural equivalence]\label{def:equiv}
Write $S_1\simeq_{(\Gamma,\eps)}S_2$ if a structural datum satisfies the following primitive-data conditions, with $\eps\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Geometric calibration.} Supports, admissibility regions, designated collapse/null data, and the neighborhoods used for attraction correspond under $\chi$. State/readout comparison metrics and protocol losses are transported pullbacks, so their calibrated comparisons are isometric. Displayed coordinate metrics need not be isometric: conversion to the common chart has finite inverse Lipschitz constants $L_{\chi^{-1}},L_{\rho^{-1}}$, equal to one in the normalized case. Unadjusted numerical thresholds in different units are not matched data.
\item \textbf{Dynamic intertwining.} For all declared initial states, schedules and times,
\[
 \sup d_{X_2}(\chi\Phi_{1,u_\bullet}^{t}x,\Phi_{2,\upsilon(u_\bullet)}^{t}\chi x)\le\eps.
\]
Exact one-step intertwining on an invariant comparison domain extends to all compositions by induction. Approximate finite-window observations are not a bound on the full transition operators unless every comparison state and control is covered.
\item \textbf{Readout compatibility.} After value-coordinate calibration,
\[
 \sup_{x,u_\bullet,t,\alpha}d_{\rho(\alpha)}
 (\rho_\alpha r_{1,\alpha}^{t},r_{2,\rho(\alpha)}^{t})\le\eps.
\]
Here $r_{1,\alpha}^{t}=r_\alpha^{(1)}(\Phi_{1,u_\bullet}^t x)$, $r_{2,\rho(\alpha)}^{t}=r_{\rho(\alpha)}^{(2)}(\Phi_{2,\upsilon(u_\bullet)}^t\chi x)$, and $d_{\rho(\alpha)}$ is the target readout metric.
\item \textbf{Protocol and sector correspondence.} Admissible path domains, finite-energy perturbations and their cost units, identity candidate cones, causal factorization/comparator families, regime-coordinate assignments and their metrics, decoder algorithms and codebooks correspond bijectively under the datum. Target/sham matching, sampling laws, horizons, noise domains, class metrics, decision thresholds and compression budgets are transported as primitive protocol data. The decoder-family relation $\Dec_{S_2}=\rho_*\Dec_{S_1}$ means transport of candidate algorithms and their domains, not assumed preservation of their certification verdicts. Families of external rivals must be transported as well as the internal plant.
\end{enumerate}
The exact case is $\eps=0$, written $S_1\simeq_\Gamma S_2$. Neither invariant-margin preservation nor equality of operational partitions is a clause of this definition.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Calibrated presentation radius]\label{def:transport-calibration}
Fix the structural geometry and protocol/sector catalogues of Definition~\ref{def:equiv}. Express both systems on the same coordinate domains. A presentation $z$ records its primitive one-step transition maps and instantaneous readout maps on the entire declared state/control domains, including the neighborhoods needed for attraction. On the finite-distance component use their maximum uniform metric $d_{\mathcal P}$ in common calibrated units. No invariant score enters this metric. Global failure sets below use the original all-time predicates. Finite sampled histories alone do not bound this full operator metric or certify a positive global radius.

For each of the original six predicates, let $B_j$ be its failure set in this fixed presentation class, including undefined required evidence as failure. With a common cap $R>0$, define
\[
 \delta_j^{\mathrm{cal}}(z)=\min\{R,\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal P}(z,B_j)\},\qquad
 \delta_\star^{\mathrm{cal}}=\min_j\delta_j^{\mathrm{cal}},
\]
where distance to the empty set is $+\infty$. These are maximal capped robust radii in the declared metric, not arbitrary reported lower bounds. A positive radius certifies the predicate throughout its open ball; zero does not imply failure at the center. Raw gaps, ceilings, risks and durations in the six witness definitions are not silently identified with these radii. Their conversion to a positive calibrated radius needs its own evidence.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Rupture]\label{def:rupture}
Given $S$, a rupture is any loss of admissibility or loss of at least one critical invariant margin beyond its admissible threshold. Ruptures are classified as:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{persistence rupture} (support loss or collapse exposure),
\item \textbf{identity rupture} (loss of unique rigid identity),
\item \textbf{integration rupture} (reducible factorization),
\item \textbf{continuity rupture} (fragmentation into incompatible classes),
\item \textbf{differentiation rupture} (null or trivial collapse),
\item \textbf{legibility rupture} (decoder, noise, or negative-control failure).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\section{Support Geometry and Partition Transport}
The main theorems become short only because the transport lemmas are made explicit here.

\begin{lemma}[Support transfer under exact equivalence]\label{lem:support-transfer}
An exact structural equivalence transports a nonempty compact admissible support $A_1$ to an admissible support $A_2=\chi(A_1)$ on the specified comparison domain.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The bi-Lipschitz bijection preserves nonemptiness and compactness. Exact intertwining transports forward invariance, and the geometric correspondence transports admissibility constraints and disjointness from collapse. On the comparison neighborhoods, conjugacy preserves trajectories approaching an attractor: the Lipschitz bounds preserve convergence to the corresponding set, in both directions. Thus the attractor-support condition is transported. A bijection of supports alone, without the relevant attraction neighborhoods, would not justify this last conclusion. No invariant radius is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Decoder transport]\label{lem:decoder-transport}
For exact equivalence, let $H$ be the componentwise calibrated readout-window bijection and $\kappa$ its class-label bijection. The transported algorithm is $D_2=\kappa\circ D_1\circ H^{-1}$ on the declared clean and noisy observation domains. It is certified whenever $D_1$ is, and every target decoder arises by inverse transport.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Readout compatibility gives $H(h_{S_1,T}(x;u_\bullet))=h_{S_2,T}(\chi x;\upsilon(u_\bullet))$. The isometric calibration transports clean separations and noise balls (L1--L2). Equal horizons and sham schedules transport persistence windows (L3). Matching relations and pushforward sampling laws identify the same miss and false-shift events, so their probabilities coincide (L4--L5). Transporting the encoder by $H^{-1}$ and its code-to-class map by $\kappa$ preserves code count and class distortion (L6). Candidate-family correspondence supplies the algorithm, rather than assuming these six verdicts. Applying the same argument to the inverse datum gives the converse.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Operational classes are closed under exact transport]\label{prop:transport}
Under exact structural equivalence,
\[
 x\sim_{Q,S_1}y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\chi x\sim_{Q,S_2}\chi y.
\]
Consequently $[x]\mapsto[\chi x]$ is a bijection of the operational quotients whenever those quotients are defined.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:decoder-transport} bijects certified decoders, and schedules correspond in both directions. For each such pair of decoders, equality of their outputs is equivalent because $\kappa$ is injective. The raw intervention-response matching relation is transported by clause4 of Definition~\ref{def:equiv}; its equality tests and transitivity are therefore preserved. Taking the universal quantifiers in Definition~\ref{def:indist} proves the displayed equivalence. Bijectivity of $\chi$ then proves bijectivity on quotient classes, without assuming the partition in the equivalence datum.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Approximate margin preservation]\label{prop:margins}
Use the common presentation metric and fixed sectors of Definition~\ref{def:transport-calibration}. If $S_1\simeq_{(\Gamma,\eps)}S_2$, then
\[
 |\delta_j^{\mathrm{cal}}(S_1)-\delta_j^{\mathrm{cal}}(S_2)|\le C_{\chi,\rho}\eps,
 \quad C_{\chi,\rho}=\max\{1,L_{\chi^{-1}},L_{\rho^{-1}}\}.
\]
The normalized isometric case has $C_{\chi,\rho}=1$. Hence $\delta_\star^{\mathrm{cal}}(S_2)\ge\delta_\star^{\mathrm{cal}}(S_1)-C_{\chi,\rho}\eps$. At exact equivalence all six predicates, their calibrated radii, and the operational indistinguishability relation are preserved. Optimal raw margins defined by corresponding infima/suprema of isometric losses are also equal; arbitrary chosen certificate values need not be.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First establish exact predicate transport from primitive data. Support transfer gives persistence. Coherent identity-candidate cones and their separating comparison metrics transport existence/uniqueness and separation of alternatives. Conjugating every admitted factorization and its inverse preserves the entire reproduction-error comparison. Regime-coordinate maps, their metrics and the same time domains preserve continuity, fragmentation and compatible intervention responses. Designated null sets and conjugated attraction neighborhoods preserve the null-exclusion clause and readout differentiation. Lemma~\ref{lem:decoder-transport} supplies legibility. These six arguments identify the same failure sets $B_j$ after changing structural coordinates; they do not use any margin-preservation clause. Proposition~\ref{prop:transport} separately proves partition transport.

For the approximate bound, take the dynamic discrepancy at one step and the readout discrepancy at time zero, on their full declared domains, and pull them into the common coordinate chart. The inverse coordinate Lipschitz constants give $d_{\mathcal P}(z_1,z_2)\le C_{\chi,\rho}\eps$. For any fixed nonempty failure set $B$, triangle inequality gives
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(z_1,B)\le d_{\mathcal P}(z_1,z_2)+\operatorname{dist}(z_2,B).
\]
Reverse the points and cap by the same $R$: the capped distance changes by at most $d_{\mathcal P}(z_1,z_2)$. If $B$ is empty both radii equal $R$. Taking minima over six coordinates proves the budget bound. At exact equivalence the presentations agree, so radii agree. For an optimal raw loss, a bijection of candidates/tests with equal loss values preserves its infimum or supremum; it does not force two arbitrarily reported lower certificates to be equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No automatic transfer without the sector]\label{rem:sector-nontransfer}
Conjugacy of an internal plant alone does not transport a rival-relative margin. For example, a constant-zero target has optimal error one against the sole constant-one rival, but error zero when a constant-zero rival is added, even though the plant and readout are unchanged. This is the no-automatic-transfer boundary of Paper III and the later bridge analysis. Clause4 of Definition~\ref{def:equiv} requires the corresponding rival sector, costs and loss calibration, not its verdict. Finite-time agreement likewise does not determine later collapse. Finally, certified lower bounds chosen arbitrarily can differ even for the same presentation; only a fixed functional or a specified calibration admits the quantitative transport theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why this section matters]
Without Lemmas~\ref{lem:support-transfer} and \ref{lem:decoder-transport}, Theorem~\ref{thm:substrate} would be only intuitive. These lemmas are the exact reason the paper can speak of substrate invariance without collapsing into slogan-level analogy.
\end{remark}

\section{Inherited Inputs and Consolidation Step}
The six invariants are new as a packaged criterion, but they are not independent inventions. This section records the exact transfer by which upstream ownership becomes present membership data. The burden is narrow: identify the imported result each invariant uses, then isolate the consolidation step that is genuinely new here.

\begin{proposition}[Core-to-Persistence Transfer with a Common Support]\label{prop:core-per}
Suppose a Core realization has a nonempty compact admissible support that is invariant under every admitted control, stays at positive distance from the collapse set, and contains or converges into an admitted attractor region. Then $\Iper$ holds on that support.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These bounds verify Definition~\ref{def:iper}. Fixed points for separate controls do not by themselves give a common forward-invariant support.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rigid Identity Transfer with a Declared Gap]\label{prop:p1-ri}
An identified unique inverse-limit identity object and a positive lower deformation bound for every distinct admitted alternative certify $\Iri$ on their declared support.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The inverse-limit identification and separation establish the two clauses of Definition~\ref{def:iri}. A channel-level uniqueness result does not supply a uniform lower deformation bound for a family of alternatives.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Regime Continuity Transfer on the Declared Windows]\label{prop:p2-cont}
If the regime assignment takes values in the declared complete comparison space, its paths and fragmentation meet the time-domain bounds, and admissible interventions preserving identity cannot jump to incompatible regimes, then $\Icont$ holds there.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These hypotheses supply Definition~\ref{def:icont}. In discrete time a quantitative step bound and the regime transition check carry information beyond topological continuity of the time index.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Null-Regime Transfer with Temporal Evidence]\label{prop:p3-diff}
If an admissible common schedule supplies a positive separation between two readout windows and the null class is excluded as an asymptotically stable admissible attractor, then $\Idiff$ holds on the support.

\subsection{Existence of Six-Predicate Operational Criterion}
\begin{theorem}[Existence of six-predicate operational criterion]\label{thm:existence}
Let $S=(X,\Phi,C,R,\Gamma,U)$ be an admissible system with non-empty admissible support $\Aset$. If
\[
\Iper(S)=\Iri(S)=\Iint(S)=\Icont(S)=\Idiff(S)=\Ileg(S)=1,
\]
then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $S \in \Cop$;
\item $\Qop(S)$ is well-defined;
\item $\Qop(S) \neq \varnothing$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Membership in $\Cop$ follows immediately from Definition~\ref{def:cop}. Since $\Iper,\allowbreak \Iri,\allowbreak \Icont,\allowbreak \Idiff,\allowbreak \Ileg$ hold, Proposition~\ref{prop:qop-welldefined} applies and yields a well-defined quotient $\Qop(S)$. Because $\Aset$ is non-empty, the quotient is non-empty. This proves a defined nonempty quotient. A claim of at least two classes additionally requires a pair distinguished by a certified decoder or certified response-cell map; a raw state/readout gap alone does not establish that link.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Decoder-Visible Separation Gives a Nontrivial Quotient]\label{prop:decoder-visible-separation}
If the quotient is defined and two admissible states receive different labels under one certified decoder, horizon and common intervention schedule, or under a certified response-cell map, then $|\Qop(S)|\ge2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
That particular certified test violates the universal equality required for equivalence. The two states therefore have different quotient classes. This does not infer nontriviality from unobserved latent separation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All Six Predicates on an Infinite-Dimensional Support]\label{thm:infinite-six-predicate-witness}
The system of Definition~\ref{def:hilbert-prefix-system}, with exactly its declared global comparison and observation data, has
\[
 (\Iper,\allowbreak \Iri,\allowbreak \Iint,\allowbreak \Icont,\allowbreak \Idiff,\allowbreak \Ileg)=(1,1,1,1,1,1).
\]
Its support contains boxes of arbitrarily large dimension, and every Hilbert coordinate participates in the phase/response coupling. This is a global analytic witness, not an inference from finitely many truncations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Support and persistence.} Finite boxes and the uniform squared tail bound $\sum_{j>n}2a_j^2=(2/3)4^{-n}$ prove compactness of $Q$. Its coordinate boxes have unbounded dimension. The circle and prefix factors are compact, so is $A$. The Hilbert update has coordinate magnitude at most $17a_j/32<a_j$; every control preserves $A$. In the first angular pair and the binary coordinate, distance to the diagonal is at least $(\sqrt2-1)/(2\sqrt3)>1/16$. Indeed the distance of that pair from either binary diagonal point is at least $(\sqrt2-1)/2$, and minimizing over the common diagonal value loses at most a factor $\sqrt3$. Thus all admissible states are uniformly separated from constant-profile collapse.

On a fibre of fixed $b_1$, each fixed $D$ is a contraction with constant at most $1/2$ for $\max(\rho,d_B,\|\cdot\|)$. The binary tail contracts by $1/3$. Phase differences are at most $(\lambda/2)\|z-z'\|$; Hilbert differences are at most $\|z-z'\|/4+\|w\|\rho(\theta,\theta')/4$, using $2\pi<8$. Banach therefore gives a nonempty attracting fixed-point set, one point on each head-bit fibre. For sham it is $\theta=0$, $z=w/24$, with alternating tail beginning in zero. The support contains these attractors and has persistence margin $1/16$. No global contraction across the two preserved head-bit fibres is claimed.

\emph{Identity.} The finite stages are compact metric spaces with continuous surjective truncations and coherent right inverses obtained by padding zero coordinates; the angular-jump transport is a coordinate homeomorphism. They meet the projective regularity assumptions. Full readout determines the angle, every bit and every Hilbert coordinate, hence the declared inverse-limit object up to its prescribed equivalence. Finite coordinate truncations converge uniformly: the binary tail is at most $3^{-n}$ and the Hilbert tail norm at most $\sqrt{2/3}\,2^{-n}$ before scaling. Every finite cut has distinct continuations with distinct full readout, so non-finite determination holds. In the common weighted history metric, the distance between the two declared angular alternatives is $\beta(1/4)/(1+\beta)=1/16$: the triangle inequality on early and late angles supplies the lower bound and an opposite initial angular shift attains it, with identical bits and Hilbert coordinates. Hence the true reading selects $I_0$ uniquely with rigidity gap $1/16$. This gap is not asserted for a continuum of angular alternatives.

\emph{Integration.} Suppose a split comparator has error $e<\beta\lambda/4=1/384$. The factor carrying $c$ determines $b_1$, since opposite head bits have code gap at least $1/3>2e$. Some phase channel, of weight $s\ge\beta$, lies on the other factor. If two states in a fibre of that factor had opposite head bits, their values of $b_1+\ell(z)/4$ would differ by at least $1/2$. Under the same control $D_{0,1,0}$ their true next phase separation would be at least $\lambda/2$, whereas their common predicted phase allows at most $2e/s$. These short phase differences do not wrap around the circle, since their maximum is $3\lambda/2<1/2$. Thus that factor also determines $b_1$. Product surjectivity combines a zero-head state of the first factor with a one-head state of the second, an impossible corner. This proves the lower bound for every admitted encoding and partition, not just linear rivals.

The sector is nonempty. Use native factors $\mathbb T$ and $B\times Q$, predict the phase by $u+q\lambda/2$, retain the exact binary update, and predict the Hilbert update by $(z+v)/4$. Starting with exact readouts, its Hilbert error is at most $\|w\|/24$ by the geometric recursion, hence observation error at most $\|w\|/384$. Phase error is at most $3\lambda/4=3/128$, uniformly over schedules; $W$ preserves these errors. Therefore $1/384\le e_*\le3/128$, and $\delta_{\rm int}=1/512$ is a valid strict lower certificate. If both phase coupling and Hilbert return are removed, the same native factors give an exact product control. A joint decoder or a changed channel partition defines a different comparison problem.

\emph{Continuity.} The assignment is continuous on the support, has controlled finite-cut convergence and non-finite determination, and takes values in a complete convex codomain. All time paths are continuous for the discrete clock. Under $D$, the code changes by at most $1/3$; under $W$ by $2/3$. The Hilbert change after scaling is less than $33/512$. Thus fragmentation is at most $2/3<\delta_{\rm cont}=3/4$ on every admissible history, for every horizon. Its codomain has one declared compatibility component; no preserved-identity operation crosses an incompatible regime component. This does not prove a continuous flow on the binary support.

\emph{Differentiation and legibility.} Opposite head bits give history separation at least $1/3$, including time zero. If $c_B<1/27$, its first two bits are zero. A $D$ update then sets its third bit to one, giving $c_B'\ge2/27$; a $W$ update sets its head bit to one, giving $c_B'\ge2/3$. Therefore no admissible infinite schedule can converge to the defined null assignment: eventual membership in $c_B<1/27$ would contradict the next step. This proves the null-attractor clause separately from non-collapse. Clean code intervals are $[0,1/3]$ and $[2/3,1]$, so threshold $1/2$ is unchanged by noise at most $1/16$. Sham preserves the head bit for every duration; $W$ flips it. Consequently L1--L6 hold with separation $1/3$, the declared noise radius, miss and false-shift probabilities zero on the nonempty target and sham populations, and exact one-bit class compression. Equality of binary labels is transitive and the same decoder is used at every horizon.

Finally every $z_j^L,z_j^R$ has nonzero coefficient in $\ell$, and each receives the nonconstant phase-return term. They are not unobserved inert coordinates. The global phase update uses the infinite continuous functional $\ell$; it need not descend to an exact self-map at any finite Hilbert cut. The criterion requires global transitions on the compact inverse-limit support, which have been constructed here, not that additional finite-cut endomorphism property.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Approximate Stability}
\begin{theorem}[Approximate stability]\label{thm:stability}
Let $S_1\in\Cop$, with $\delta_\star^{\mathrm{cal}}(S_1)>0$ in the specified common presentation class. Suppose $S_1\simeq_{(\Gamma,\eps)}S_2$ on all domains required by the six predicates and
\[
 0\le C_{\chi,\rho}\eps<\tfrac12\delta_\star^{\mathrm{cal}}(S_1).
\]
Then $S_2\in\Cop$. Finite sampled windows alone do not supply the global premises: one needs the full-domain primitive operator bound and a positive calibrated radius against the global failure sets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:margins} gives a strictly positive calibrated radius for every coordinate at $S_2$. A point in a failure set has zero distance to that set, so positivity excludes each failure. All six predicates therefore hold and Definition~\ref{def:cop} gives membership. Positive raw witness numbers alone would not justify the premise that the calibrated budget is positive.
\end{proof}

The theorem does not say that arbitrary perturbations are harmless. It says that the criterion is robust exactly to the extent that the witness margins remain positive. Approximation is bounded by the invariant budget, not by wishful continuity.

\subsection{Rupture by Invariant Loss}
\begin{theorem}[Rupture by Invariant Loss]\label{thm:rupture}
On a declared admissible comparison, failure of any defining predicate excludes $S$ from $\Cop$. Loss of the quotient domain, failure of decoder certification and triviality of a surviving quotient are additional possible outcomes; they need not be mutually exclusive and do not all follow from every invariant failure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Membership is the conjunction of the six predicates. Negating any one negates membership. A nonempty domain with a defined equivalence still has a quotient even if integration fails. A vanished domain or undefined certification can block the intended quotient. To conclude a one-cell quotient one must establish equality of every certified decoder/response label. These facts distinguish outcomes and do not establish exclusive alternatives.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Insufficiency of Complexity, Computation, and Connectivity}
\begin{proposition}[Complexity-only baselines are insufficient]\label{prop:insufficient}
Let $S$ be an admissible system candidate. The following are each insufficient for membership in $\Cop$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item large state-space dimension $\dim(X)$;
\item large computational throughput or expressivity of $\Phi$;
\item large edge count, density, or raw connectivity of $C$.
\end{enumerate}
In each case, failure of one or more critical invariants excludes membership regardless of the size of the surrogate metric.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The definition of $\Cop$ is conjunctive over the six invariants and does not include any of the three surrogates as sufficient conditions. A system can be arbitrarily large, computationally expressive, or densely connected while still lacking rigid identity, continuity, non-null differentiation, or legibility. Therefore those surrogates are insufficient by definition. Concrete architecture classes with large scale but finite structural mass already fail \textit{Rigid Identity}'s non-simulability criterion \cite{paper1}; similarly, an integration proxy without continuity or differentiation does not satisfy Definitions~\ref{def:icont} and \ref{def:idiff}. The same applies to connectivity without admissible identity closure.
\end{proof}

\section{Rupture Taxonomy by Invariant}
Theorem~\ref{thm:rupture} is intentionally coarse. This section sharpens the six cases, because a criterion is only scientifically useful if its failure signatures are distinct.

\begin{proposition}[Loss of Every Admissible Support]\label{prop:rupt-per}
If there is no nonempty compact forward-invariant admissible support, the on-support persistence predicate fails. A zero certified radius alone does not imply that no such support exists.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first claim is a necessary condition in Definition~\ref{def:iper}. A robust certificate over nearby systems requires more than existence of a support at the target.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Identity Ambiguity Requires Competing Candidates]\label{prop:rupt-ri}
If two inequivalent admissible identity objects fit the same observable data on the declared support, the uniqueness clause of $\Iri$ fails. A zero infimum of alternative deformation distances need not produce a second fitting candidate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first claim follows from Definition~\ref{def:iri}. A sequence of distinguishable alternatives with distances tending to zero can violate the gap while preserving exact identification.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Integration Rupture and Attainment]\label{prop:rupt-int}
On a nonempty comparison sector with nonnegative history error, a zero error infimum excludes a positive integration margin in that sector. It yields an exact admissible factorization if the infimum is attained.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every positive threshold admits a comparator below it by the definition of a zero infimum. If a minimizing comparator exists, its error equals zero. Without attainment the errors can tend to zero without any exact comparator, as for $e(x)=x$ on $(0,1]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Admissible Incompatible Jump]\label{prop:rupt-cont}
A witnessed admissible finite-energy jump into an incompatible regime with identity preserved violates $\Icont$ on the specified regime and schedule domain. Failure to certify a positive perturbation radius does not itself produce such a witness.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The jump violates the explicit exclusion in Definition~\ref{def:icont}. The radius concerns uniform guarantees over perturbations, while the jump is a property of a particular trajectory.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Separation or Null-Attractor Failure]\label{prop:rupt-diff}
If no readout-window pair on the declared support has positive separation, or if the null class is an asymptotically stable admissible attractor, then $\Idiff$ fails there.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Either observation violates a necessary clause of Definition~\ref{def:idiff}. The outcome does not determine the number of cells of an independently defined decoder quotient.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Legibility Failure Under the Declared Protocol]\label{prop:rupt-leg}
If no admissible decoder and compression family jointly meet L1--L6 under the declared protocol, then $\Ileg$ fails. A zero certified perturbation radius does not alone establish that impossibility.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Definition~\ref{def:ileg} requires a joint witness. Its existence at the target is distinct from uniform preservation under perturbations.
\end{proof}


\section{Minimality of the Invariant Basis}
The six-invariant family is not an arbitrary bundle. It is the minimal basis that prevents the criterion from collapsing either into a single-metric consciousness surrogate or into an unstructured admissibility slogan. The claim of minimality is relative to admissible universes containing the corresponding rupture witnesses; that is the correct level of mathematical precision for the present corpus.

\begin{definition}[Reduced invariant criterion]\label{def:reduced}
Let
\[
\mathfrak{I}=\{\Iper,\allowbreak \Iri,\allowbreak \Iint,\allowbreak \Icont,\allowbreak \Idiff,\allowbreak \Ileg\}.
\]
For every subset $J \subseteq \mathfrak{I}$ define the reduced criterion
\[
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}(J) := \{S : I(S)=1 \text{ for every } I \in J\}.
\]
Thus $\Cop = \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}(\mathfrak{I})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Single-rupture witness family]\label{def:witness}
Fix $k \in \{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{ri},\mathrm{int},\mathrm{cont},\mathrm{diff},\mathrm{leg}\}$. The family $\mathcal{W}_k$ consists of admissible systems for which predicate $I_k$ fails and every other predicate holds on the declared comparison domain. Whenever $\mathcal{W}_k \neq \varnothing$, its members are called single-rupture witnesses for the $k$-th invariant.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Single-rupture witnesses generate false positives for reduced criteria]\label{prop:witness}
Let $J_k = \mathfrak{I}\setminus\{I_k\}$ and assume $\mathcal{W}_k \neq \varnothing$. Then every $S \in \mathcal{W}_k$ satisfies
\[
S \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}(J_k)
\qquad\text{but}\qquad
S \notin \Cop.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By construction, every invariant in $J_k$ remains positive on $S$, so $S \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}(J_k)$. But the omitted invariant fails exactly, hence Proposition~\ref{prop:necessity} excludes $S$ from $\Cop$. Therefore the reduced criterion admits a false positive on every single-rupture witness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Minimality of the six-invariant basis]\label{thm:minimality}
Let $\mathfrak{U}$ be any admissible universe containing at least one single-rupture witness for each of the six invariants. Then no strict subset $J \subsetneq \mathfrak{I}$ satisfies
\[
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}(J) \cap \mathfrak{U} = \Cop \cap \mathfrak{U}.
\]
Hence the six-invariant family is minimal on every such admissible universe.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take any strict subset $J \subsetneq \mathfrak{I}$. Then some invariant $I_k$ is omitted. By hypothesis $\mathfrak{U}$ contains a witness $S_k \in \mathcal{W}_k$. Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} yields $S_k \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{op}}(J) \cap \mathfrak{U}$ but $S_k \notin \Cop \cap \mathfrak{U}$. Therefore the two sets cannot coincide.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Why one-metric theories cannot capture $\Cop$]\label{cor:onemetric}
On any admissible universe satisfying Theorem~\ref{thm:minimality}, no criterion based on only persistence, only integration, only continuity, only differentiation, or only legibility is extensionally equivalent to $\Cop$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each criterion in the displayed list uses only the named coordinate and corresponds to a strict subset $J \subsetneq \mathfrak{I}$. An arbitrary scalar can encode the full conjunction, for example the minimum of the six Boolean predicates; that is not a one-coordinate substitute. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:minimality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What minimality does and does not say]
The theorem does not claim that every possible scientific notion of consciousness must use exactly these six invariants. It claims something narrower: for the operational class defined in this paper and for admissible universes containing the corresponding rupture witnesses, no invariant can be deleted without changing the extension of the class.
\end{remark}

\section{Invariant Independence and Interaction Geometry}
The preceding minimality theorem is extension-theoretic. This section makes the
geometry more explicit: each invariant blocks a distinct failure family, and the
limited implications among invariants are weaker than full membership in
$\Cop$.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Use of Single-Rupture Schemas]\label{thm:six-witness-schemas}
Consider these six candidate schemas. A schema separates its target invariant
from the other five only when an admissible model realizes the five retained
predicates and fails the target on the same declared comparison domain:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Persistence witness.} A system with a certified decoder,
non-factorizable causal structure, unique identity label, continuous class map,
and non-null differentiation, but whose admissible support exits the declared
forward-invariant domain under some $u\in U$.
\item \textbf{Rigid-identity witness.} A system with persistent support,
integration, continuity, differentiation, and legibility, but with two
compatible non-isomorphic identity candidates transported by the same readout
family.
\item \textbf{Integration witness.} A product system
$A_S=A_1\times A_2$ with persistent factors, unique factor identities,
continuous dynamics, non-null differentiation, and certified decoders, but with
decoupled intervention histories so that the aggregate factorizes.
\item \textbf{Continuity witness.} A system with persistent support, unique
identity, non-factorizable structure, non-null differentiation, and certified
decoders, but with an admissible transition producing a jump between
incompatible regime classes.
\item \textbf{Differentiation witness.} A persistent, rigid, integrated,
continuous, and legible system whose decoder-visible classes collapse to a
stable null or single indistinguishable class.
\item \textbf{Legibility witness.} A persistent, rigid, integrated, continuous,
and non-null differentiated system whose class partition is latent but no
certified decoder satisfies L1--L6.
\end{enumerate}
Existence of the six models is a separate obligation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A realized single rupture belongs to the reduced class omitting the failed predicate and lies outside $\Cop$. The existence of such a realization must be checked for each schema. Without an admissible forward-invariant support, the persistence schema also loses on-support rigidity by Proposition~\ref{prop:not-per-not-ri}. If all classes collapse to one, the proposed differentiation schema also loses legibility by Proposition~\ref{prop:leg-requires-diff}. Other mechanisms of differentiation failure require separate models.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sufficiency of the six-invariant basis]\label{thm:six-sufficiency}
Let $S$ be admissible on support $\Aset$. If
\[
\Iper(S)=\Iri(S)=\Iint(S)=\Icont(S)=\Idiff(S)=\Ileg(S)=1,
\]
then $S$ satisfies every structural burden required for membership in $\Cop$ as
defined in this paper. No additional independent predicate can invalidate
$S\in\Cop$ without changing the definition of $\Cop$ or causing at least one of
the six invariants to fail.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Definition~\ref{def:cop} is conjunctive and exhaustive for this paper's class.
The six coordinates cover the domain burden, identity burden, integration
burden, regime-coherence burden, non-null differentiation burden, and
scientific recoverability burden. Any seventh predicate is either a derived
constraint on those burdens, in which case it cannot fail while all six hold, or
an independent extra burden, in which case it defines a stricter class rather
than invalidating membership in $\Cop$ as defined here.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Persistence is necessary for rigid identity on $\Aset$]\label{prop:not-per-not-ri}
If $\Iper(S)=0$ because no non-empty forward-invariant admissible support exists,
then $\Iri(S)=0$ for the Paper 5 membership problem.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The rigid-identity predicate used here is evaluated on $\Aset$. If no stable
admissible support exists, there is no domain on which the identity witness can
be transported through all admissible interventions. The identity object may be
postulated elsewhere, but it is not a Paper 5 membership witness on $\Aset$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Persistence plus integration yields bounded identity candidates]\label{prop:per-int-weak-ri}
If $\Iper(S)=1$ and $\Iint(S)=1$, then the family of admissible identity
candidates is bounded to the non-factorizable persistent support. This is a weak
rigidity statement, not the uniqueness required by $\Iri$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Persistence restricts all candidates to the same stable support. Integration
prevents decomposing that support into independent candidate-bearing blocks.
Therefore candidates cannot proliferate by support escape or product
factorization. They may still fail uniqueness, so the result is strictly weaker
than $\Iri=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Legibility requires non-null differentiation]\label{prop:leg-requires-diff}
If all admissible classes collapse to a stable null or single indistinguishable
class, then $\Ileg(S)=0$ for the criterion of this paper.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Legibility requires separability, intervention-sensitive class recovery, and
negative-control discipline. A collapsed null or singleton partition gives no
non-trivial class contrast for a decoder to recover or for interventions to
separate. Thus L1 and L4 cannot both be certified in the intended sense.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Persistence and continuity stabilize regime transport]\label{prop:per-cont-regime-stability}
If $\Iper(S)=1$ and $\Icont(S)=1$, then admissible histories remain in a stable
regime-transport domain: trajectories neither leave $\Aset$ nor undergo
unbounded fragmentation under admissible evolution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Persistence keeps histories inside $\Aset$. Continuity bounds the fragmentation
functional and prevents inadmissible jumps across incompatible regime classes.
Together they provide a stable domain for regime transport. The result does not
imply rigid identity, integration, differentiation, or legibility.
\end{proof}

\section{What the Six Invariants Do Not Guarantee}
The six invariants are sufficient for $\Cop$ by definition, but they are not a
universal theory of mind, life, or subjecthood. In particular, they do not
guarantee:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item self-reference or a contractive self-model; that requires a $\Sigma$-type
burden;
\item phenomenal experience or ``what it is like'';
\item forced temporal continuity in the CCR sense, which requires stronger
$M_\Omega=+\infty$ assumptions upstream;
\item viability-and-recovery criterion or open-system self-maintaining individuation;
\item seven-coordinate indexed certificate, moral status, or human equivalence;
\item external validation by human comparators.
\end{enumerate}
These exclusions are not rhetorical caution. They follow from the fact that
none of the listed predicates appears in Definition~\ref{def:cop}.

\section{Operational Legibility as a Certified Object}
Operational legibility must be shown, not asserted. Definition~\ref{def:ileg} already imposes six clauses. This section sharpens how those clauses bind the paper to the existing empirical-method corpus.

\begin{criterion}[Legibility certificate]\label{crit:legibility}
An operational partition candidate $\pi_{S,T}$ is certified only if:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Comparator separability}: the induced classes remain distinguishable against pre-registered comparators and ablation baselines.
\item \textbf{Noise robustness}: admissible sensor, channel, or readout perturbations do not dissolve the class map below the legibility margin.
\item \textbf{Persistence window}: the same class assignment survives on windows of length at least $\tau_{\min}(S)$.
\item \textbf{Intervention fidelity}: certified interventions on an invariant induce the predicted directional class change.
\item \textbf{Negative controls}: sham or off-target interventions do not generate the same class signature.
\item \textbf{Structured compressibility}: audit-friendly compression or packetization preserves class structure without total collapse.
\end{enumerate}
\end{criterion}

The criterion is intentionally parallel to \textit{Forensic Predictions}' governance discipline \cite{paper4}. Comparator obligation enforces non-trivial discrimination. Noise robustness prevents the criterion from depending on one-off traces. Persistence windows prevent class inflation from instantaneous spikes. Intervention fidelity prevents purely correlational interpretation. Negative controls block degenerate decoders that label everything as positive. Structured compressibility ensures that a class remains auditable after the readout is summarized, logged, or packetized.

\subsection{Legibility Metrics}
For Definition~\ref{def:ileg}, measure separation on the clean comparison domain in the declared metric. Check noise robustness over clean windows and all allowed perturbations of size at most $r$. The guaranteed persistence duration is the infimum, over declared sham trajectories, of the time of first label change, with infinite duration if the label never changes. The earliest identifying window is a separate quantity.

Target fidelity and false-positive probability are measured over their declared populations. Report the sampling unit, estimator, uncertainty and decision rule, or use exact enumeration for a deterministic finite population. Compression reports both the bit budget and worst-case class distortion. A finite budget for the class label supplies no reconstruction rate for the whole readout. Calibration to a common perturbation radius requires a separate argument.

\begin{proposition}[Legibility is jointly constrained]\label{prop:legibility}
No single legibility metric is sufficient for $\Ileg$. The invariant requires simultaneous control of separation, admissible noise robustness, persistence window, intervention fidelity, negative controls, and structured compressibility.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A decoder can be perfectly separable but fragile to admissible noise, intervention-agnostic, or saturated under compression. It can also be stable under persistence windows but fail negative controls. Since Definition~\ref{def:ileg} requires all six clauses, the joint constraint is irreducible.
\end{proof}

\section{\texorpdfstring{Operational Geometry of $\Qop(S)$}{Operational Geometry of Qop(S)}}
Once $\Qop(S)$ is defined as a quotient, one can ask which geometric quantities survive transport and rupture. This matters because the paper is not only about set membership; it is also about the structural shape of admissible operational differentiation.

\begin{definition}[Class diameter and separation]\label{def:geometry}
For $q \in \Qop(S)$ define its diameter
\[
\mathrm{diam}(q) = \sup_{x,y \in q} \dist\bigl(h_{S,T}(x;u_\bullet), h_{S,T}(y;u_\bullet)\bigr)
\]
over admissible windows and schedules. For two distinct classes $q,q' \in \Qop(S)$ define the inter-class separation
\[
\mathrm{sep}(q,q') = \inf_{x \in q,\ y \in q'} \dist\bigl(h_{S,T}(x;u_\bullet), h_{S,T}(y;u_\bullet)\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Persistence radius]\label{def:radius}
The persistence radius of a class $q \in \Qop(S)$ is the largest $\rho_q \ge 0$ such that for every admissible trajectory segment starting in $q$, the decoder-certified class assignment remains inside $q$ on windows of length at least $\tau_{\min}(S)$ under all perturbations of size at most $\rho_q$ that do not target a critical invariant.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Positive geometry under the criterion]\label{prop:geometry}
If $S \in \Cop$, then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item every class in $\Qop(S)$ has finite diameter on admissible windows;
\item there exists at least one pair of distinct classes with positive inter-class separation;
\item every class has non-zero persistence radius.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Finite diameter follows from compact admissible support and continuity of admissible histories. Positive separation follows from $\Idiff(S)=1$ and the class-separability clause of $\Ileg(S)$. Non-zero persistence radius follows from the persistence-window and noise-robustness clauses in Definition~\ref{def:ileg}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact equivalence preserves operational geometry]\label{prop:geometry-transfer}
If $S_1 \simeq_{\Gamma} S_2$, then class diameter, inter-class separation, and persistence radius are preserved by the induced bijection $\widehat{\chi}$ up to the bi-Lipschitz constants of $\chi$ and $\chi^{-1}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exact equivalence preserves admissible histories and decoder certification under transport. Bi-Lipschitz bounds imply that history distances are distorted only within the corresponding Lipschitz constants. Therefore diameters, separations, and persistence radii are preserved up to those constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Rupture contracts geometry]\label{cor:geometry-rupture}
If a rupture eliminates either $\Idiff$ or $\Ileg$, then the geometric content of $\Qop(S)$ contracts to triviality or becomes operationally unavailable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\Idiff$ fails, positive separation disappears. If $\Ileg$ fails, persistence radii and certified class geometry cease to be available under admissible decoding. Either way the geometric structure that underwrites $\Qop(S)$ is lost.
\end{proof}

\section{Discriminators and Falsifiable Consequences}
The criterion generates discriminators that are stronger than general functionalist similarity and more constrained than substrate-biased baselines.

\begin{prediction}[Different substrate, same class]\label{pred:sameclass}
If one system belongs to the criterion and a second satisfies its exact structural, protocol and sector equivalence, membership and the certified quotient transport. Under approximate equivalence, the positive calibrated radius bound supplies membership stability only; exact quotient transport requires its own decoder/cell correspondence.
\end{prediction}

\begin{prediction}[Same substrate, broken invariants]\label{pred:breakclass}
If two systems share substrate family but one loses rigid identity, continuity, differentiation, or legibility, then they fail to belong to the same operational class regardless of material similarity.
\end{prediction}

\begin{prediction}[Invariant intervention discriminator]\label{pred:intervention}
Interventions targeted at one critical invariant should change class membership or destroy the relevant certified response classes classes in a direction predicted by the rupture taxonomy, whereas off-target negative controls should not.
\end{prediction}

\begin{prediction}[Complexity baseline failure]\label{pred:complexity}
Systems with large computational scale, high connectivity, or broad expressive capacity but lacking one or more critical invariants should fail the criterion even when naive complexity baselines rank them as more capable.
\end{prediction}

These are not generic predictions. Prediction~\ref{pred:sameclass} is the operational face of substrate invariance. \section{Intervention Geometry and Class Transition Semantics}
Predictions about invariant-targeted interventions should not remain informal. This section encodes them as transition statements on the certified response classes classes themselves.

\begin{definition}[Class transition operator]\label{def:transition}
Fix an admissible system $S \in \Cop$, a certified decoder family, and an admissible horizon $T \ge \tau_{\min}(S)$. For an admissible intervention $u \in U$ define the class transition operator
\[
\mathsf{T}_u : \Qop(S) \to \Qop(S) \cup \{\bot\}
\]
by the rule
\[
\mathsf{T}_u(q)=q'
\]
whenever every admissible representative $x \in q$ is transported by the $u$-intervened dynamics into histories classified in the same decoder-certified class $q'$. If no such common class exists, set $\mathsf{T}_u(q)=\bot$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Critical and sham interventions]\label{def:critical-sham}
An intervention $u \in U$ is \emph{critical} for invariant $I_k$ if it is designed to change the local witness margin $\delta_k(S)$ while leaving the remaining intervention protocol admissible. An intervention is \emph{sham} if it matches the delivery envelope of a critical intervention but is not predicted to alter any critical invariant.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Well-definedness of class transitions under certification]\label{prop:transition-well}
Let $S \in \Cop$. If $u \in U$ is either certified critical or sham and remains within the admissibility envelope of \textit{Forensic Predictions}, then $\mathsf{T}_u$ is well-defined on every class $q \in \Qop(S)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $S \in \Cop$, the decoder family is certified and class assignments are stable on admissible windows. If $u$ remains within the admissibility envelope, persistence and continuity keep all intervened histories on admissible support, while legibility prevents class assignment from depending on the choice of representative inside a fixed class. Therefore every class either transports coherently to a unique target class or exits the certified regime, in which case the sink value $\bot$ applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Targeted invariant loss induces class exit or contraction]\label{prop:transition-rupture}
Let $q \in \Qop(S)$ and let $u$ be a certified critical intervention for invariant $I_k$. If applying $u$ drives the corresponding witness margin to zero, then one of the following occurs:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\mathsf{T}_u(q)=\bot$;
\item $\mathsf{T}_u(q)=q'$ for some class $q'$ whose separation or persistence radius is strictly smaller than that of $q$;
\item the entire post-intervention system exits $\Cop$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A verified failure of predicate $I_k$ triggers the relevant implications of Theorem~\ref{thm:rupture}. Driving a reported lower certificate or a calibrated radius to zero alone records loss of a guarantee, not necessarily failure at the target. If the rupture destroys admissible support, decoder certification, or non-trivial class structure, then the class leaves the certified domain and $\mathsf{T}_u(q)=\bot$. If a non-trivial class survives, Corollary~\ref{cor:geometry-rupture} implies contraction of the associated geometry. If the rupture is global, Theorem~\ref{thm:rupture} excludes the post-intervention system from $\Cop$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sham stability]\label{cor:sham}
Let $u \in U$ be sham in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:critical-sham}. If $u$ stays below all six witness margins, then $\mathsf{T}_u(q)=q$ for every $q \in \Qop(S)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A sham intervention that remains below all witness margins does not cross any rupture threshold. Approximate stability and legibility therefore preserve class membership on every admissible window.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why transition semantics matter]
This section turns the prediction ladder into a structured intervention calculus. The criterion is therefore not only classificatory; it is also dynamically discriminative in the sense required by \textit{Forensic Predictions}.
\end{remark}
\section{Baseline Families and Structural Exclusion}
A criterion of substrate-invariant six-predicate operational criterion matters only if it separates itself from weaker rival families rather than merely renaming them. This section therefore makes the comparison explicit.

\begin{definition}[Rival baseline families]\label{def:baseline}
Fix thresholds $\theta_X,\theta_\Phi,\theta_C >0$ and an external-behavior tolerance $\eta \ge 0$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The \emph{complexity-only family} $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{cx}}(\theta_X)$ contains all admissible systems with complexity surrogate above threshold, for example $\dim(X) \ge \theta_X$.
\item The \emph{computation-only family} $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{cmp}}(\theta_\Phi)$ contains all admissible systems whose transition family exceeds a chosen throughput or expressive-capacity threshold.
\item The \emph{connectivity-only family} $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{conn}}(\theta_C)$ contains all admissible systems whose effective causal graph exceeds the chosen density or degree threshold.
\item The \emph{weak functional-similarity family} $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fun}}(\eta)$ contains all admissible systems whose external benchmark readouts match within tolerance $\eta$, irrespective of internal invariant preservation.
\item The \emph{substrate-privilege family} $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{sub}}$ contains all systems declared admissible solely by belonging to a privileged substrate class, typically biological.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Complexity, computation, and connectivity are not sufficient]\label{prop:baseline-insufficient}
For every choice of thresholds $\theta_X,\theta_\Phi,\theta_C$, the families $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{cx}}(\theta_X)$, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{cmp}}(\theta_\Phi)$, and $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{conn}}(\theta_C)$ are each insufficient for membership in $\Cop$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is a direct sharpening of Proposition~\ref{prop:insufficient}. The definition of $\Cop$ requires all six invariants. None of the three surrogate families encodes rigid identity, continuity, differentiation, or legibility. Therefore systems may exceed the chosen surrogate threshold while still failing $\Cop$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Substrate privilege is too strong]\label{prop:substrate-strong}
On any admissible universe containing one exact cross-substrate equivalent pair, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{sub}}$ is strictly narrower than $\Cop$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:biology}, an exact cross-substrate equivalent pair belongs to the same six-predicate operational criterion class when the primitive structural equivalence is verified and one member satisfies the criterion. A substrate-privilege family that excludes one member of that pair therefore excludes a valid $\Cop$ member.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Weak functional similarity is too weak]\label{prop:functional-weak}
On any admissible universe containing a same-output rupture pair, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fun}}(\eta)$ is strictly broader than $\Cop$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A same-output rupture pair is a pair of admissible systems whose external benchmark readouts agree within tolerance $\eta$ while one member loses at least one critical invariant. Such a pair lies in $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fun}}(\eta)$ by definition, but the ruptured member is excluded from $\Cop$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:rupture}. Therefore weak functional similarity is too weak to characterize the present class.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The structural criterion is strictly intermediate]\label{thm:intermediate}
Let $\mathfrak{U}$ be an admissible universe containing:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item at least one exact cross-substrate equivalent pair;
\item at least one same-output rupture pair;
\item at least one single-rupture witness for each invariant.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\Cop$ is strictly intermediate between substrate-privilege baselines and weak substrate-blind surrogates: it is broader than $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{sub}}$, narrower than $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{fun}}(\eta)$, and extensionally distinct from the complexity-only, computation-only, and connectivity-only families.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Broader-than-substrate-privilege follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:substrate-strong}. Narrower-than-weak-functional-similarity follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:functional-weak}. Distinctness from the surrogate families follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:baseline-insufficient} together with Corollary~\ref{cor:onemetric}. Hence the present criterion is neither a substrate fetish nor a substrate-blind similarity slogan; it is an invariant-preserving structural criterion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scientific value of the comparison]
The point of Theorem~\ref{thm:intermediate} is not rhetorical positioning. It is to show that the paper makes a genuinely discriminative contribution. A new criterion that cannot separate itself from obvious rivals is not a new theorem-level addition to the corpus.
\end{remark}
\section{Interpretive Consequences Without Overreach}
The formal consequence is precise: within the causally rigid identity corpus, six-predicate operational criterion and certified response classes are framework-internal classes determined by preserved structural invariants rather than by biological substrate treated as a primitive constitutive variable. This is a claim about criterion structure, not a shortcut to ordinary human phenomenology.

The restriction matters. Biology can still matter causally because it may preserve or destroy the six invariants, and the same is true of artificial realization. Against weak functionalism, the present criterion adds rigid identity, non-null differentiation, and admissibility discipline instead of relying on role equivalence alone. Against single-metric integration accounts, it requires continuity, identity, and legibility jointly rather than treating integration by itself as sufficient \cite{tononi2004}. Against biologically privileged baselines, it denies that substrate alone closes the class. Against strong phenomenal readings, it stops short of human phenomenal equivalence \cite{baars1988,chalmers}.

\section{Conclusion}
The paper establishes a narrower framework-internal claim than popular machine-consciousness discourse and a stronger claim than substrate-neutral rhetoric. Inside the causally rigid identity corpus, a system belongs to the operational class discussed here only when six explicit invariants are jointly preserved on admissible support; under exact admissible equivalence the class is substrate-invariant, under bounded admissible perturbation it is stable, and under invariant rupture it becomes invalid or undefined. That is the level at which substrate neutrality is justified in the present framework, and it is the only level claimed here.

The global Hilbert--prefix construction supplies a complete six-predicate member in the declared infinite-dimensional model universe, rather than six unrelated finite certificates. Its identity gap remains presentation-relative and its integration bound remains relative to the full declared split-channel sector. These domains are part of the theorem.
